Solaria's stock market plunge in Madrid stands out for both its magnitude and its timing. The Spanish renewable energy firm, listed on the Madrid exchange, saw investors harshly punish the announcement of its Uruguayan expansion — a sign that international markets are not convinced that betting on a small market with weak growth and high costs will generate shareholder value in the short term. The country retains its investment grade — Fitch Ratings reaffirmed the rating this week, though it accompanied the decision with explicit warnings about low growth and rising debt — and that rating is the asset that allows Montevideo to tap markets at reasonable costs.
