# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-16

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-16*

Crude oil displaced soybean meal as Argentina's top export product in the first nine months of 2026 — a data point that, just five years ago, would have seemed impossible — and that structural shift captures better than any other indicator the silent transformation under way in Argentina's economy as the government fights to reach the 2027 elections with enough political capital still intact.

According to Indec data processed by the Rosario Stock Exchange, crude exports reached USD 7.753 billion between January and September, 58.6% more than in the same period of 2025 and 6.5 times the value recorded in 2021. Soybean meal, which since the dawn of the 21st century had dominated Argentine foreign trade with a hegemony that seemed immovable, came in second at USD 6.971 billion. The engine behind this reversal is Vaca Muerta, whose unconventional output kept setting records, amplified by Brent crude trading above USD 100 per barrel following the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and averaging 23% above the prior year. Simultaneously, total agribusiness exports broke their own historical record: according to the BCR, main grains and byproducts exceeded 101 million tons over ten months, 13% more than the previous year, with corn leading shipments at 37.1 million tons. Argentina is exporting like never before, but markets are watching with growing unease what is happening behind closed doors.

That unease materialized in a country risk spread that came close to 940 basis points in early November — the highest level in a year — before pulling back toward 830 points by the end of the week. Global bonds recovered roughly 3% in the second week of the month, though Argentine equity prices remain at their 2026 lows: YPF was trading around USD 37.90 per ADR, Grupo Galicia below USD 30 and Mercado Libre below USD 1,500. The divergence between an official dollar that has risen only 6% year-to-date — some 20 percentage points below cumulative inflation — and financial assets that have collapsed shows investors are pricing in 2027 electoral risk, not an imminent FX stress. Implied yields between 2027 and 2028 debt maturities sit near 30%, a premium that Economy Minister Luis Caputo explicitly attributes to "fear of a return of Kirchnerism" and which, as he stated this Monday, is holding back even already-approved credit lines at BICE worth ARS 100 billion that companies are not drawing down. JP Morgan, for its part, expects Argentina to reach the elections with inflation still around 2% monthly, with risks skewed to the upside if FX pressures prove greater than expected.

October's inflation print of 2.2% according to Indec — above the market consensus that had pencilled in between 1.7% and 2.1% — dominated the week. The government clung to core inflation, which fell to 1.5%, its lowest level in 15 months, to argue that disinflation remains on track. Economists are less sanguine: specialist Miguel Kiguel noted the program "underestimated inertia," while Carlos Melconian was blunter, declaring that bringing inflation to the low single digits "will be left for the next government." Orlando Ferreres warned that November will not be a quiet month for prices either, casting doubt on whether annual CPI will close near the 30% the government projects. The total basic basket rose 3.5% in October, the biggest jump in eight months, meaning a typical family now needs to earn at least ARS 1,690,000 to avoid being technically poor. This backdrop gives context to a revealing figure from consultancy Zentrix: 67.7% of Argentines run out of income before the 20th of each month, and 59.4% have taken on debt in the last six months, mostly to cover food, health and basic services.

The tension between a record-export macro picture and a deteriorating domestic market is also evident in employment. Between December 2025 and August 2026, nearly 10,000 companies with registered workers shut down, with manufacturing losing 1,841 active units. Formal private employment has strung together 15 months of negative readings, with a net loss of 150,000 jobs. The Labor Formalization Incentive Regime added 81,808 workers between May and August, but over the same period 37,858 formal private jobs were lost in net terms. Industrial capacity utilization reached 60.1% in September, its best level of the year, yet still below the 61.4% of September 2025.

On the energy front, megaprojects continue to advance: the government added two Transportadora de Gas del Sur initiatives worth USD 3.245 billion to the RIGI regime for natural gas liquids processing, with operations slated to begin in 2030 and estimated exports of USD 1.1 billion per year. San Juan has accumulated approved or in-progress investments worth USD 22.4 billion, driven by four mining projects under RIGI. And from Neuquén, the first concrete agreements are taking shape to export Vaca Muerta gas to Brazil, with Governor Figueroa advancing a memorandum of understanding with Brazilian firms Pré-Sal Petróleo and GásBra. The government also launched the tender of 27 segments of national highways for private concession via Decree 1358/2026.

The domestic universe has its own contradictions. The Central Bank bought USD 103 million last Thursday, the largest single-day purchase of November, and has accumulated USD 16.147 billion year-to-date, exceeding its target floor by more than 60%. Gross reserves closed at USD 49.078 billion. However, the decline in gold prices since January's high shaved USD 1.816 billion off holdings without the Central Bank selling a single ounce. For next January, the Treasury faces maturities of nearly USD 4.4 billion, and the market now acknowledges that the financial program unveiled in July has become obsolete — Morgan Stanley labelled it as such explicitly — with the USD 3.7 billion buffer meant to partially finance those payments having been compromised by the halt in dollar bond placements in the local market.

What comes next is the release of October's fiscal result this Monday, which will determine whether the government meets the surplus target agreed with the IMF and whether the expected December disbursement materializes on time. That money is the first identified source for the January maturities. In parallel, the market will monitor rate dynamics in the United States — where the probability of a Fed hike in December climbed to 85% after October's inflation print —, the behavior of the Brazilian real following Lula's reelection, and whether domestic credit manages to reactivate enough to deliver some growth in the fourth quarter. Consensus among consultancies places 2026 growth between 0.5% and 1%, barely positive and mostly carried by statistical drag from the previous year.

## Corporate Watch

### Integra Capital / GDN Argentina (private)
In less than a week, the investment group chaired by José Luis Manzano closed two significant acquisitions: the purchase of the 107 Burger King restaurants in Argentina from Mexico's Alsea — the brand's second-largest transaction in Latin America — and its entry as a shareholder in GDN Argentina (ChangoMás) as part of the acquisition of more than 900 Día Argentina branches from the Spanish group, a deal valued at approximately USD 53.8 million. The group, which already controls 894 Shell service stations, the Dock Sud refinery and Vaca Muerta assets, thereby consolidates simultaneous positions in food service, supermarkets and energy, forming one of the most diversified consumer and services conglomerates in the local market.

### Molinos Río de la Plata (private, Pérez Companc group)
The company announced the acquisition of the Tía Maruca cookie brand in Argentina and certain countries of the region, its fifth purchase of the year, adding to the recent acquisition of an industrial plant in Tucumán and enabling it to compete directly with Arcor and Mondelez in the biscuits segment; the deal is subject to regulatory approval.

### Cocos (private)
The Argentine fintech closed a USD 34 million round led by Kaszek — the venture capital fund founded by Mercado Libre co-founders — with Spice Ventures as co-investor, reaching a valuation of approximately USD 500 million; it marks the first time the company has received external capital since its founding in 2021, and the funds will be used to finance its conversion into a digital bank in Argentina and its recent expansion into the Brazilian market.
