# Chile: Economic Pulse — 2026-10-21

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-10-21*

The Superintendency of Pensions' decision to open a simultaneous sanctioning process against all seven AFPs in the system —Capital, Provida, Habitat, Cuprum, Planvital, Uno and Modelo— dominates the day with an intensity that eclipses virtually everything else, and it does so because it reveals a convergence of pressures that extends well beyond fixed-income portfolio management.

The formal trigger is well known: Fund E accumulated a real decline of 10.39% between January and September, with a 4.92% drop in September alone, battered by rising rates in the United States and, more seriously from a regulatory standpoint, by exposure to rate derivatives —particularly swaps on US Treasury bonds— that reportedly extended portfolio duration well beyond what is required to match annuity liabilities. It is that active investment decision, rather than simply the market environment, that the Superintendency considers potentially in breach of the duty of loyalty and diligence required by article 147 of DL 3,500. Mario Valderrama, the regulator's chief counsel, described the process as one of "very high complexity" and warned it could extend for up to nine months, but added the detail that will most unsettle the administrators' boards: the regulator's findings could become inputs for civil lawsuits and criminal complaints for disloyal administration. The maximum fine established by law is 10,000 UF per administrator, but the reputational risk and the potential for civil litigation are substantially more serious.

The market reacted with moderate risk aversion. The IPSA closed lower while the Chilean peso also lost ground, though in the latter case external factors converged: the dollar fell below $980 at the close, pressured by a decline of as much as 3.3% in Brent —linked to rumors, later denied, of a peace proposal between Washington and Tehran mediated by Qatar and Pakistan— and by weaker confidence in the artificial intelligence sector on Wall Street, where the S&P 500 barely added 0.3% and the Nasdaq closed flat. The IPSA, however, did not benefit from that dollar weakness, suggesting that domestic noise around the AFPs weighed more heavily than the currency tailwind. Elsewhere in the region, the Bovespa led gains by a wide margin.

Behind the pension fund scandal, the Chilean economy faces a backdrop of simultaneous pressures that President José Antonio Kast acknowledged as a "complex economic moment," though he insisted that a "historic opportunity" exists. Activity fell 0.5% cumulatively between January and August, unemployment stands at 9.6% —a five-year high— and annual inflation of 4.1% in September points to further acceleration. Projections for October range between 0.3% and 0.6%, driven by increases in fuels —gasoline up around $40 and diesel up $90— and food, with additional risk stemming from recent weather events. Several economists estimate that inflation will close the year between 4.7% and 5%, complicating the Central Bank's room for maneuver.

Copper output aggravates the diagnosis. The eight operations of BHP, Antofagasta Minerals and Anglo American —which last year accounted for 47% of national production— have accumulated an 11% drop through September, equivalent to 200,000 tonnes less than in the same period of 2025. BHP's Spence is down 26%. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz spoke of a "mining recession" and acknowledged that the magnitude of the impact exceeded the government's estimates. This drags growth projections lower: the Central Bank revised GDP from 2.1% in the first quarter to 0.7% in the third, according to the Public Finance Report. On top of this comes the fisheries crisis in the north: anchovy landings have accumulated a 77.3% drop so far this year due to El Niño, directly hitting operators such as Corpesca and Camanchaca, whose suppliers warn of bankruptcy risks. The fisheries industry group is asking the government to review the tax on fishing quotas in this extraordinary context.

Amid that tension between cyclical deterioration and the reform agenda, the Chamber's Finance Committee advanced the article-by-article vote on the MK4 reform, approving the creation of the National Housing Fund (Fonavi), a vehicle that will be able to acquire mortgages of up to UF 6,000, with an initial state contribution of up to US$2 billion and leverage capacity of up to US$20 billion. Also approved was the elimination of the Stamps and Duties tax on borrowers outside the banking system —a measure Minister Quiroz called "historic" for its immediate effect on funding costs— and the creation of a junior exchange. The goal is to clear the bill from committee this Wednesday. In parallel, the SII opened the application window for the substitute tax on accumulated earnings (Iusif), which seeks to capture taxation on US$155 billion accumulated in the inherited FUT without tax payment, while stepping up oversight of taxpayers with undeclared offshore accounts exceeding $3.2 trillion.

On the institutional front, the Senate approved with 31 votes in favor the nominations of Macarena Lobos and Juan Esteban Laval as new CMF commissioners, replacing Bernardita Piedrabuena and Augusto Iglesias. Lobos, an independent close to the DC and holder of a doctorate in Law from the Universidad Complutense de Madrid, served as minister of the Segpres during the Boric government; Laval is chief counsel at Abif and former general counsel of the Central Bank. The appointment reinforces the regulator's technical credentials at a moment when its institutional legitimacy is more necessary than ever, given the simultaneity of the sanctioning process against the AFPs and the 20,000 UF fine the CMF applied to former Azul Azul president Michael Clark for failing to launch a tender offer when indirectly taking control of the company.

In the real sector, Sofofa concluded its tour of the 16 regions, identifying barriers to investment with direct impact on employment, while CPC and Inacap presented President Kast with a portfolio of more than US$40 billion in projects for Atacama that would mobilize more than 56,000 temporary jobs. Kast has ten days to close an agreement with regional governors on the 2027 Budget, a negotiation that is unfolding with presidential approval below 30% and 44 resignations of regional secretaries accumulated since March. An arson attack delayed the start of operations at the Cancura Wind Farm in the La Araucanía region, while Codelco reported 59 arrests in an operation targeting copper theft at the El Teniente mine, with 36 complaints accumulated since March.

What to watch in the coming days: the pace of MK4's progress through the Chamber floor, the special Senate session on October 27 on Fund E, and any signal from the Superintendency of Pensions on the evidentiary standard it will apply in the sanctioning process. If the regulator's chief counsel moves toward conclusions that trigger civil liability, the impact on the pension system —already under political pressure from new withdrawals— could redefine the terms of the pension debate with an urgency the government had not anticipated.
