# Chile: Economic Pulse — 2026-11-17

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-17*

The Chilean peso broke through the psychological barrier of $1,000 per dollar today for the first time since April 2025, closing at $999 according to Bolsa Electrónica de Chile data, with an intraday high of $1,003.80 that made it the second worst-performing emerging-market currency of the day, trailing only the Brazilian real. The move did not happen in a vacuum: copper fell to exactly US$6 per pound on the London Metal Exchange, the IPSA retreated sharply to around 10,500 points, and on Wall Street the Nasdaq shed 2.5%, the S&P 500 1.9% and the Dow Jones 1.4%, all amid growing jitters across global markets over AI-related debt loads and their implications for risk-asset valuations. Chile, with its twin dependence on copper and Chinese demand, absorbed the blow with particular force.

The currency depreciation compounds a fiscal picture already carrying its own strains. Congress passed into law yesterday, in a marathon session that stretched past ten at night, the bill freezing gasoline and diesel prices through April 7, 2027, with an additional $100-per-liter reduction for diesel. The measure, the product of last week's agreement between the government of President José Antonio Kast and the trucking unions that paralyzed the country's main highways for three days, will carry a fiscal cost of US$820 million. The Autonomous Fiscal Council has already warned that, given the geopolitical environment and the inability to rule out fresh oil price spikes, Congress should have examined the risks under alternative price scenarios with more rigor. The fast-track process—approved in a single day with 125 votes in favor in the lower house—left little room for that analysis. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz, who also had to issue an apology on behalf of the government over ill-advised remarks by his Agriculture counterpart about the impact of rainfall on fruit exports, now faces a fiscal agenda complicated on several fronts simultaneously.

One of them is the 2027 budget, whose second reading in the Senate begins this week with the Finance Ministry restoring line items that were rejected and reinstating expanded borrowing authority. The question over the use of the additional US$6.2 billion in borrowing requested in June, analyzed by the think tank Rumbo Colectivo, adds political pressure to the debate: according to that analysis, US$3.629 billion—60% of the total—ended up in the Treasury's cash position, while the additional deficit that served as initial justification fell 63%, from US$3.8 billion to US$1.4 billion. The Central Bank, for its part, unanimously confirmed the continued buildup of the Countercyclical Capital Requirement toward 1% of risk-weighted assets, due by May 2028, at an estimated cost of US$1.5 billion for the banking sector. In its statement, the monetary authority flagged the deterioration in the debt-servicing capacity of households and firms, consistent with a weaker labor market, and underscored that global financial risks remain elevated, including the system's exposure to cyberattacks.

The weakening labor market has its correlate on the streets. A survey by the Santiago Chamber of Commerce shows that 26% of the unemployed reported some informal income-generating activity, and 91% would prefer to work under contract—a portrait of informality that the Multigremial de Emprendedores sought to address today with a seven-point proposal to create and formalize close to 100,000 jobs over two years, including hiring subsidies and regulatory changes. Meanwhile, public-sector workers opened salary adjustment negotiations by requesting a 6.3% raise, in an agenda that also includes reducing inequality and improving job stability.

On the external front, Chilean exports to China fell roughly 20% between August and October, dragged down by mining, according to estimates by OCEC UDP—a figure that lends stark context to President Kast's speech at the APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen, where he argued for legal certainty as environmental policy and warned that "no one invests on a thirty-year horizon if the rules change every three or four years." The flip side of mining's weakness is strength in food: food exports totaled US$12.107 billion between January and October, a record for the period going back to 2003 and a 6.6% year-on-year increase, driven largely by salmon, which accounted for US$437 million of the US$748 million total increase. The opening of the Vietnamese market to Chilean beef—with six plants certified—and air-freight access for cherries to that destination, both negotiated during the presidential tour, point toward an export diversification that takes on strategic urgency precisely as the China channel shows signs of saturation.

In the energy sector, the withdrawal of the US$16 billion green hydrogen megaproject drew the attention of former ministers and industry players. The H2 Chile association insisted that the industry is not stalled, though doubts about the viability of projects of that scale under current conditions of rates, capital costs and global demand are hard to dispel. The Energy Ministry awarded 19 transmission works for US$508 million, with participation from Celeo, Engie, Alupar, Transemel and Ferrovial—a signal that core infrastructure continues to move forward. Canada's Tiernan Gold began environmental and hydrogeological field work at its Volcán project in Atacama, with an estimated investment of US$1.019 billion and a target of completing prefeasibility in the first half of 2027. And Antofagasta's Environmental Assessment Commission unanimously approved the operational continuity project at Codelco's Gabriela Mistral Division, worth US$868 million, which would extend the mine's life through 2055, though the resolution is still subject to appeal.

Next week will demand attention on several fronts: the release of the Fed minutes on Wednesday, which markets will comb for signals on the pace of rate moves; third-quarter Chilean GDP data, which the Central Bank will deliver that same day; the choice of a buyer for Transbank, where Banco de Chile, Bci and BancoEstado submitted non-binding offers and shareholders have 45 days to decide; and the progress of the 2027 budget in the Senate, where the line items restored by the Finance Ministry will face renewed scrutiny in light of the cumulative fiscal deterioration. Morgan Stanley projects Chilean GDP growth of 3.5% for 2027, above the market consensus of 2.8% and the government's own estimate, but that medium-term optimism contrasts with a 2026 that the same bank expects to close with just 0.3% expansion, the worst showing among the six Latin American economies the report covers.

## Corporate Watch

### Entel (Bolsa de Santiago: ENTEL)
Entel shares surged 17.6% to $5,939, levels not seen since September 2019, after the formal sale of its Peruvian subsidiary to Millicom—whose shares trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker TIGO and rose 3.73%—for an Enterprise Value of US$1.714 billion, equivalent to 5.3 times trailing twelve-month EBITDA; Bci estimates Entel will receive net cash of approximately US$1.4 billion once tower lease liabilities are deducted. Holding company Almendral, Entel's main shareholder, posted an even sharper 29.66% gain in the session.

### Grupo Falabella (Bolsa de Santiago: FALABELLA)
At its Investor Day held in New York, Falabella announced a US$1.1 billion investment plan for 2027 in Latin America—the largest in its history—allocating roughly 45% to expansion and transformation of physical assets, 35% to technology and logistics development, and 20% to the opening of 20 new stores across the region, with Mexico identified as a high-potential market for medium-term growth.

### CMPC (Bolsa de Santiago: CMPC)
CMPC, controlled by the Matte group, completed the sale of its forestry operation in Argentina—approximately 94,000 hectares in Corrientes and Misiones, mainly pine plantations—to Faro Verde, owned by Argentine businessman José María Larocca, for US$170 million, with payment expected in December, as part of its strategy of concentrating assets in operations with greater industrial integration; in parallel, the Fiscalía Nacional Económica closed an eight-year investigation against CMPC and Arauco over anticompetitive practices in the forestry sector.
