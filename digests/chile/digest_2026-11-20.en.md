# Chile: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-20

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-20*

The dollar closed Thursday above $1,015 on the Bolsa Electrónica de Chile, its highest level since January 2025 and the third-highest on record — a mark that encapsulates several of the tensions defining the country's current economic moment: a Federal Reserve that the minutes of its latest meeting portray as willing to raise rates in successive moves, copper now riding an eight-session losing streak and sliding to US$5.70 a pound, and a local economy that HSBC projects will grow barely close to 3% in 2027, following what will very likely be a close to 2026 near zero or in negative territory. The HSBC economist who presented those projections was explicit in noting that lower local rates have structurally punished the peso, an observation the FX market has validated with eloquence. Meanwhile, the IPSA strung together its fourth consecutive session of losses, down 0.3%, on a day when Wall Street plunged close to 2% and marked its worst losing streak since April 2024, after Anthropic's stock market debut failed to inject the optimism some had anticipated.

Behind that convergence of adverse external signals, the real story of the day played out in Congress, where the Senate sent the 2027 Budget to its third legislative stage in the early hours of the morning, closing a week of negotiations that clearly illustrated the limits of available fiscal room. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz managed to unblock the Education allocation — the most contentious of the process — through a 24-point protocol agreed with Senator Yasna Provoste and a cross-party group of legislators, which included the reinstatement of the "Yo Elijo mi PC" program and the assignment of an additional $47.927 billion to the portfolio. Approval by 25 votes to 2 was the week's most comfortable parliamentary endorsement, though it came preceded by agreements that reveal how much the Executive had to concede to sustain its budgetary architecture. Not everything was orderly concession: the Agriculture allocation was rejected in a 14-vote tie that had to be repeated to confirm the same result, after the opposition and ruling-party Senator María José Gatica questioned cuts to Indap and to the Social Development Program for small farmers. In Housing, the government withdrew an amendment that would have eliminated subsidy advances under the DS49 program — a measure that, had it held, would have jeopardized the continuity of housing projects carried out by SME construction firms in the regions.

The cost of fiscal concessions does not end with Education. The fuel price cap law — approved this week to end the truckers' strike — projects a maximum impact of US$820 million on the fiscal coffers, a figure that could exceed US$1 billion if oil prices again cross US$100 per barrel. Against that backdrop, the fiscal discipline the markets demand collides head-on with an economy where unemployment has reached 9.8%, its highest level in five years, and where a report by Universidad Andrés Bello reveals that people aged 25 to 34 concentrate a negative gap of 466,400 jobs relative to the historical trend, equivalent to a 17.4% shortfall against projections. Domestic demand fell 1.1% in the third quarter, investment plunged 6.7% and household consumption grew just 0.7%, according to data cited in an analysis published in La Tercera. That there is technically no recession, as Universidad Autónoma de Chile dean Rodrigo Montero notes, "is small consolation."

To that picture must be added a Codelco that, according to projections disclosed by board chairman Bernardo Fontaine, will close 2026 with copper production of around 1.2 million tons — its lowest level since 1995 — with similar prospects for the following year. Experts warn of simultaneous pressure on costs, debt and transfers to the State, just as the Environmental Superintendency filed serious charges against the Ministro Hales mine for breaches in the control of particulate emissions in Calama — a zone declared saturated — leaving the copper company exposed to the revocation of its environmental license or to fines of up to 5,000 UTA per charge. In parallel, the Comptroller's Office detected breaches, overpayments and delays in the construction of the Chacao Bridge — a project with investment exceeding $450 billion — and announced objection proceedings to recover the public funds paid in excess.

On the corporate front, the day featured notable moves. The sale of Entel's Peruvian assets to multinational Millicom — valued at just over US$1.7 billion, equivalent to 5.0 times estimated 2026 EBITDA — leaves CEO Antonio Büchi with close to US$700 million in fresh resources to redirect toward the Chilean market, with an eye on eventual consolidation in fixed-line telephony. Grupo Patio inaugurated its first mall in Spain, in Aranjuez south of Madrid, with 100% occupancy and a portfolio of four projects that will total more than 60,000 m² of commercial space and a total investment of US$100 million, in what represents a significant internationalization bet for Chile's real estate sector. Cencosud, for its part, opened a new Jumbo store in Linares with an investment of US$9 million, expanding the brand's presence in the Maule region to four locations. And the Internal Revenue Service announced its largest institutional reorganization in history, with the creation of two new deputy directorates aligned with the risk management model recommended by the OECD — changes that will begin operating in January with the stated goal of reducing VAT and income tax evasion.

In the coming weeks, three focal points will dominate: the trajectory of the exchange rate as the market prices in further Fed rate hikes and copper remains under pressure; the third legislative stage of the 2027 Budget, where the rejected Agriculture allocation will have to be resolved in a joint committee; and the situation at Codelco, whose production numbers and the environmental sanctions process at Ministro Hales are piling up pressure that the government of President Kast — currently on tour in Asia seeking Japanese investment — will find increasingly difficult to manage from afar.

## Corporate Watch

### Entel (Santiago: ENTEL)
CEO Antonio Büchi valued the Peruvian subsidiary at US$1.7 billion before investors at the company's investor day, equivalent to 5.0 times projected 2026 EBITDA, and confirmed that the roughly US$700 million in fresh resources the deal — agreed with multinational Millicom — will leave on hand will be allocated to Chile, where the company is weighing consolidation in the fixed-line market.

### CMPC (Santiago: CMPC)
The pulp and paper company completed the modernization of its Guaíba pulp mill in Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil, with total investment exceeding US$670 million that raised Line 2 capacity by more than 18%, to 1.85 million tons per year, and reduced Line 1 production costs by 21% — part of a biennial capex plan of more than US$1 billion for 2025-2026.

### Masisa (Santiago: MASISA)
The wood products company received another downgrade to its credit risk rating less than two months out from a key date: in January it must amortize at least US$50 million, and if it fails to meet the conditions agreed with its creditors it could face accelerated maturity on up to US$88 million of restructured debt.
