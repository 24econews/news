Copper's slide to a one-year low is redrawing Chile's fiscal and financial risk map at a moment when the country was already contending with anemic growth and inflation stubbornly above target, creating a convergence of pressures that leaves the Central Bank facing its most delicate monetary policy decision in years.

This Wednesday, benchmark three-month copper on the London Metal Exchange touched its lowest level since December 2025, falling 1.04% to US$5.30 per pound. Therein lies the central tension of the moment: the Central Bank of Chile faces an impossible trinity. Inflation stands at 4.5% as of October, 150 basis points above the 3% target.
