# Chile: The Economic Pulse — 2026-10-16

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-10-16*

Today's story is not about an isolated data point or a struggling sector: it is about a pension system that failed those who depended on it most, and whose crisis has unleashed a chain of responsibilities that no actor — regulators, administrators, or supervisors — is willing to fully assume.

The AFP Fund E, designed to protect members closest to retirement, has accumulated a real decline of 10.39% so far in 2026, with a 4.92% drop in September alone. The magnitude of the deterioration has shaken the Chilean financial system and opened a first-order institutional dispute. The central bank noted this week that it has spent several semesters warning about the risks of pension funds' high exposure to foreign interest-rate derivatives — particularly swaps — having flagged these in its Financial Stability Reports for the second half of 2025 and first half of 2026, as well as in interagency coordination forums. Former Pensions Superintendent Osvaldo Macías, for his part, stated that the regulator "fulfilled its duty of warning the AFPs repeatedly," though the administrators did not reduce their exposure. What industry sources are now revealing is that executives from the AFPs themselves privately alerted the regulator about the risks of those positions, but acknowledged the difficulty of abandoning a strategy that was generating high short-term returns. The strategy of extending fund duration via rate swaps worked while rates remained low; when the Federal Reserve tightened monetary policy, the effect on the market value of portfolios was devastating.

Former Securities Commission chairman Alejandro Ferreiro was categorical: "What happened is not something the AFPs can feel especially proud of," warning that if rates remain high, "the damage will be recorded in accumulated savings and probably in people's pensions." The president of the AFP Association, León Fernández de Castro, sought to contain the reputational damage by arguing that only 10% of Fund E members are over 60 and that these are losses not yet materialized in flow terms. The technical argument may hold up over the long run, but it is politically untenable for retirees watching their accumulated savings erode. The Senate joined the pressure: the chair of the Labor Committee, Luciano Cruz Coke, formally requested Superintendent Joaquín Cortés provide detailed explanations about the losses, the supervision applied, and the effects on those about to retire.

This episode comes amid a macroeconomic backdrop that economist and former central bank president José de Gregorio described this week as genuinely hard to explain: "Chile's economic slowdown is surprising," he said. The labor market confirms that perception. A study by the Centro de Estudios Públicos (CEP) estimated that the deterioration in employment prevented 122,000 people from exiting poverty between 2024 and July 2026, reversing nearly two-fifths of the potential reduction. The industrial trade group Asimet, meanwhile, warned about the harsh impact of the manufacturing contraction on regional economies, with lower reabsorption of specialized workers and a growing dependence on productive sectors undergoing deindustrialization. Chile reached a 71.2% labor force participation rate under OECD standards — matching a record for the organization — but simultaneously posts the fifth-lowest employment rate in the bloc, a contradiction that speaks to a market where people are looking for work but not finding it.

Against this domestic backdrop, news from the external front offers a notable counterpoint. Chilean exports surpassed US$90 billion for the first time between January and September, reaching a record US$93.837 billion, growing 18.5% year-over-year and delivering the largest trade surplus since 2007. Copper remains the engine, with returns of US$49.247 billion driven by prices that averaged US$6.09 per pound over the period. One underlying geopolitical data point: India displaced Brazil as the fifth-largest destination for Chilean copper, a signal that the reconfiguration of global supply chains — accelerated by the US-China rivalry — is reshaping the country's trade flows. Undersecretary for International Economic Relations Paula Estévez also confirmed that Chile is studying the signing of a reciprocal trade agreement with the United States, in line with what Peru and Colombia are already negotiating.

On the energy front, the Ministry announced an average reduction of 4.3% in electricity bills starting in January 2027, following the expiration of more expensive generation contracts. The cut will particularly benefit the mining north — Antofagasta will see an 11% drop, Atacama 10.2% — though three southern and far-southern regions will record increases. In parallel, Engie completed the country's first conversion of a coal-fired thermoelectric plant to natural gas, with the commercial start-up of its 377 MW plant in Mejillones, a US$78 million investment that sets a precedent for the Chilean energy transition. Also announced was the start of work on an electrical interconnection with Bolivia, an initiative that opens a relevant geopolitical dimension in the bilateral relationship.

Fuels are moving in the opposite direction: the market is pricing in a fifth consecutive increase next week, with rises of up to $45 per liter in gasoline and $100 in diesel, which will further pressure transportation costs and services inflation. In the financial sector, the MK4 capital markets reform bill advanced in the Chamber's Finance Committee with amendments including caps on Fonavi — the vehicle to finance mortgages up to UF6,000 with a US$2 billion state contribution — greater protections for minority shareholders, and adjustments to the fraud law.

In mining, the week was marked by Minera Spence's lawsuit — controlled by BHP through its Pampa Norte division — against the operation's Union N°1, seeking $8.45 billion for illegal stoppages in July that caused an estimated loss of 567 tons of fine copper. The legal action, which coincided with the close of BHP's fiscal year, sets a precedent for labor relations in the sector. ### Antofagasta plc (LSE: ANTO)

### Grupo Falabella
