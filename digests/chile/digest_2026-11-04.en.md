# Chile: Economic Pulse — 2026-11-04

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-04*

The exchange rate closed higher and copper gave up 2.2% as Americans headed to the polls in a midterm election that, according to analysis from GeoGig Consulting, will not necessarily bring the tariff relief some emerging markets are anticipating: even a Republican defeat could intensify Trump's trade agenda through the presidential veto mechanism. For Chile, whose economy depends structurally on the red metal, the signal is uncomfortable. The dollar climbed to $980, after falling $16 on Monday on the back of signs of de-escalation between Washington and Tehran and the Finance Ministry's announcement of expanded dollar sales during November. Brent crude, which traded around $70 before the outbreak of the Middle East conflict, hovered near $103.8, a floor that continues to pressure transport and production costs across the region.

It is precisely in this context of volatile commodities and fragile domestic demand that today's corporate results take on greater significance. Latam Airlines, whose shares trade on the Santiago Stock Exchange and whose post-pandemic restructuring is still closely watched by international investors, reported earnings of $441 million in the third quarter, a 16% year-on-year gain, with revenue reaching $4.567 billion. The result is solid on paper, but the airline cut its guidance: the 52% jump in fuel prices meant a $520 million hit in the quarter, a figure that exceeds its own profits and that illustrates starkly how much pressure the business model is absorbing. The company has chosen to pass part of the cost through to ticket prices, which protects it in the short term but exposes it to an eventual demand contraction if the Middle East conflict drags on.

The weakness of that demand is already visible in domestic indicators. Ipsos's Consumer Confidence Index fell 1.7 points in October to 38.8, the lowest reading in the region, erasing much of the previous month's recovery. Unemployment, which climbed to 9.8% in the July-September quarter with a youth rate of 24.4%, is acting as the main drag on household sentiment. Economist José De Gregorio, former minister and former central bank governor, was categorical in his diagnosis: he described the Chilean economy's performance as "poor" and "disappointing" and emphasized that this is the country's first recession not triggered by an external shock, implying that the causes are partly structural and domestic. In that framework, he argued that the central bank should not rule out further cuts to the monetary policy rate in the coming months. Labor informality, which affects more than 2.4 million workers and rose 0.2 percentage points over twelve months even as the absolute number of informal workers declined slightly, confirms that the labor market recovery remains superficial.

De Gregorio's diagnosis found an implicit echo in the view of SOFOFA, whose president Rosario Navarro, after completing a tour of the country's 16 regions, identified legal uncertainty, slow permit processing, and infrastructure gaps as the common denominator. The business association estimates that executing the projects currently in environmental evaluation could reduce the unemployment rate from 9.6% to 8%, a margin that seems modest but amounts to hundreds of thousands of direct jobs. That unblocking agenda has its geopolitical counterpart: before European ambassadors accredited in Chile, Navarro proposed that the country act as a bridge between the European Union and the CPTPP, positioning critical minerals and export diversification as pillars of the commercial relationship. President José Antonio Kast, meanwhile, took a similar message to Madrid, where before 800 executives at the Encuentro Santander América Latina he highlighted that the stock of Spanish investment in Chile exceeds $20 billion and that since March approved projects total nearly $37 billion.

On the industrial front, the day brought two significant consolidation moves. The National Economic Prosecutor's Office (FNE) unconditionally approved Aceros AZA's acquisition of control of Compañía Siderúrgica Huachipato in a deal valued at $484 million, paving the way to restart steel production in Talcahuano, halted since September 2024. AZA will control 85% of the combined company, while CAP will retain 15% that gives it the right to appoint a board member. The FNE ruled out relevant competitive risks under the reasoning that, without the transaction, Huachipato would not resume production on its own. The project's strategic axis is the shift toward green steel, which inserts it into the global narrative of industrial decarbonization and could facilitate access to international climate financing. Simultaneously, in the salmon industry — the country's second-largest source of foreign exchange after copper — AquaChile is negotiating the purchase of Blumar's salmon business, while Multi X is exploring the acquisition of Ventisqueros. If both transactions proceed, the sector's map would be deeply reconfigured in favor of larger-scale operators, at a moment when salmon prices are favoring concentration.

Transelec, the country's largest electricity transmitter with more than 10,700 kilometers of lines and coverage of 98% of the population, raised more than $250 million through a bond issue maturing in 2051, with a bullet structure except for the prepayment option. The transaction signals that the Chilean debt market remains open for high-quality issuers even in an environment of elevated global rates. On the pension front, the Superintendency of Pensions published the regulations governing the transfer of voluntary savings into the new generational funds scheme: AFPs must enable the choice starting in January 2027, and those who do not select an alternative will be assigned automatically, a mechanism that will replicate the default-enrollment logic already consolidated in other individual capitalization systems worldwide.

The budget discussion, meanwhile, keeps adding open fronts. The opposition blocked the Justice Ministry's budget to press for the departure of Minister Fernando Rabat, amid placards and skirmishes in the Joint Committee. The debate over childcare funding pits Finance against Congress over who absorbs the cost of co-payments, while cuts in health — including a 28 billion peso reduction to the Ricarte Soto Law — have generated a front of parliamentary and industry resistance. The Budget Office (Dipres) also revealed that cross-analysis of records identified more than 12,000 beneficiaries of Indap programs located in the highest income brackets of the Social Household Registry, and cases of deceased persons who continue receiving fiscal benefits, political ammunition the government will use in the debate over rationalizing social spending.

What to watch in the coming days is threefold: the market's reading of the U.S. election results and their impact on the exchange rate and copper; the concrete progress of negotiations in the salmon industry, which if they materialize will require FNE scrutiny; and the eventual signal from the central bank regarding the rate cycle, in an environment where political pressure to stimulate activity clashes with inflation that higher oil prices keep as a latent risk variable.

## Corporate Watch

### LATAM Airlines Group (Bolsa de Santiago: LTM)
The airline reported earnings of US$441 million in the third quarter, a 16% year-on-year increase, with revenue of US$4.567 billion (+18%), but cut its annual guidance after absorbing a US$520 million hit from the 52% rise in fuel prices stemming from the Middle East conflict. Year-to-date it has accumulated earnings of US$1.142 billion, a 17% increase.

### CCU (Bolsa de Santiago: CCU / NYSE: CCU)
The bottler controlled by Quiñenco — the Luksic group's holding arm — and Heineken improved its third-quarter results with earnings up 5% year-on-year, underpinned by 15.5% revenue growth, but has accumulated a 20.4% decline in net earnings in the first nine months of the year due to non-operational effects in income taxes. The company operates in six Latin American countries and recently consolidated 100% of Aguas CCU Chile after acquiring the remaining 49.9% minority stake in June.

### Mallplaza (Bolsa de Santiago: MALLPLAZA)
Falabella's shopping-center subsidiary reported a 24.3% quarterly improvement in earnings in the third quarter, but has accumulated a nearly 27% decline year-to-date due to weak second-quarter results; the company projects expanding its parking revenues — which already represent 12.2% of its revenue in Chile and grew 18.1% over twelve months — and is evaluating the outlet format as a growth vector in Chile, Peru, and Colombia.
