# Chile: Economic Pulse — 2026-11-26

The Chilean peso closed Wednesday at $1,038.90 per dollar on the Bolsa Electrónica de Chile, its weakest level since July 2022, in a session that saw it touch $1,049.70 intraday. That figure encapsulates the perfect storm buffeting the Chilean economy: copper strung together its twelfth consecutive losing session — the longest streak since Cochilco began keeping records in 2005 — accumulating a 16.59% drop from US$6.36 per pound on November 9 to US$5.31, its lowest level since December 2025 and technically in bear-market territory after exceeding a 20% retracement from its August nominal peak. The confluence of three forces — the collapse in AI-related investment dragging down global markets, expectations of further Federal Reserve rate hikes in December and January, and weak Chinese demand — is hitting Chile with an intensity that outstrips that of its emerging-market peers in November.

According to a Scotiabank analysis, the peso has depreciated 11.7% against the dollar year-to-date in 2026, and the real exchange rate now sits above the long-term equilibrium range estimated by the Banco Central — around 113.6 points in spot terms versus the equilibrium corridor — formally opening the debate over FX intervention. Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz publicly acknowledged that the rise in the dollar "represents a problem" given its effects on import costs and inflation targets, though he reiterated that the decision to intervene or not rests exclusively with the Banco Central. The institution has not announced any concrete measures. JP Morgan, in its "Global FX Strategy 2027" report, anticipates that the peso will remain weak throughout next year and that the moment of greatest pressure could arrive toward June 2027, with the dollar potentially reaching $1,200, driven by interest rate differentials between the United States and the rest of the world at their widest levels since the 1980s.

The inflationary impact of this depreciation is already a concrete concern among private-sector players. The head of research at the Cámara Nacional de Comercio, Bernardita Silva, warned that the first blow will be felt in retail goods — which are entirely imported — and that the pass-through to consumer prices will intensify toward the first quarter of 2027. Holiday-season merchandise was purchased before the currency's escalation, which will cushion the effect over the coming weeks, but inventory restocking at the new dollar prices will begin to show up on store shelves in the first months of next year. This dynamic adds a layer of complexity to the macro picture: Moody's Analytics, through its Latin America director Alfredo Coutiño, identified in recent days a deepening of Chile's recession, a signal that contrasts with the legislative optimism surrounding President José Antonio Kast's government on Wednesday.

Indeed, Congress on Wednesday passed the 2027 Budget into law — the first budget of the Kast administration — by a crushing majority: 113 votes in favor in the Chamber and 44 in the Senate. The political cost was modest but real: concessions from the Finance Ministry in the joint committee implied additional spending of roughly US$140 million relative to the original proposal, with restorations in Education, Health, Interior, Agriculture and Culture line items, negotiated with support from opposition sectors such as the Christian Democrats and the Communist Party — in what constituted a visible fracture within the opposition. Quiroz described the budget as "responsible" and centered his defense on the increases earmarked for public works, housing and security. The external context vindicates his emphasis on fiscal prudence: with copper plunging and the dollar near historic highs, fiscal revenues tied to the metal's price are under severe pressure.

That same day, Congress also passed the Universal Childcare reform, ending the obligation to offer the benefit solely to companies with 20 or more female employees and thereby eliminating a structural disincentive to female hiring that had persisted for decades. Implementation will be gradual, phased in over four years, and will begin with workers on formal contracts. The reform was presented by President Kast in a nationwide broadcast as a historic milestone shared with his predecessors Piñera and Boric, in an exercise of symbolic politics seeking to anchor bipartisan legislative achievements at a moment of economic turbulence.

On the trade front, Washington's new tariff offensive — with duties of up to 40% announced for 14 economies — adds a dimension of commercial risk that experts warn is difficult to mitigate through existing free trade agreements. The limitations of those instruments in guaranteeing preferential access against unilateral decisions by the Trump administration generate uncertainty about non-mining export flows and about Chile's negotiating position. Over the coming days and weeks, markets will be watching three variables: whether the Banco Central announces any FX intervention mechanism in response to a real exchange rate out of equilibrium, whether copper finds a technical floor after its record losing streak, and whether the Fed confirms in December the rate-hike path that JP Morgan and market analysts are already pricing in as the base case.
