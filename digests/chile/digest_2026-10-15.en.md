# Chile: The Economic Pulse — 2026-10-15

Chile's pension system became the epicenter of the national economic agenda today, after confirmation that losses in the AFPs' Fund E have accumulated a real decline of 10.39% so far in 2026, equivalent to roughly US$11 billion in market value, triggering a confidence crisis that spans the regulator, fund managers, the political arena, and a global market that offers no relief.

The magnitude of the deterioration is hard to downplay. According to data from the Superintendencia de Pensiones, Fund E — the most conservative in the system, designed precisely to protect those approaching retirement — posted a 4.92% drop in September alone. The paradox is that the source of the losses was neither equities nor high-risk assets, but rather an interest-rate derivatives strategy that AFPs quietly built up over the past two years. According to background published by Bloomberg and cited by several outlets, positions in US rate swaps swung from a positive mark-to-market of nearly US$1.4 billion a year ago to an accumulated loss of roughly US$7.7 billion in September. The Federal Reserve's rate hikes did the rest: the US September CPI print — 3.7% annually, slightly above estimates — reignited inflation alarms on Wall Street and pushed up expectations for a tighter-than-anticipated monetary cycle.

Superintendente de Pensiones Joaquín Cortez acknowledged he was "on top of the issue" and "concerned," held urgent meetings with the administrators, and did not rule out that there may have been risk excesses within the regulatory framework. The president of the Asociación de AFP, for his part, publicly defended the positions, framing them as "long-term investment strategies" rather than speculative bets, though he conceded there may have been misguided decisions. That distinction matters: if the losses stem from strategies that, however questionable, remained within existing regulatory limits, political pressure will fall on the regulator. If excesses are determined to have occurred, scrutiny will shift to the managers. Franco Parisi, from the opposition camp, has already demanded consequences at both the AFPs and the Superintendencia, and called on President Kast to take a public stand.

The timing is particularly delicate. The new pension reform contemplates an auction of the affiliate stock starting in August 2027, and in that context Fintual and Multiplica took part this week in their first public event as prospective entrants to the AFP market. The chief investment officers of Capital and Cuprum warned about the risks posed by transferring alternative assets during that process. The Fund E crisis thus lands precisely as the system faces its largest structural transformation in decades, complicating the narrative of stability that incumbents need to sustain.

The debacle of the most conservative fund did not occur in a vacuum. The US inflation print also dragged on the IPSA, which lost the 11,000-point mark during the session, while the dollar closed higher above $985, driven by a dollar index advancing 0.4% to 18-month highs. Scotiabank, meanwhile, broke from consensus by projecting that the Banco Central de Chile will hike rates by 25 basis points in December and potentially again in January — a view that contrasts with market expectations of the TPM frozen at 4.5% for nearly two years.

The mining agenda added another layer of tension. Codelco called an extraordinary shareholders' meeting for November 5, at the request of the Finance and Mining ministers, to review newly detected inconsistencies in its 2024 and 2025 production figures. The news momentarily helped stabilize the copper price during the session.
