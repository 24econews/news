# Chile: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-24

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-24*

Copper took center stage on Monday from two distinct yet simultaneous angles: Jensen Huang's return as CEO of Nvidia sent the metal's price soaring from nearly eight-month lows, dragging the Chilean peso along with it and forcing a pause in the dollar's upward streak, while in parallel international traders are accelerating copper shipments from the United States to China amid tariff risks and critically low inventories in the Asian giant. That corporate news from a Santa Clara semiconductor company moves the exchange rate in Santiago more forcefully than any domestic variable says a great deal about the structural fragility of the Chilean economy at this moment.

The dollar closed around $1,016.75, a drop of $5.75 from Friday, thereby breaking three consecutive sessions of gains that had pushed the currency up to $1,023. The Central Bank observed the move with equanimity: according to Diario Financiero, the institution considers that the FX market is functioning normally, and economists ruled out that a dollar around $1,000 would generate significant inflationary pressures in a context of weakened demand and elevated unemployment. However, positioning in the derivatives market tells a different story: the outstanding net amount in forwards held by foreign agents reached its most bullish level since October 2019 regarding the dollar's price, suggesting that the peso's structural weakness won't dissipate with a single day of relief. Sebastián Moncada of the XTB platform summed it up precisely: "It looks more like a one-off relief than a change in trend."

Copper had briefly fallen below US$12,000 per tonne early in the session before rebounding strongly. The metal's sensitivity to the artificial intelligence narrative reflects something markets have internalized but that Chile has not yet converted into industrial policy: copper today is simultaneously the metal of decarbonization and of high-performance computing. That both theses converge in a single market event — Huang's return — illustrates why structural demand for the metal has a more solid floor than short-term swings suggest. Physical flows also back that reading: traders are moving copper from the US to China amid doubts over Trump's tariffs and inventories in the Asian giant described as critical, with freight and premium prices that already make the arbitrage profitable.

Against that macroeconomic backdrop, the domestic debate over monetary policy takes on an additional dimension. Pablo García, chairman of the National Productivity Commission and former vice-president of the Central Bank, told Diario Financiero that he would raise the rate in December and that the Monetary Policy Report should focus on "thinking about what comes next." García projects growth of 3% for next year, a figure that contrasts with the accumulated deterioration in 2026: GDP fell 1.1% year-on-year in the third quarter and accumulates -0.5% between January and September, while Peru is growing at 3.1% and Mexico at 3.2% over the same period, according to data compiled by La Tercera. Santander, for its part, is cautiously eyeing the signs of a rebound in October non-mining activity, analyzing commerce and manufacturing indicators that have yet to confirm a sustained reversal.

The 2027 Budget moved toward joint committee after a tense day in the Chamber of Deputies in which the fractures within the opposition were laid bare. Deputies rejected allocations for former presidents and the Security, Culture, Women, Sport, Interior and Agriculture line items, while approving Article 3, which authorizes the government to borrow up to US$25 billion. The PC and the DC were decisive in that vote, in a pragmatism that PS deputy Raúl Leiva received with audible sarcasm on the floor. The gesture illustrates a broader political paradox: José Antonio Kast's government requires the votes of the moderate left and the PC to approve its own borrowing ceiling, in a fiscal picture where, as La Tercera noted, the effective adjustment was far smaller than announced and public investment fell 22.3% through September while current spending grew 3.1% in real terms.

On the capital markets front, the session was dominated by the tender offer that the Austrian group Plansee launched for Molibdenos y Metales, with which it seeks to raise its stake in the company from the current 31.18% to 54.55%, offering $11,400 per share — a 5.53% premium over the market price — and committing to disburse up to $354.198 billion. Molymet shares rose 3.77% on the day. The deal has a transparent industrial logic: Plansee vertically integrates molybdenum processing with the global supply chain of high-temperature materials, and acquiring control of one of the largest refiners of the metal outside China consolidates that position.

On the infrastructure front, VíasChile submitted a US$456 million project for fourth lanes and a Free Flow system on Ruta 5 Norte, with works scheduled for 2029 and entry into service in 2033. On the trade front, Chile and the Undersecretariat of International Economic Relations expect to close negotiations with India by early 2027, while Sofofa is positioning the country as a potential bridge between the European Union and the CPTPP, with critical minerals as the central negotiating asset.

Next week will be defined by the outcome of the budget joint committee and by any additional moves from the Central Bank or the derivatives market on the exchange rate. If copper's price sustains its rebound above US$12,000 and October's non-mining activity data confirm some recovery, García's scenario — 3% growth next year with a rate hike in December — will gain credibility. If, on the other hand, the dollar resumes its trajectory toward $1,040 and the joint committee prolongs fiscal uncertainty, the Central Bank's room for maneuver narrows precisely when it is needed most.

## Corporate Watch

### Molymet (Santiago Stock Exchange: MOLYM)
The Austrian group Plansee launched a tender offer for up to $354.198 billion to raise its stake in Molibdenos y Metales from the current 31.18% to 54.55%, offering $11,400 per share with a 5.53% premium over the market price; the stock responded with a 3.77% gain on the day. The deal, in force until December 22 with an option to extend, has global relevance given that Molymet is one of the leading molybdenum refiners outside China, a metal essential in high-strength steels and industrial alloys.

### Forus (Santiago Stock Exchange: FORUS)
The Chilean company linked to the Swett family signed an agreement with Brazil's Vulcabras to acquire the ownership rights to the Azaleia brand in Chile and Peru, consolidating 14 stores in the Peruvian market and announcing the opening of two new stores in Chile during 2027, along with the launch of the Azaleia Beauty line. The deal reinforces the "One Forus" regional expansion strategy and brings to 26 the portfolio of international brands the firm distributes in South America.

### WOM (private)
Revenues at the telecom operator totaled $168.824 billion in the third quarter, a 6.8% year-on-year increase, although adjusted EBITDA retreated 1.8%, a sign of margin pressure in a highly competitive market; CEO Andrés Ok described the results as a demonstration of "operational resilience" under strict financial discipline. WOM operates in a sector where price competition structurally compresses margins, and its results reflect the tension between revenue growth and profitability that the telecom industry faces across the region.
