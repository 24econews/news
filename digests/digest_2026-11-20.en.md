# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-20

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-20*

Argentina's country risk closed Thursday at 931 basis points, its highest level in weeks, after climbing nearly 10% in a single session. The figure is not just a number: it is the synthesis of a week in which the market began simultaneously pricing in the deterioration of the global backdrop, the fragility of the local FX front, and the political uncertainty hanging over 2027. The storm came from abroad, but it found a domestic economy with few defenses to cushion the blow.

Thursday was one of the toughest sessions of the year for Argentine assets. The Merval fell 2% in pesos to 80,831 points, dollar-denominated bonds —Bonares and Globales— gave up 3% on average, with losses of as much as 4% on notes such as the AL41D, and among ADRs trading in New York the losses were steeper: Banco Supervielle lost 10.4%, Bioceres 9%, YPF dropped 5.1% to USD 33.28 —its lowest price since January— and Grupo Galicia retreated 6.2% to USD 26.06, a floor it had not touched since March 2024. Mercado Libre, whose ADRs trade on Nasdaq, shed 4.8%. The backdrop was adverse for all emerging markets: the VIX climbed to 25.45 points, the Dow Jones fell 2.2% and the Nasdaq 2.3%, while the Federal Reserve hinted in its minutes that it could raise rates by 50 basis points in December, with the artificial intelligence sector identified as a source of inflationary pressure.

But external contagion is not the only factor. Analysts consulted by local media agree that the domestic political component has gained increasing weight. With presidential elections less than a year away, the market is beginning to price in an electoral risk premium. Country risk had hit a low of 403 points in July; since then it has more than doubled. The possible suspension of the PASO primaries —for which the Government obtained a favorable committee opinion in the Senate— is seen by traders as the main political catalyst available to ease that pressure, by compressing the horizon of electoral uncertainty to October rather than stretching it from August. The consensus among local banks and international funds is that its formal approval would constitute a "positive event" for assets. But the suspension is not yet a done deal, and last week it became clear that the Government can lose votes it had considered won, as happened on Wednesday when allies failed to provide quorum in the Chamber of Deputies for the reform of the BCRA Charter and the Sovereignty Law.

The FX front intensified the tension. The contado con liquidación reached $1,730, the MEP $1,643, and the official wholesale dollar closed at a nominal record of $1,565, with a weekly cumulative rise of $21.50 pesos, the largest in months. The gap between the CCL and the official exchange rate widened to 10%, its highest level since September of last year. The volume traded in the wholesale market fell to an average of USD 352 million per day this week, 40% below October levels: an unmistakable sign that FX supply has begun to retract. The BCRA bought just USD 3 million in Thursday's session —0.7% of spot supply— and gross reserves closed at USD 49,443 million after losing USD 108 million on the day. Minister Luis Caputo himself had emphasized days earlier that the greater integration between the official and financial dollars was proof of the solidity of the FX framework; this week that argument came under pressure.

The devaluation of the Brazilian real, which surpassed 5.95 per dollar and has accumulated a 19% drop since the runoff that kept Lula in power, acted as an accelerator. Brazil absorbed 35% of Argentine exports from regional economies and 44% of non-mining industrial manufactures; a weakened Brazilian currency makes Argentine products more expensive for buyers in the country's main trading partner. The BCB had to intervene by selling USD 2 billion to halt the dollar's advance before it hit 6 reais.

All of this is happening at a time when Argentina's export macro picture is posting its best performance in decades. Goods exports totaled USD 10,061 million in October, up 26.4% year-on-year and a new all-time record —surpassing September's— with Minister Caputo highlighting that over the last twelve months they have accumulated USD 102,943 million. October's trade surplus was USD 3,394 million, more than four times the balance of a year ago. The energy engine was extraordinary: fuel and energy exports jumped 93.9% year-on-year to USD 1,734 million in the month, driven by Brent prices that climbed from USD 60 in January to around USD 94 now. The province of Neuquén has already collected USD 1,441 million in oil royalties between January and September, surpassing the total for all of 2025. YPF CEO Horacio Marín said at the Marval Legal Forecast gathering that Argentina could be among the world's top ten crude exporters by 2030, with the expansion of the Vaca Muerta Oil Sur pipeline toward one million barrels per day.

The contradiction is uncomfortable for the Government: a trade balance breaking records coexists with country risk that has doubled since July, domestic demand that is not rebounding, and industrial business confidence that INDEC measured at -20% in October, worse than September's -18.9%. Nearly half of manufacturing firms reported an order book below normal. Household credit delinquency reached 18.2% in September, almost 9 points higher than a year ago. Argentina is, according to a BDO report for the NGO Lógica, the country with the highest tax burden on personal loans among 30 economies, making credit 39% more expensive versus the base financial cost.

The Government tried to send signals on several fronts simultaneously. The Senate approved on Thursday adherence to the Patent Cooperation Treaty —PCT—, fulfilling one of the commitments made with the United States in the bilateral trade agreement and with Mercosur partners in the agreement with the European Union. Argentina was the only G20 member not participating in the system. Also announced was an expansion of the 2026 Budget by $15.6 trillion via Decree 1391/2026 —equivalent to 10.2% of the current appropriation—, which reduces the projected surplus by some $7.5 trillion, mainly due to PAMI and debt interest line items that proved insufficient against a real inflation rate of 33% versus the original 18%. The surplus does not disappear, but it shrinks.

In the private sector, Tesla launched the official sale of its vehicles in Argentina on Friday with an event in La Boca, also opening a second showroom at Alcorta Shopping on November 23, in a market where 0 km car sales total 17,859 units over the first ten days of November —with a 4.3% nominal drop but positive in terms of business days. Mercado Libre, whose ADRs trade on Nasdaq under the ticker MELI, announced a partnership with Carrefour for 45-minute supermarket deliveries, scaling up its position in the quick commerce segment. The Uribelarrea family, through MSU Green Energy, agreed to purchase two solar parks in the Salta Puna from France's Neoen, which will make it the country's largest private renewable energy generator with 1,977 megawatts of installed capacity.

Next week will concentrate several risk factors and catalysts. The Senate will vote on the suspension of the PASO primaries, a result that will largely determine financial sentiment in the days that follow. The payment of the December aguinaldo —whose effective due date is the 24th of that month— will begin to pressure corporate spending in a context of weak demand. November inflation data will arrive with the question of whether October's 2.2% was a peak or a new floor. And the United States Supreme Court is preparing to request the White House's opinion on whether to review the ruling that overturned the USD 16.1 billion judgment against Argentina in the YPF litigation brought by funds financed by Burford Capital —a process that analyst Sebastián Maril of Latam Advisors warns could become a decisive factor in the 2027 presidential campaign.
