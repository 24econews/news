# "Markets Rally While Economies Crumble Across Mercosur"

*Friday, September 4, 2026*

## The Big Picture

The dominant theme of the week across Mercosur was the widening fault line between external financial credibility and domestic economic pain — a contradiction that appeared, in different registers, in virtually every country covered. [Argentina's sovereign bonds decoupled from a global fixed-income selloff](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-09-02-middle-east-tension-unexpectedly-lifts-argentina-into) as Middle East tensions sent Brent above $95, with country risk briefly piercing 500 basis points, while at home manufacturing output sat 12% below 2023 levels and the Musimundo appliance chain was declared bankrupt by a court in Resistencia. [Brazil's Ibovespa notched an 11-session winning streak driven not by economic strength but by electoral polling](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-09-03-brazilian-stocks-surge-on-election-poll-shift-not-economic), with the Quaest survey showing Flávio Bolsonaro closing on Lula — even as gross public debt hit 82.5% of GDP and household consumption contracted 0.4% in Q2. [Chile recorded its fifth monthly contraction in seven months](https://www.24econews.com/chile/2026-09-02-copper-mining-collapses-to-23-year-low-as-chile-enters), with copper output at a 23-year low, yet fiscal revenues surged 15.8% in real terms in July, driven by copper price-linked mining taxation — the metal enriching the state while failing to lift production. [Paraguay continued posting 5.9% GDP growth](https://www.24econews.com/paraguay/2026-09-03-paraguays-rapid-growth-masks-fiscal-crisis-that-threatens) while the IMF exposed a 3.5% fiscal deficit and former ministers called the 2027 budget unrealistic. The region's outlier, in the starkest sense, was [Bolivia, where YPFB was placed under a five-ministry intervention](https://www.24econews.com/bolivia/2026-09-04-ypfb-intervention-signals-energy-sovereignty-collapse-as) — an implicit admission that the nationalization model of the Morales era has collapsed — as the dollar hit a new all-time high of 12.32 bolivianos and the central bank projected a 3.6% GDP contraction.

The geopolitical variable that ran beneath all of this was the U.S.-Iran escalation in the Strait of Hormuz, which pushed Brent above $95 and U.S. 30-year Treasury yields above 5.20% — levels not seen in roughly two decades. For commodity exporters, the oil move improved terms of trade and boosted equities; for open economies dependent on energy imports, like Chile, it compounded an already deteriorating cycle. The rate signal was unambiguous: with U.S. 10-year yields at 4.80% and the probability of a Fed hike at the September meeting jumping from 35% to nearly 60% in a single session, the global cost of money shifted decisively higher, narrowing the room for every central bank in the region to maneuver and making any sovereign return to international debt markets more expensive. [Uruguay, which is actively sounding out investors for a new external issuance](https://www.24econews.com/uruguay/2026-09-04-montecon-arbitrage-withdrawal-clears-path-for-uruguays-debt), felt this tension most acutely, with growth tracking below 1% just as it needs favorable spread conditions.

For the global investor, this week's Mercosur offered a lesson in sequencing risk: financial markets are pricing reform continuity and electoral outcomes, while the real economy in most of these countries is still waiting for the promised dividends of adjustment to materialize. The gap between sovereign spread compression and household consumption data — visible in Argentina, Brazil, and Uruguay simultaneously — is the region's defining investment risk heading into the final quarter of 2026.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**Casas Bahia (B3: BHIA3)** — The São Paulo bankruptcy court granted a 180-day stay on all creditor actions in the retailer's judicial reorganization, with shares surging more than 40% at the open on Monday before climbing as high as 77.5% during the week. With estimated liabilities of R$28 billion, the company subsequently cut 90% of its media contracts and filed for court protection against supplier terminations — a full-scale restructuring playing out in public.

**Magazine Luiza (B3: MGLU3)** — Magalu's partnership with Mercado Livre to sell up to 28,000 items — including KaBuM electronics and Época Cosméticos beauty products — directly on MELI's marketplace drove shares up 16.88% in a single session, signaling a strategic pivot from costly organic digital growth to capital-light distribution through a dominant rival platform.

**Azzas 2154 (B3: AZZA3)** — The holding company formed by the merger of Arezzo&Co and Soma announced its dissolution into two separately listed entities plus a standalone structure for Farm Rio, following an irreconcilable impasse between founders Alexandre Birman and Roberto Jatahy. Shares jumped 11.28% on the day — the market explicitly pricing in the elimination of the conglomerate discount.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Argentina's August inflation print** (INDEC, expected around September 10): private forecasters estimate 1.6% month-on-month with core at just 0.7% — if confirmed, it would be the program's strongest disinflation signal yet and a key input for the electoral narrative heading into 2027 campaign season.
- **Chile's Monetary Policy Report** (Banco Central, Wednesday): the BCB is expected to cut its 2026 growth forecast from 1%-1.75% to as low as 0.25%-0.75%, a downgrade that will reset market expectations on the rate path and test the credibility of the Kast government's recovery narrative — watch whether the capital markets reform submission to Congress in the same week absorbs or amplifies the reaction.
- **Bolivia's IMF negotiations and fuel decree fallout**: new Economy Minister Morales faces simultaneous pressure to advance the USD 1.9 billion IMF agreement — which requires sustained fuel price liberalization — while Santa Cruz agricultural and civic groups maintain active ultimatums against Decree 5676; any rollback will be read by markets as a signal that the adjustment is politically unsustainable.
