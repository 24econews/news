# Commodity Gains, Employment Losses: Mercosur's Widening Divide

*Friday, August 7, 2026*

## The Big Picture

The dominant theme of this week across Mercosur was a widening fracture between macro stabilization and real-economy stress — visible in every country but most starkly in Argentina and Bolivia, where headline numbers and ground-level conditions have rarely looked further apart. [Argentina's export boom is tracking toward a historic USD 100 billion in 2026](https://24econews.com/argentina/2026-08-05), yet formal private employment has shed 235,000 positions since November 2023, 41,000 kiosks have shuttered, and a Zuban Córdoba poll now shows Peronism surpassing La Libertad Avanza in voting intention for the first time. In Bolivia, a staff-level IMF agreement for USD 1.9 billion and an S&P sovereign upgrade coexist with a diesel shortage that has truckers queuing for fuel four days out of seven, agricultural chambers warning of harvest losses, and [natural gas export revenues collapsing below USD 500 million in the first half](https://24econews.com/bolivia/2026-08-05) — a fraction of the gas-boom figures that once financed the expansionary MAS cycle.

The geopolitical pivot of the week — signals of a Strait of Hormuz reopening and a sharp drop in Brent crude toward and then below USD 80 per barrel — cut across the region with asymmetric force. For Chile, it was an unambiguous gift: [copper hit a new all-time high above USD 6.43 per pound](https://24econews.com/chile/2026-08-05) as oil fell, the peso strengthened to its best level since June, and Senate passage of the reconstruction mega-reform added a policy tailwind. For Brazil, the calculus was murkier: [cheaper crude eased rate expectations enough for economists to cut their year-end Selic projection for the first time since March](https://24econews.com/brazil/2026-08-03), but the oil pullback also stripped Petrobras of the commodity support its ADRs had relied on, arriving just as [Raízen secured approval for Brazil's largest-ever out-of-court restructuring at R$98.63 billion](https://24econews.com/brazil/2026-08-04) — a case that signals the high-rate environment is now consuming companies of systemic scale. Uruguay and Paraguay occupied a quieter middle ground: [the IMF's Kristalina Georgieva used her Montevideo visit to deliver a pointed warning that stability cannot substitute for growth](https://24econews.com/uruguay/2026-08-05), while [Paraguay's customs revenue dropped 10.4% year-to-date](https://24econews.com/paraguay/2026-08-05) even as GDP expansion runs near 5% — exposing the region's perennial structural fault line between growth and the state's capacity to tax it.

The investor takeaway is not that Mercosur is in crisis — it isn't, uniformly. It is that the region is bifurcating at speed. Commodity-exposed sectors and sovereigns with reform credibility are attracting capital on favorable terms: Uruguay placed USD 1.3 billion in sovereign bonds at record-low spreads, Bolivia returned to international debt markets for the first time in years, and Chilean lithium contributions to Corfo quintupled in the first half. But the domestic economies beneath those headline flows are fragmenting — by sector, by geography, and increasingly by the political durability of reform coalitions that are running ahead of their social license.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**Raízen (Brazil)** — The Cosan-Shell joint venture secured judicial approval for Brazil's largest-ever out-of-court restructuring at R$98.63 billion in liabilities, with Shell injecting R$3.5 billion and 45% of restructured claims converted to equity at R$0.50 per share. The case sets a precedent for deploying the mechanism at systemic scale and signals how severely Brazil's high-rate environment is straining leveraged conglomerates.

**Embraer (NYSE: ERJ)** — The manufacturer signed a contract to supply two KC-390 military cargo aircraft to the Colombian Air Force — the first Latin American buyer outside Brazil — while BNDES approved up to R$3.7 billion in financing for 19 jets bound for Canada's Porter Aviation, and subsidiary Eve completed the transition-phase maiden flight of its urban air mobility vehicle.

**Mercuria Energy Group** — The Swiss commodities trader completed its acquisition of Raízen's Argentine downstream assets — including the Dock Sud refinery and 700 Shell service stations — for USD 1.420 billion, the largest foreign corporate transaction in Argentina in the first half of 2026 and the most significant private bet on Argentine fuel distribution in years.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Bolivia's IMF board vote and exchange-rate test:** The staff-level agreement still awaits Executive Board approval; markets will be watching whether the boliviano can approach Minister Espinoza's promised Bs 11 threshold — or whether the diesel crisis and social pressure force a policy retreat before the program is formally ratified.

- **Brazil's Copom guidance and Enel São Paulo ruling:** The Copom statement's language on the September meeting will drive DI rate futures; simultaneously, Aneel's board delivers its verdict on revoking Enel's distribution concession in Greater São Paulo — a decision with no modern precedent in Brazilian energy markets.
