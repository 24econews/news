# Fiscal Credibility Crumbles as Commodity Strength Masks Regional Decay

*Friday, November 20, 2026*

## The Big Picture

The dominant theme of this week in Mercosur was a collective loss of fiscal credibility — not simultaneously engineered, but arriving from different directions and reinforcing one another in ways that rattled regional assets across the board. [Brazil's "Moretti plan"](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-11-17-market-rejects-moretti-plan-dollar-surges-to-april-2025-high) was approved by the Senate and immediately rejected by markets: the dollar surged to its highest level since April 2025, closing Thursday's session at R$5.931, with the Ibovespa technically entering bear market territory after retreating more than 20% from its October peak. The package's core problem — identified by analysts at Banco Pine as "visibly not a fiscal adjustment" — is that it is predominantly a tax hike on companies, not a spending cut, leaving the nominal deficit at 9.5% of GDP structurally untouched. [Argentina](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-11-20-argentinas-risk-premium-doubles-as-export-boom-masks) posted a record trade surplus and the largest monthly financial surplus of the year in October, yet country risk doubled since July to close the week at 931 basis points — a number that reflects not current-account stress but a $17 billion projected 2027 financing gap and a market pricing in electoral uncertainty at an accelerating pace. [Chile's GDP contracted 1.1% in the third quarter](https://www.24econews.com/chile/2026-11-19-coppers-ai-crash-collides-with-chiles-weakest-quarter-in), its worst performance since 2020, while copper plunged 4.6% in a single session on a reassessment of AI-driven metals demand, pushing the peso above $1,015 — a 20-month high for the dollar. [Paraguay's Ueno Bank collapse](https://www.24econews.com/paraguay/2026-11-19-ueno-banks-15-billion-collapse-shatters-paraguays-fiscal) confirmed a preliminary $1.5 billion shortfall, with $888 million in public funds from state entities including IPS, Itaipú and Yacyretá directly committed — a figure equivalent to roughly two percentage points of GDP in a country whose fiscal deficit already reached 2.3%.

The divergence within this common pressure is equally instructive. [Uruguay](https://www.24econews.com/uruguay/2026-11-17-award-winning-finance-minister-returns-to-steer-uruguay) welcomed back award-winning Finance Minister Azucena Arbeleche with a mandate for austerity, a World Bank $300 million approval and a $1 billion renewable energy commitment from Spain's Solaria — a picture of institutional continuity that stands in sharp contrast to the political chaos visible in Asunción and La Paz. [Bolivia](https://www.24econews.com/bolivia/2026-11-18-bolivias-gas-reserves-collapse-to-20-year-low-as-structural), meanwhile, confirmed proven natural gas reserves of just 2.3 TCF — the lowest in twenty years, down 78% since 2013 — while cycling through a cabinet reshuffle mid-crisis and leaning on an emergency external liquidity scaffolding of more than $5 billion from the IMF, World Bank, IDB and CAF. The global backdrop amplified every domestic vulnerability: the Federal Reserve's minutes signaled the possibility of a 50-basis-point December hike, the VIX climbed toward 25, and an AI-driven copper selloff hammered Chile just as its pension funds disclosed more than $14 billion in cumulative derivatives losses on US interest rate bets. For global investors, the message is consistent: in Mercosur this week, commodity export strength and institutional stress were not opposites — they were simultaneous realities coexisting in the same balance sheet.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**Entel (Santiago: ENTEL) — USD 1.714 billion Peru sale to Millicom:** The deal, struck at 5.0x estimated 2026 EBITDA, sent Entel shares surging 21.78% in a single session and leaves CEO Antonio Büchi with roughly USD 700 million to redeploy into the Chilean market — a rare piece of unambiguously positive corporate news in a week of regional stress.

**BlackRock acquires SulAmérica Investimentos (Rede D'Or: B3 RDOR3) for R$850 million:** The world's largest asset manager deepens its Brazilian platform at the precise moment domestic equities are in bear market territory, a countercyclical conviction bet that signals long-term institutional confidence even as local investors flee.

**Toyota Argentina — USD 1.341 billion RIGI commitment:** The largest single automotive investment in Argentine history, anchoring production of the hybrid Tacoma in Zárate from 2030, arrived the same week country risk doubled from its July low — the starkest single illustration of the gap between long-cycle investment horizons and short-cycle political risk pricing.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Brazil's Lower House vote on the Moretti plan (Monday, Nov. 23):** With the government's fiscal credibility on the line and the dollar above R$5.90, any dilution of the package in the Chamber — or signals that the fourth Lula administration is considering a constitutional amendment to cut mandatory spending — will be the decisive trigger for Brazilian assets in the near term. The bimonthly Revenue and Expenditure Assessment Report due Tuesday adds a hard deadline.
- **Argentina's Senate vote on PASO suspension:** Analysts and international funds identify this as the single political catalyst capable of compressing Argentina's country risk — which has doubled since July to 931 basis points — by removing the August electoral shock risk. A failed vote would leave the full electoral premium intact heading into year-end.
- **Paraguay's Senate budget vote and BCP governance:** The 2027 General Budget faces a Senate debate with Fitch watching and a projected deficit at 3.9% of GDP that business associations call unsustainable. Simultaneously, two BCP board vacancies need filling and a USD 1.5 billion shortfall at Ueno Bank demands criminal accountability and regulatory reform to move in parallel — not in sequence.
