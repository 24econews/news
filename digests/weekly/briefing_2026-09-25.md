# Bolivia's Reform Gamble Tests Mercosur's Export-Demand Collapse

*Friday, September 25, 2026*

## The Big Picture

Bolivia's elimination of the diesel subsidy — lifting prices from a fraction of cost to Bs 17.95 per liter — was the week's most structurally consequential event in Mercosur, but it didn't happen in isolation. It arrived alongside a $1.9 billion IMF agreement ratified by a two-thirds legislative supermajority, a $1 billion sovereign bond placement that drew five times its target in demand, and country risk that compressed below 500 basis points — suddenly placing [Bolivia ahead of Argentina on that metric](https://www.24econews.com/bolivia/2026-09-24-diesel-subsidy-cuts-signal-bolivias-imf-backed-economic). The reform is real, painful, and contested: intercity bus fares jumped 125% on some corridors, soy producers warned of rising production costs, and the transport sector threatened Monday blockades. Whether the PEPE II cash transfer and CNG conversion plans can hold the social coalition together is the most consequential open question in the region heading into October.

Brazil supplied the week's most crowded fiscal narrative. [Dividend tax revenues came in at R$10.5 billion](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-09-23-dividend-tax-overhaul-yields-one-third-of-promised-revenue) — one-third of the R$29 billion originally projected — as the Central Bank cut its GDP growth forecast to 1.8% for 2026 and consumer confidence hit its lowest since November 2022. The Lula government responded by loosening its budget freeze from R$17.9 billion to R$13.6 billion, extending a diesel subsidy, and raising Bolsa Família payments by 15% less than three weeks before the first round of the presidential election. [The dollar surged past R$5.16](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-09-24-dollar-hits-r-516-as-iran-tensions-trump-threats-converge) on a single session combining Middle East tensions, U.S. tariff threats, and domestic fiscal deterioration — forcing R$4 billion in Central Bank FX interventions over the month. Meanwhile, [Argentina moved in the opposite direction on international optics](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-09-23-us-backs-argentinas-7-billion-energy-bet-as-sovereign-risk): the Andes-Atlantic Corridor deal with Washington unlocked up to $7 billion in infrastructure financing and formalized YPF's LNG partnership with ENI and XRG — yet sovereign bonds still yielded above 10% and country risk breached 580 basis points by week's end, as July's EMAE printed -2.9% versus June and poverty rose to 32.3%.

For global investors, the week distilled into a single, uncomfortable takeaway: across Mercosur's six economies, the export sectors are outperforming while domestic demand stagnates or contracts. [Chile's unemployment hit a five-year high of 9.5%](https://www.24econews.com/chile/2026-09-23-unemployment-at-five-year-high-triggers-chiles-governance) and the peso weakened past 964 per dollar. [Uruguay's economy contracted 0.8% in the second quarter](https://www.24econews.com/uruguay/2026-09-22-uruguays-economy-shrinks-as-fiscal-credibility-crumbles) even as the World Bank approved $300 million in new financing. [Paraguay's GDP grew 6.6% in 2025](https://www.24econews.com/paraguay/2026-09-22-paraguays-growth-masks-deepening-institutional-credibility) but institutional noise around the central bank's independence spooked economists who spent years building that reputation. The export booms are real. The domestic recoveries are not.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**Afya / Yduqs merger (Brazil):** The deal — creating a group with R$9.4 billion in revenue and 17% of Brazil's medical education market — is the largest M&A transaction in Brazil's education sector. Afya will delist from Nasdaq, Bertelsmann ends up with 47% of the combined entity, and estimated synergies exceed R$2 billion.

**Vista Energy (Argentina):** Vista reopened its 2038 bond to raise an additional $400 million at 7.875%, compressing the spread over the original issue from 350 to 300 basis points — a striking display of Argentine corporate credit strength at the very moment sovereign bonds were yielding above 10%.

**CMPC (Chile):** The Matte family's forestry group agreed to sell its corrugated packaging business to Smurfit Westrock for $420 million at a 7.6x EBITDA multiple, concentrating capital on its Brazilian pulp expansion — the most significant Chilean corporate asset sale of the week.
