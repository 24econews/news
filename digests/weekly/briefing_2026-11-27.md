# Credibility Reprices Faster Than Policy in Mercosur

*Friday, November 27, 2026*

## The Big Picture

The dominant theme of this week in Mercosur was the fragility of institutional credibility — and what it costs when markets price its absence. Brazil commanded the most dramatic arc: the real, already the world's worst-performing major currency since Lula's reelection, [briefly broke R$6 to the dollar mid-week](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-11-24-market-indifference-to-fiscal-plan-signals-deeper-crisis) as markets greeted the Moretti fiscal package's approval with conspicuous indifference, then whipsawed to its [sharpest weekly dollar collapse since October 2008](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-11-27-trabucos-appointment-triggers-biggest-dollar-collapse-since) on the appointment of Bradesco chairman Luiz Carlos Trabuco as incoming Finance Minister. The Ibovespa gained 6.13% on the week — its best performance since the first round of the presidential election — while the DI for January 2031 fell from 14.62% to 13.89%. The implied probability of a December Copom hike dropped from 16% to 5.3%. One personnel decision repriced the entire Brazilian yield curve. That is a measure of how much the market had been demanding a credible face, not just a credible policy.

Argentina offered an inverted version of the same dynamic. [Country risk surged past 1,100 basis points intraday](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-11-26-argentinas-2027-election-premium-already-distorting-bond) before closing the week below 1,000 — a 10% drop in a single session on Friday, catalyzed by a cluster of legislative wins including Central Bank charter reform, PASO suspension, and Budget approval, plus the detail of Minister Caputo's January debt payment plan. But the relief, like Brazil's, is conditional. JP Morgan's Argentina 2027 Outlook quantified the structural problem with unusual precision: the AO27 bond yields 4.7% annually; the AO28, maturing under the next administration, yields 21.6% — a nearly 17-percentage-point gap that represents 43% of current country risk. Electoral uncertainty is not a future variable here; it is the dominant price-setter today. Meanwhile, [technical recession was confirmed](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-11-25-argentinas-recession-confirmed-as-currency-pressure-hits) with a 1.8% third-quarter GDP contraction, while the energy sector moved in the opposite direction: Argentina LNG's admission to the RIGI — a USD 50.75 billion investment by YPF, ENI, and XRG — was formalized and its Final Investment Decision signed this weekend.

Across the region, the week illustrated a consistent pattern: macro aggregates and energy export stories flatter; institutional and labor-market data disappoint. [Chile's unemployment hit 10%](https://www.24econews.com/chile/2026-11-27-unemployment-hits-four-year-high-as-stock-market-surges-on), its highest since 2021, with 170,000 more people out of work than a year ago, even as [copper posted its longest losing streak since 2005](https://www.24econews.com/chile/2026-11-26-coppers-longest-losing-streak-since-2005-weakens-peso-to) — twelve consecutive sessions — and the peso touched a four-year low. Paraguay's crisis was the most dramatic in structural terms: the Ueno Bank collapse consumed the week, culminating in [the arrest of former Economy Minister Carlos Fernández Valdovinos](https://www.24econews.com/paraguay/2026-11-27-former-economy-minister-arrested-as-ueno-bank-crisis), entangling bilateral relations with Brazil over USD 220 million in frozen Itaipú funds, and exposing a supervisory failure that the BCP president herself acknowledged with the words "there is much more to unravel." Bolivia, meanwhile, confirmed what its own budget had already admitted: [the IMF projects a 3% contraction](https://www.24econews.com/bolivia/2026-11-24-bolivias-budget-admits-15-contraction-while-imf-sees-deeper) against the government's own 1.5% forecast, as a diesel price hike to Bs 20.57 per liter ignited transport strikes and threatened to unravel the social compact of fiscal adjustment just as the Fund validated its terms. Uruguay alone offered relative calm — [unemployment at a 12-year low of 6.5%](https://www.24econews.com/uruguay/2026-11-24-uruguays-jobless-rate-falls-to-its-lowest-in-nearly-12), country risk at 88 basis points — though the [dollar hitting a 17-month high](https://www.24econews.com/uruguay/2026-11-26-dollar-hits-17-month-high-as-regional-contagion-overwhelms) confirmed that even the region's most stable economy is not insulated from the contagion running through its neighbors.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**Braskem (NYSE: BAK)** narrowly avoided a Chapter 11-equivalent filing when lawyers arrived Sunday with documentation ready — a preliminary restructuring agreement was reached at the eleventh hour, envisaging USD 4.9 billion in total capitalization, including a USD 1.3 billion immediate injection from Petrobras and USD 3.6 billion in debt-to-equity conversion by creditors.

**YPF (NYSE: YPF)** formalized Argentina LNG's admission to the RIGI with USD 50.75 billion in total investment through 2050 alongside ENI and XRG; the Final Investment Decision was signed this weekend, with projected annual exports of USD 10 billion starting in 2031 — the largest foreign investment commitment in Argentine history.

**Embraer (NYSE: ERJ)** fell more than 9% in a single session after the USTR proposed a 10% tariff on Brazilian aircraft, a measure analysts described as "surgical" retaliation for Brazil's WTO challenge to U.S. tariffs — with the added sting that American airline executives warned the duty would raise their own operating costs, given Embraer's dominance in regional jets.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Brazil:** The market's first sustained test of the Trabuco rally — watch for the formal scope of the fiscal package his team inherits, any signal on the constitutional amendment to cap mandatory spending, and Lula's remaining cabinet appointments ahead of the December 15 congressional recess. The IMF's December Copom meeting repricing will hinge on whether a credible second adjustment round materializes.

- **Argentina:** The IMF's third program review, expected in the first week of December, is the linchpin of Caputo's January payment scheme; failure to unlock the USD 1.2 billion multilateral tranche would immediately reprice the AO27-AO28 spread the market is already watching as its electoral risk thermometer. November inflation data — consultancies project ~1.8% — could offer the government its first genuinely positive narrative moment in weeks.

- **Paraguay & Bolivia:** In Asunción, the Chamber of Deputies receives the Senate-approved 2027 Budget and judicial proceedings against Fernández Valdovinos advance — either development has the capacity to deepen or contain the sovereign risk damage from Ueno. In La Paz, Monday's threatened indefinite trucker strike is the immediate test of whether the diesel price hike survives politically; YPFB's signaled downward correction may defuse it, but the IMF disbursement formalization in December will determine whether Bolivia's adjustment retains external credibility regardless of domestic outcome.
