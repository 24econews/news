# Mercosur's Institutions Fracture as Four Economies Unravel Simultaneously

*Friday, November 6, 2026*

## The Big Picture

The week that just closed may be remembered as the moment Mercosur's political risks stopped being hypothetical and started being priced. [Brazil's post-election collapse](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-11-02-lara-resendes-rate-cap-proposal-triggers-unprecedented-rift) set the tone: Lula's reelection to an unprecedented fourth term triggered an 18.12% weekly decline in the Ibovespa — its worst performance since March 2020 — while listed companies shed roughly R$963 billion in market value and the dollar posted its biggest weekly gain since 2008. The proximate catalyst was not the election result itself but the circulation of a proposal to cap the Selic rate, which exposed the fragility of Brazil's monetary policy framework at precisely the moment markets needed reassurance. By Wednesday, [the Copom voted unanimously to halt its easing cycle at 13.75%](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-11-05-central-bank-passes-buck-to-lula-on-interest-rate-future), explicitly passing responsibility for the next chapter of monetary policy to Palácio do Planalto — a central bank that rarely makes political subtext this legible.

The Brazil shock radiated unevenly but unmistakably across the bloc. [Argentina's country risk crossed 925 basis points by Thursday](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-11-06-trumps-congressional-defeat-fractures-argentinas-lifeline) — its highest level since the 2025 midterms — amplified by the Democrats' recapture of Congress in US midterm elections, which markets read as reducing Washington's political bandwidth to backstop Buenos Aires. The move was disproportionate relative to peers: since mid-September, Argentine sovereign spreads had widened at roughly 3.4 times the pace of a comparable emerging-market basket. The implied probability of Milei's reelection collapsed from roughly 90% to around 50%, and that repricing of political continuity risk — not any deterioration in the fiscal data — was what drove the asset punishment. Chile absorbed the week differently: [Codelco's disclosure of 42,100 tons of artificial production between 2024 and 2025, with an accounting impact of up to US$140 million](https://www.24econews.com/chile/2026-11-06-codelcos-140-million-production-fraud-shatters-chiles), arrived as a governance shock that struck the country's most critical fiscal transfer vehicle, sending the peso to its worst single-day performance among emerging-market currencies and dragging copper below US$6.25 per pound.

The week's most underappreciated story, however, may belong to Paraguay, where [the collapse of Ueno Bank triggered the resignation of the central bank president, the detention of 27 individuals, and the freezing of approximately US$355 million in IPS pension fund assets](https://www.24econews.com/paraguay/2026-11-06-guarani-crashes-as-ueno-bank-collapse-triggers-paraguays) — pushing the guaraní to the worst-performing currency in the world against the dollar according to Bloomberg. For global investors, the week's takeaway is structural: across four of Mercosur's six economies, the dominant story was not commodity cycles or external shocks but the cost of institutional fragility. Where institutions held — Uruguay's investment grade, Chile's budget process, Brazil's central bank independence — they held under extraordinary pressure. Where they did not, the damage was swift and the contagion immediate.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**Tractian (Brazil):** The Brazilian industrial AI startup closed a US$130 million Series D at a post-money valuation of US$1.4 billion — double its valuation two years ago — with annualized recurring revenue of nearly US$100 million and a presence in more than 2,000 factories. The round makes it the largest AI company in Latin America by valuation and signals that global capital appetite for Latin American industrial tech remains intact even as the macro backdrop deteriorates.

**Ueno Bank (Paraguay):** The BCP's resolution procedure against the digital lender — following discovery of an 8,000% expansion in holdings in related companies — has frozen roughly US$355 million in pension funds and exposed governance failures at both the institution and its regulator. With 27 individuals charged and the central bank presidency vacant, the case has moved from a bank failure into a potential sovereign rating event.

**MercadoLibre (NASDAQ: MELI):** Shares fell as much as 13% in New York after third-quarter earnings per share came in US$0.51 below consensus, with margin compression from aggressive logistics investment cutting the operating margin to 6.3% — a rare stumble for a stock that had anchored the regional tech narrative all year, and a data point that will sharpen scrutiny on its Brazil-heavy growth model.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Brazil's economic team announcement:** The names Lula selects for Finance and the central bank board will be the single most market-moving event in Mercosur next week — Itaú projects 2027 GDP growth of just 0.5% and inflation of 6.1%, trajectories that reverse only if the government signals genuine fiscal discipline before long-term rates entrench further.
- **Paraguay's BCP leadership and Ueno Bank deposit timeline:** The confirmation of Raúl Vera Bogado as permanent BCP president and the schedule for returning frozen deposits — particularly the US$355 million in IPS funds — will determine whether the guaraní stabilizes or whether Moody's warning of fiscal risk translates into a formal rating action.
- **Bolivia's IMF negotiations:** With the 2027 budget publicly maintaining fuel subsidies in apparent breach of program commitments, Economy Minister Morales will need to show the Fund a credible path to subsidy reform before year-end — or risk the US$1.9 billion program's next disbursement tranche.
