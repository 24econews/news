# # Exports Boom While Economies Stall Across Mercosur

*Friday, October 23, 2026*

## The Big Picture

The dominant story of this Mercosur week was the widening gap between what macroeconomic data says and what markets believe — a disconnect that played out simultaneously in Argentina, Chile, Uruguay, and Bolivia, and that no amount of official reassurance fully closed. [Argentina's country risk broke above 700 basis points](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-10-22-technical-recession-looms-as-argentinas-oil-boom-fails-to) even as the Central Bank accumulated reserves at a record pace and exports topped USD 10 billion in a single month for the first time in history. [Chile's pension regulator opened simultaneous sanctioning proceedings against all seven AFPs](https://www.24econews.com/chile/2026-10-21-regulator-opens-simultaneous-case-against-all-seven-pension) after the most conservative fund in the system lost 10.39% in real terms — not from equity exposure, but from a concentrated derivatives bet on falling U.S. rates that never arrived fast enough. [Uruguay attracted the largest foreign investment pledge in its history](https://www.24econews.com/uruguay/2026-10-19-spanish-solar-firm-bets-57-billion-data-center-on-uruguays) — a $5.7 billion data center from Spanish firm Solaria — while private analysts cut the country's 2026 growth forecast to 1% and Fitch paired its investment-grade reaffirmation with explicit warnings about weak growth and mounting debt. And [Bolivia's new economy minister arrived with a mandate to stabilize an economy the IMF projects will be the only one in South America to contract this year](https://www.24econews.com/bolivia/2026-10-23-bolivia-faces-latin-americas-only-2025-contraction-while), while simultaneously managing LPG smuggling networks allegedly involving state oil company employees, a diesel subsidy ultimatum from Evo Morales loyalists, and a gas sector that experts now describe as structurally dependent on imports.

Brazil added its own version of this contradiction. [Oil surpassed soybeans as Brazil's leading export](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-10-20-oil-surpasses-soy-as-brazils-top-export-amid-electoral) and [Brazil overtook Saudi Arabia as China's second-largest crude supplier](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-10-21-brazil-surpasses-saudi-arabia-as-chinas-second-largest) — structural shifts of historic magnitude — yet the Ibovespa's post-first-round rally collapsed in the final days of the week as the runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro tightened to a statistical tie and analysts across the political spectrum agreed that whoever wins inherits a fiscal bill, now estimated at R$356 billion in announced measures, that leaves almost no room for the adjustment markets have already priced in. Paraguay, the region's outlier, sustained 5.7% cumulative GDP growth through August — but its own success became a liability as the guaraní became Latin America's best-performing currency, forcing cattle slaughter down 21% and meatpacking plants to half capacity.

For global investors, the week's clearest takeaway is structural: the commodity export boom running through Brazil, Argentina, and Paraguay is generating foreign exchange at record levels while failing to translate into broad-based domestic activity. The gap between what the export ledger shows and what households, manufacturers, and formal employment data confirm is not a temporary lag — it is becoming the defining political and financial risk of the 2027 electoral cycle across the region.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**Braskem (NYSE: BAK / B3: BRKM5):** Funds linked to Bracebridge Capital filed an involuntary Chapter 11 petition against Braskem America Finance Company, the subsidiary holding roughly USD 589 million in international notes — a parallel legal front to the company's ongoing out-of-court restructuring in Brazil that adds significant uncertainty to its credit profile.

**CMPC (Santiago: CMPC):** The Matte group accumulated close to USD 800 million in divestments in under a month — selling 60,000 forestry hectares in Maule to JP Morgan Natural Capital for USD 365.5 million and its corrugated business to Smurfit Westrock for USD 420 million — to finance the USD 4.6 billion Natureza pulp megaproject in Brazil.

**Prudential Financial / CNP Assurances:** Prudential announced the sale of its Brazilian and Mexican operations to France's CNP Assurances for USD 4.03 billion in cash, a transaction that will make Brazil CNP's largest single market, raising its share of revenue from 15% to 24% upon closing expected in the second half of 2027.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Brazil's runoff result on Sunday** will be the region's single most market-moving event: a Flávio Bolsonaro win could trigger the R$225 billion in foreign inflows BTG Pactual has modeled, while a Lula victory will force an immediate repricing of fiscal risk on a debt trajectory already approaching 82% of GDP.
- **Argentina's September EMAE release** will formally confirm or deny the technical recession that virtually all economists already treat as inevitable — and any reading that surprises to the downside will sharpen pressure on the government to recalibrate monetary policy ahead of the 2027 electoral cycle.
- **Chile's MK4 capital markets reform floor vote** and the first formal findings from the Superintendency of Pensions' AFP sanctioning process will determine whether the Fund E crisis remains a contained institutional controversy or escalates into a structural pension reform debate that reshapes the political agenda for the remainder of the Kast administration.
