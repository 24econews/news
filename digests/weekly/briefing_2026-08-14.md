# Profits Soar While Households Sink Across Mercosur

*Friday, August 14, 2026*

## The Big Picture

The dominant theme across Mercosur this week was the widening gap between corporate and sovereign performance on one hand, and household and fiscal reality on the other. In Brazil, [Nubank posted its largest quarterly profit in history](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-08-14-nubank-hits-record-profit-as-foreign-investors-flee) — US$1.061 billion in net income, up 49% year-on-year — while foreign investors pulled R$32 billion from Brazilian equities since April and [JPMorgan formally downgraded Brazilian stocks](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-08-12-jpmorgans-brazil-downgrade-triggers-worst-stock-rout-since), triggering the worst single-day selloff since March. In Argentina, [YPF reported the highest adjusted EBITDA in its history](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-08-11-ypf-hits-record-profits-as-argentinas-economy-stumbles-on) — USD 2.804 billion, up 150% year-on-year from Vaca Muerta — while country risk climbed toward 475 basis points and 59% of Argentines rated their economic situation as bad or very bad. In Chile, [Empresas Copec posted half-year profits of US$712 million, up 63%](https://www.24econews.com/chile/2026-08-14-corporate-profits-soar-while-chilean-household-insolvencies), on the same day that personal debt renegotiations soared 131% year-on-year. The pattern repeated itself across the region: balance sheets at the top of the economy are robust; the middle and bottom are under acute stress.

The second connecting thread was fiscal deterioration and the political limits of adjustment. [Paraguay acknowledged its fiscal deficit would close near 3.2% of GDP](https://www.24econews.com/paraguay/2026-08-10-paraguays-fiscal-deficit-surges-to-32-forcing-government-to) — more than double its legal 1.5% ceiling — with no return to compliance before 2028, even as its economy expanded at 6.6% in 2025. [Uruguay sounded international debt markets](https://www.24econews.com/uruguay/2026-08-13-uruguays-credibility-gap-investors-cheering-while-citizens) while its growth forecast was revised toward 1%, with half its population calling the economic situation poor. Brazil's Congress approved legislation expanding spending outside the fiscal framework in an election year, while the share of federal debt indexed to the Selic hit a 20-year high — a direct reflection of investor skepticism. And Bolivia, navigating the most acute crisis of the group, pushed through three simultaneous reforms in a single week — a new Investment Law, intervention at YPFB, and a formal monetary emissions ceiling — while diesel queues stretched to three days in Cochabamba.

For global investors, the read is nuanced but clear. The region's export sectors and large-cap corporates remain genuinely competitive — from Vaca Muerta to Vaca Muerta LNG, from Embraer's record quarter to Codelco's capitalisation milestone and Antofagasta's 72% profit jump. But electoral calendars in Brazil and Argentina are beginning to price into risk assets, the consumer-led growth models in both countries are visibly exhausting, and fiscal slippage in Paraguay and Bolivia introduces sovereign risk that investment-grade ratings may not fully capture for long. The divergence between what financial markets celebrate and what households experience is not a temporary lag — it is becoming the defining structural tension of the second half of 2026.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**JBS (NYSE: JBS):** Indonesia's sovereign wealth fund Danantara will invest US$2.5 billion in a joint venture covering JBS's Australia and New Zealand assets, valued at US$7.5 billion — half the company's total market cap. The deal also confirmed that Wesley Batista Filho, 34, will assume global CEO duties in January 2027, consolidating family control of the world's largest protein company.

**Nubank (NYSE: NU):** The Brazilian fintech reported record net income of US$1.061 billion in Q2 2026, up 49% year-on-year, with ROE of 33% and a risk-adjusted net interest margin that expanded from 9.5% to 12.4% quarter-on-quarter — shares surged ~9% in after-hours trading, a stark counterpoint to the broader Brazilian market rout.

**YPF (NYSE: YPF):** Beyond its record EBITDA quarter, YPF filed the Argentina LNG project under RIGI — a USD 51 billion venture with Eni and ADNOC's XRG — while simultaneously divesting its Metrogas stake to Edenor for USD 780 million, reshaping its balance sheet around a single unconventional bet on Vaca Muerta.
