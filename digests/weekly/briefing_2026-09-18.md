# Reform Momentum Crashes Into Election-Year Fiscal Reality

*Friday, September 18, 2026*

## The Big Picture

The dominant theme this week across Mercosur was the collision between reform momentum and political fragility — a tension sharpest in the two economies generating the most headlines. In Brazil, [Super Wednesday delivered the expected Copom cut](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-09-16-brazil-cuts-rates-while-fed-tightens-amid-oil-shock-and) — a fifth consecutive 25bp reduction bringing the Selic to 13.75% — but the real story was what the central bank refused to say: its statement avoided any forward guidance on November, citing "significant uncertainty" and "de-anchored expectations" in language analysts read as a soft pause signal. Hours later, [a 15% Bolsa Família increase worth R$5.8 billion this year and R$22.7 billion in 2027](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-09-18-governments-r58-billion-bolsa-familia-increase-triggers) arrived with an election 17 days away, triggering a devastating IFI response: the independent fiscal body calculated the government's 2027 primary deficit could reach R$86.1 billion — versus the R$18.6 billion surplus projected by the Executive, a R$104.8 billion gap. In Argentina, [the 2027 Budget landed in Congress projecting 4% growth and 18% inflation](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-09-16-mileis-2027-budget-defies-global-turmoil-as-exports-surge) while private analysts forecast 2.9% and 20.5% respectively, and [second-quarter GDP data confirmed unemployment rising to 7.9%](https://www.24econews.com/argentina/2026-09-18-argentinas-jobless-surge-as-domestic-demand-collapses) with private consumption contracting 2.4% quarter-on-quarter, even as the export sector posted its strongest year in history.

The external environment amplified every domestic stress point. The Federal Reserve's unanimous decision to raise rates to 3.75%–4.00% under Kevin Warsh — the first hike since 2023 — sent a hawkish signal across emerging markets, with the U.S. 10-year Treasury briefly touching 5.04%, its highest since 2007. Brent crude above $108 added a second vector of pressure: it fueled the Fed's resolve, complicated Brazil's disinflation path, consumed hundreds of millions in Chilean fuel subsidies, and threatened the margins of every economy reliant on energy imports. The monetary divergence — Brazil easing while the Fed tightens — produced the unusual spectacle of [the real outperforming regional peers](https://www.24econews.com/brazil/2026-09-17-brazils-real-outperforms-as-fed-tightens-and-central-bank) on the day of the simultaneous decisions, but the underlying tension between a Selic at 13.75% and a Fed signaling further hikes has narrowed the window for continued Brazilian easing considerably.

For global investors, the week distilled a core risk that runs across the region: fiscal credibility is being tested simultaneously in countries at very different stages of their reform cycles. Bolivia's Chamber of Deputies approved the IMF's $1.9 billion program with more than two-thirds of the vote, yet [the minister who designed it had already been censured and dismissed](https://www.24econews.com/bolivia/2026-09-16-fuel-subsidy-collapse-threatens-bolivias-imf-agreement-and), while a parliamentary commission documented YPFB spending 470 million bolivianos on fuel-tracking systems that were never operational. [Uruguay's GDP contracted 0.8% in the second quarter](https://www.24econews.com/uruguay/2026-09-16-uruguays-farm-collapse-exposes-growth-mirage-as-forecasts) and its Consejo Fiscal Autónomo publicly labeled the government's projections "overestimated." [Paraguay's Caja Fiscal reform passed as a "minimal" measure](https://www.24econews.com/paraguay/2026-09-17-strong-growth-masks-paraguays-fiscal-crisis-and-debt-spiral) that experts say squanders a historic opportunity, while the state owes contractors more than $300 million. The message for portfolio managers is consistent: macroeconomic stability headlines are increasingly disconnected from institutional execution capacity. That gap is where this region's real risk lives.

## Corporate Watch of the Week

**Nubank (NYSE: NU) — US expansion and global account launch.** The Brazilian fintech announced the launch of US operations targeting 100–150 million unbanked customers, while simultaneously rolling out a 35-country global account with free transfers, placing it in direct competition with Revolut at a moment when Revolut obtained a provisional US banking license. The move marks Latin America's most ambitious fintech cross-border push of the year.

**A5X — R$360mn Series D at R$2.7bn valuation.** The new Brazilian derivatives exchange raised its Series D led by Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and Kaszek, with participation from global market makers including Jump Trading, Optiver, and XTX Markets — a capital composition that signals concrete intent to contest B3's monopoly in high-margin futures products.

**YPF (NYSE: YPF) — Record bond issuance and LNG progress.** YPF placed $1.2bn in 9-year notes at a 300bp spread over Treasuries — its tightest spread ever — while CEO Horacio Marín confirmed Argentina LNG is close to signing 2–3 sales contracts with Asian and European buyers ahead of a November final investment decision on what would be Argentina's largest-ever private investment at $51bn over 20 years.

## What to Watch Next Week

- **Argentina's IMF third review (from Sept. 21) and poverty data (Sept. 24):** The Fund's mission arrives as Caputo and Milei are in New York for the UN General Assembly, and must assess the first-half fiscal target miss alongside $800mn in principal due Sept. 25. The INDEC poverty figure, expected to show between 1.5 and 2 million new poor relative to the 2025 low, will be the week's highest-stakes political number across the entire region.
- **Brazil's bimonthly revenue and expenditure report (Sept. 24):** The government must demonstrate R$6bn in fiscal space to accommodate the Bolsa Família increase. Combined with any TCU ruling on the decree's suspension and the next Focus survey reading on inflation expectations, this report will determine whether the IFI's devastating R$104.8bn gap estimate gains or loses market credibility.
- **Chile's 2027 Budget submission and Uruguay's Competitiveness Bill progress:** Both governments face their first major legislative tests of the reform agendas sold to investors at ChileDay — Chile's budget must square fiscal austerity with a 9.5% unemployment rate, while Uruguay's Parliament will signal whether the competitiveness bill has the votes to move before the economic picture deteriorates further.
