Less than two decades after the nationalization of hydrocarbons became the most potent symbol of the MAS political project, Rodrigo Paz's government has decreed the intervention of YPFB for 180 days, entrusting its oversight to a commission made up of five ministries, while the Agencia Nacional de Hidrocarburos undergoes a parallel review by an intervention commission of its own. The implicit diagnosis in both measures is devastating: the company that was meant to be the engine of energy sovereignty reaches 2026 with fuel production at levels insufficient to meet domestic consumption, irregular fuel dispatches within its ranks according to reports from Los Tiempos, and a 13.4% contraction in the hydrocarbons sector that is dragging the entire economy down with it.

The crisis is anything but abstract.
