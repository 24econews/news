# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-11

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-11*

The Argentine market reopens this Wednesday after two sessions without local activity, carrying a data point that wasn't in investors' script barely a week ago: country risk, which last Friday had brushed 940 basis points — its highest level since the October 2025 midterm elections — is now trading around 853 points following a partial recovery that nonetheless coexists with an external environment that grew more complicated precisely during the hours of local paralysis. The combination of a hotter-than-expected U.S. inflation print, a weakly bid U.S. Treasury auction, and the near-consolidated certainty of a 50-basis-point Federal Reserve rate hike in December returns the Argentine market to a tension its operators know well: sovereign assets improving on their own merits but sailing against an adverse global wind.

The U.S. consumer price index rose 0.4% month-on-month in October, above the 0.3% expected, raising to 85% the share of analysts and investors pricing in a December rate move. With fed funds positioning toward a 4.25%-4.50% range, and ten-year Treasuries rolling over at 5.02% in the latest auction, the global cost of capital is climbing for all emerging markets. Argentina — with a country risk that quadruples the regional average of 208 points and more than sextuples that of Paraguay, whose financial crisis around Ueno Bank even led to the resignation of its president — remains Latin America's second-highest risk premium, trailing only Venezuela. Morgan Stanley estimates that the weight bond prices assign to an adverse post-election scenario jumped in three months from a range of 11-20% to one of 41-52%, a sign the market is buying 2027 political risk at a price the government considers overdone.

The central paradox dominating local analysts' reading is precisely that: while sovereign risk has doubled from the 402-point lows of July, the official exchange rate hasn't moved. The wholesale dollar opens this Wednesday at $1,545, with the spread to the contado con liquidación — trading around $1,679 — holding at manageable levels. The government intervened actively in the free FX market, in futures, and in the financial segments to prevent the exchange rate from becoming the adjustment variable for political noise, according to the description circulating among consultancies. The Central Bank's implicit crawling peg, depreciating the peso at a little over 1% monthly — below inflation — sustains a scheme that can only hold as long as external FX flows cooperate. For now, that flow exists: the BCRA has purchased more than USD 16 billion year-to-date, propelled by record exports from energy, mining, and agriculture.

That's the other side of the story, and it's an extraordinarily good side. Oil companies operating in Argentina took in USD 17.475 billion from crude sales between January and August, 42% more than the same period of 2025, according to consultancy Economía & Energía. Vaca Muerta produced 700,000 barrels per day in September, 28% more than a year earlier. Argentine gold exports topped USD 2.921 billion in the first half, up 52%, with Switzerland as the main destination. Wheat posted historic record shipments in the September-October bimester, and Brazil — one of the main buyers — is heading for a deficit harvest that opens an unprecedented opportunity for Argentine exporters. Wine exports also surprised to the upside in October, with a 19.3% jump in value and 28% in volume, though the annual cumulative still fails to reverse the negative balance.

The problem is that this export boom isn't trickling down to the domestic economy. Manufacturing output fell 1.8% year-on-year in September, though it posted a slight 0.7% monthly improvement. SME retail sales accumulated a 2% real decline year-to-date, and October sales fell 1.6% year-on-year. CyberMonday 2026 sold 20% less in real terms than the previous year. Consumer credit has been falling for six consecutive months. According to private estimates, the economy will close 2026 with growth close to zero or even negative, with a carryover effect that conditions the start of 2027.

In that context, the RIGI — the Large Investment Incentive Regime — emerges as the government's long-term bet, but its own numbers raise uncomfortable questions. The 27 approved projects total USD 76.173 billion in commitments but would generate only about 133,000 jobs, equivalent to roughly two positions for every million dollars invested. Oil and gas concentrate 60.6% of the capital but barely 29% of projected employment. Imports linked to the regime hit a monthly peak of USD 140 million in October, reopening the debate on what space remains for local industry within the scheme. The government responded with a business roundtable in Rosario that brought the large RIGI companies together with 1,800 SMEs, a sign that the productive problem is recognized though not yet solved.

Meanwhile, the consortium formed by YPF, Eni, and Adnoc's investment arm — the Emirati XRG — launched the largest pipeline tender in Argentine energy history, valued at around USD 2 billion, for the Argentina LNG trunk gas pipeline. In parallel, San Matías Pipeline has already begun welding on the duct that will connect Vaca Muerta to the San Matías Gulf to export liquefied natural gas. These are the two pillars of a project that promises USD 10 billion in annual exports and that, on the scale of the country's structural transformation, represents perhaps the most ambitious bet in decades.

On the political-financial front, Donald Trump's defeat in the U.S. midterm elections generated the sharpest drop in oil prices since the pandemic — Brent gave up 16% in a week before stabilizing below USD 90 — which eased global inflationary pressures but cut local oil-sector revenue. AmCham warned that the new U.S. political scenario could condition financial support to Argentina in 2027, including the precedent of the USD 20 billion swap. Minister Caputo himself acknowledged before FIEL that "as we advance, our economy is more exposed to what happens in the world."

What lies ahead is a compressed, dense agenda. On Thursday, Indec will publish October's inflation figure, which private consultancies place around 2% monthly — a slight acceleration from September. Early high-frequency readings for November show more moderate signals, though the effect of CyberMonday discounts distorts the signal. The CGT confirmed it is preparing a strike action before year-end. Banco Ciudad received from Moody's the highest local credit rating, AAA.ar, in a data point that contrasts with the pressure sovereign risk exerts on the financial system as a whole. And starting next Monday, the new prior-control regime for economic concentrations takes effect, substantially modifying the rules for mergers and acquisitions in the country. The local market, which reopens today with accumulated lag and without the full backing of the U.S. bond market owing to Veterans Day, will have to process all of that at once.
