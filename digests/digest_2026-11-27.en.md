> TITLE: Argentina's risk premium crashes below 1,000 basis points on legislative sweep

# Argentina: Economic Pulse — 2026-11-27

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-27*

Argentina's country risk fell 107 basis points on Friday — a 10% drop in a single session — to close below 1,000 basis points, piercing a psychological barrier the market had taken weeks to recover. The move capped a sequence of legislative and financial signals that, combined with the regional tailwind from Brazil, delivered a provisional reprieve to assets that had absorbed a severe beating through November.

The immediate catalyst was a cluster of legislative wins. Congress enacted the reform of the Central Bank's Charter — passed in the lower house by 141 votes in favor with none against — establishing the preservation of the currency's value as the monetary authority's sole mandate and expressly prohibiting financing to the Treasury, provinces and municipalities. In parallel, the Senate granted initial approval to the suspension of the open primaries (PASO), while the lower house approved the 2027 Budget in the early morning hours by 137 votes in favor. The ruling bloc lost the arm-wrestling match over university funding — the opposition imposed $11 trillion versus the $7.35 trillion in the official bill — but the tally of victories in a single legislative night was enough to shift market sentiment.

Layered on top was the financial plan that Minister Luis Caputo laid out publicly to cover the nearly USD 4.37 billion maturity in January 2027: roughly USD 1.2 billion from the IMF and multilateral agencies, another USD 1 billion in guaranteed financing from CAF and Fonplata, USD 300 million from the sale of AySA, and USD 1.8 billion that the Treasury will purchase directly from the Central Bank using pesos from the primary surplus. The scheme cleared the main doubt that had pressured bonds over the previous two weeks, though the July 2027 maturity — USD 5.7 billion — remains without an equivalent answer. Deputy Minister José Luis Daza further confirmed that the government has submitted the formal request to activate USD 5 billion from the swap line with China as a liquidity cushion.

The external backdrop also cooperated. The appointment of banker Luiz Carlos Trabuco Cappi — chairman of Bradesco's board — as incoming Finance Minister in Brazil under Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's next term triggered an immediate reaction across regional markets: the real appreciated close to 2% and the Bovespa index posted a 6% gain on Thursday, its biggest advance in months. The stabilization of the Brazilian currency took pressure off Argentina's official exchange rate, which closed Friday near $1,605 per wholesale dollar after accumulating a rise of roughly 4.5% over the course of November. ADRs of Argentine companies listed in New York climbed as much as 5% in the shortened Black Friday session, led by Grupo Galicia (+5.3%), Central Puerto (+5.1%) and Banco Supervielle (+5%).

The drop in country risk is welcome, but it doesn't resolve the underlying tensions. JP Morgan, in its Argentina 2027 Economic Outlook report signed by Diego Pereira and Lucila Barbeito, projects 1.8% growth for 2027 and inflation of 23%, but warns that electoral uncertainty accounts for roughly 43% of the current country risk level — illustrated by the nearly 17-percentage-point gap between the yield on the AO27 bond, which matures before the change of government, and the AO28, which matures after. Moody's, for its part, noted that "for the moment" it will not downgrade the sovereign rating it raised in July 2026, when country risk was hovering near 400 points.

While markets were recovering ground, the real economy was sending signals in the opposite direction. Private estimates from Analytica, Orlando J. Ferreres & Asociados and Equilibra all agreed that activity contracted both month-on-month and year-on-year in October 2026, with construction as the principal drag. Mass consumption isn't rebounding either: supermarket sales have now logged nine consecutive months of year-on-year declines, with a 2.9% contraction in the first nine months of 2026 relative to the same period a year earlier, according to Indec. More revealing still, 59% of tenant households in the Buenos Aires Metropolitan Area took on debt over the past year, up from 37% in 2025, and 72% of those who borrowed did so to pay rent. In the non-bank credit provider segment — apps, cards, cooperatives — the delinquency rate reached 30% in August, according to the Central Bank itself.

The energy backdrop offers the sharpest contrast. The government formally approved Argentina LNG's admission to the RIGI via a resolution signed by Caputo, enabling the landmark USD 50.75 billion investment that YPF will execute together with Italy's ENI and the UAE's XRG. The Final Investment Decision (FID) will be signed between Sunday and Monday at an event for 500 people at the Palacio Libertad. The project contemplates annual exports of USD 10 billion starting in 2031, with investment of roughly USD 25 billion concentrated between 2027 and 2030. Chinese company Wison will build the two floating liquefaction vessels, valued together at close to USD 7 billion, while Chinese financial institutions are in talks to participate in the financing. That energy geopolitics coexists with Washington's concerns: a bipartisan commission of the U.S. Congress warned in November about the risks of growing economic dependence on China in Latin America, with several passages devoted to Argentina.

What the market will be watching closely in the coming weeks is multiple: approval of the third review of the IMF agreement, expected in the first week of December; the handling of the 2027 Budget in the Senate and the tension around university funding, which the government fears could break the fiscal balance; the completion of the AySA sale and the progress of the privatization roadshow that ATEP head Diego Chaher will present on December 10 in Washington. November inflation, which consultancies estimate at around 1.8%, could be the first genuinely good news for the government in weeks. But the real test will come in January, when the first USD 4.37 billion comes due and the market finds out whether Caputo's financial scheme is executed as promised.

### YPF (NYSE: YPF / BCBA: YPFD)

### Grupo Financiero Galicia (NASDAQ: GGAL / BCBA: GGAL)
The bank's shares rebounded 5.3% in New York on Friday as part of the broad rally in Argentine assets; in parallel, the institution launched Éminent Élite, a new high-net-worth banking segment targeting clients with monthly income above USD 25,000 or more than USD 500,000 in investments, aimed at roughly 210,000 eligible clients within its current base.
