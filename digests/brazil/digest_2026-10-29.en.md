# Brazil: The Economic Pulse — 2026-10-29

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-10-29*

The Ibovespa has shed 16.6% in three trading sessions — and the destruction of individual value is even more brutal than the index suggests.

The re-election of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva to an unprecedented fourth term, confirmed by the TSE on Sunday, has triggered one of the most violent corrections in the Brazilian market in years. On Monday, the index activated the circuit breaker and closed down 13.88%, the dollar surged 8.21% to R$5.40, and DI contract rates jumped more than 300 basis points in a single session. On Wednesday, the Ibovespa gave up another 2.67%, closing at 171,006 points, while the dollar stabilized after the Federal Reserve held U.S. rates steady. The sector-level bloodletting is eloquent: Vamos has lost 60% of its market value since the vote count, Magazine Luiza is down 59%, Cosan 55%, Hapvida 52%, and Cyrela 50%. Casas Bahia, already in judicial reorganization, plunged another 20% on Wednesday alone.

But the market is not reacting solely to the PT victory. It is reacting to the devastating combination of three simultaneous factors: the absence of a clear fiscal signal from the incoming government, a tax rate that came in well above expectations, and a household debt picture that the Central Bank has just portrayed in all its gravity.

The first shock came from the TCU, which unanimously approved on Wednesday a reference rate of 11.3% for the CBS — the Contribution on Goods and Services that will replace PIS and Cofins starting in January 2027. The number surprised tax experts and the market, which had been expecting something between 8% and 9.5%. If the estimated 18.7% rate for the state and municipal IBS is confirmed when it takes effect in 2028, Brazil will operate with a combined VAT of 30%, becoming the country with the highest value-added tax in the world — above any European nation. The Finance Ministry is trying to manage anxiety, arguing that the effective average rate will come in below 8.5% after sectoral exemptions are applied, and the architect of the reform, Bernard Appy, insisted that the 11.3% figure is imprecise and tends to decline through 2033. But for thousands of companies just over 60 days from the start of the next fiscal year, uncertainty carries an immediate planning cost — and BTG revised its 2027 IPCA projection from 4.8% to 5.9%, flashing a yellow light on the continuity of the rate-cutting cycle.

The second shock is fiscal and political. The market is pressing for urgency in naming the finance minister as a way to signal commitment to budget responsibility, but the PT is resisting the pressure, and the internal dispute between those defending the adjustment — embodied by current acting minister Dario Durigan and Planning Minister Bruno Moretti — and those preaching the party's historic developmentalism, with Guido Mantega among the influential veterans, is far from settled. According to Valor Econômico, the conversation remains intense inside the Planalto and Alvorada palaces. Rogério Xavier, co-founder of SPX Capital — whose Nimitz fund retreated only 0.6% on Monday against an average 4% drop in its category — summed up the posture of the more prudent managers: "We have opinions, not facts." Funds with explicit political engagement in the Flávio Bolsonaro campaign, such as Exploritas, suffered return collapses that turned a positive monthly result of 72.88% in October into a loss of 42.92% in a span of days.

The third shock is structural. The Central Bank revealed that the number of Brazilians in over-indebtedness reached 20.3 million in June, up 75% over five years. Household debt service as a share of income hit 29%, the highest level in 21 years — and for the population earning up to two minimum wages, that figure rises to 42%. JPMorgan, in a note following its review of the BC's Financial Stability Report, maintained caution on Brazilian banks, projecting margin compression, slower loan book growth, and higher provisions. Santander Brasil's numbers confirm the thesis: profit fell 28% year-on-year to R$2.9 billion, 7% below consensus, with ROE of 11.9% versus 17.5% a year earlier.

Against this austere backdrop, a few narratives are emerging that point to the tensions of Lula 4. Petrobras, whose ADRs trade on the NYSE, hit simultaneous records in oil and diesel production in the third quarter — 3.512 million barrels of oil equivalent per day, up 11.7% year-on-year — but faces mounting pressure from its board to correct the gap between domestic fuel prices and international markets. For diesel, that gap reached 127% after the post-election currency jump, according to Abicom. The adjustment needed would be so abrupt that it would, by itself, generate an inflationary shock. The decision on how and when to make it is one of the first ticking time bombs of the new term. At the same time, states approved through Confaz an increase in ICMS rates on fuels effective February 2027, adding further pressure on prices in an environment already strained by the war in Iran.

On the corporate agenda, Raízen closed a R$22.4 billion tax settlement with the PGFN and the Federal Revenue Service, the largest such operation announced in recent months. BP made a new hydrocarbon discovery in the Tupinambá block, in the Santos Basin, near the Bumerangue field where it had announced its largest discovery in 25 years in 2025. The TCU approved with modifications the return of more than 3,000 kilometers of railways from the Transnordestina concession, held by the CSN group, with R$1.8 billion in compensation to the federal government. And the Federal Police indicted Daniel Vorcaro, founder of Banco Master, and 14 other individuals — including the former president of BRB — on suspicion of financial crimes in the sale of credit portfolios to the state-owned bank, while Master's liquidator is pursuing an action in the United States to recover a R$140 million Miami apartment he alleges was acquired with diverted funds.

What to watch in the coming sessions is simple and decisive: the name of the finance minister. Each day of silence feeds uncertainty about the fiscal trajectory and prolongs the pressure on domestic assets. The yield curve, the exchange rate, and the Ibovespa are, at this moment, waiting on a single political variable to recalibrate.

## Corporate Watch

### Petrobras (NYSE: PBR / B3: PETR4)
The state-controlled company posted record average production of 3.512 million barrels of oil equivalent per day in the third quarter, up 11.7% year-on-year, driven by the P-78 and P-79 platforms at the Búzios field and by peak production at Mero; at the same time, the board pressured management to correct the 127% diesel price gap versus the international market, an adjustment that could generate direct inflationary impact at the start of Lula 4.

### Itaú Unibanco (B3: ITUB4 / NYSE: ITUB)
Latin America's largest bank acquired NeoSpace, a Brazilian foundational AI startup, for approximately R$3 billion, with the declared goal of transforming the institution into a "financial ChatGPT," according to CIO Ricardo Guerra; the deal mirrors the playbook of the Zup acquisition for R$500 million in 2019, but with considerably greater scale and ambition.

### Ambev (B3: ABEV3)
The company reported adjusted net profit of R$4.39 billion in the third quarter, up 14.3% year-on-year and above the consensus estimate of R$3.9 billion, supported by organic revenue growth of 7.6% and organic adjusted EBITDA growth of 8.9%, with the premium portfolio and diversification into alternative beverages offsetting volume pressures in the beer segment.
