Oil surged past $109 per barrel on Thursday after the Houthis seized control of the port of Mocha in Yemen — and this external shock reshaped, in a matter of hours, Brazil's entire economic agenda, from the exchange rate to fiscal policy, taking in Petrobras's credibility and the cost of living on the eve of a presidential election.

The Ibovespa closed up 1.42% at 188,268 points, buoyed by gains in energy companies, while the dollar ended at R$5.10 — a notable reversal from the previous session, when the U.S. currency had climbed to R$5.11 amid caution triggered by the very same oil rally. The divergence between domestic assets and international markets was stark: Asian stocks fell on Friday, tracking losses on Wall Street, where 30-year U.S. Treasury yields hit 5.35%, the highest level since 2007, pressured by the combination of surging oil and a U.S. fiscal deficit approaching 6% of GDP. For Brazil, the rise in long-dated Treasuries — which Valor Econômico describes as "bad news" for emerging markets — raises the cost of external capital at a moment when the country itself is struggling to demonstrate fiscal discipline.

It is against this backdrop that Fitch chose precisely this Thursday to issue a warning: Brazil's 2027 budget proposal reveals serious difficulties in fiscal consolidation, and the projected pace is insufficient to stabilize public debt. The agency was blunt in asserting that the country's fiscal credibility will be shaped by the outcome of the upcoming elections and by the willingness of Congress — a diagnosis that shifts the economic debate onto political terrain at a moment when Vice President Geraldo Alckmin was, on that very day, promising business leaders that a potential second Lula term would deliver a primary surplus. The pledge is ambitious given the landscape: Correios has formally requested a sovereign guarantee for a new R$7 billion loan from four international banks — Banco ABC, Citibank, Deutsche Bank, and J.P. Meanwhile, the FGTS Trustee Council approved the full withdrawal of the R$5.85 billion available in the FGTS Investment Fund — ten years without disbursements, and now the fund is drained all at once.

The oil shock has an immediately domestic and politically incendiary side: the lag in diesel prices has surged to levels equivalent to those seen at the start of the Iran war, and the Lula administration announced a new package of subsidies on the eve of the election. Beyond fuels, the government is proposing the first PPSA auction for October, opening to the market the pre-salt natural gas belonging to the federal government — and Petrobras has already signed an agreement with the state entity to commercialize that volume, signaling a new arrangement in the gas market that Scotiabank analysts consider secondary in light of the transformational potential of the Foz do Amazonas Basin, whose northwestern portion could produce up to 842,000 barrels per day of oil at peak in 2038.

Master's Cayman Islands holding company held its first creditors' meeting — with claims of $1 billion — with no cash available to present, according to a report by liquidator Grant Thornton. STF Justice Luiz Fux gave the government, the BC, and the FGC 15 days to respond to the Federal District's request for the federal government to guarantee a loan to bail out BRB, a bank orbiting the periphery of this financial crisis. It is a storyline that combines poor regulatory supervision, moral hazard, and institutional instability — and one that Fitch, not by coincidence, implicitly cites in conditioning Brazil's fiscal credibility on the electoral outcome.

The episode comes at a moment when the Brazilian financial system is undergoing structural transformations: the Banco Central's regulation of crypto assets is expected to shrink the sector to no more than ten licensed operators, triggering a wave of closures and mergers that already includes Bitnuvem, NovaDAX, and Coinext. ### MercadoLibre (Nasdaq: MELI)
The company founded by Marcos Galperin raised $1 billion with the issuance of a 10-year bond maturing in 2036, at a spread of 130 basis points over the equivalent Treasury, with peak demand of $2.1 billion; the spread was aligned with those of other Brazilian investment-grade issuers such as Suzano and JBS, cementing the company's credit perception in the international market.

### Light (B3: LIGT3)
The Rio de Janeiro electricity distributor formally concluded its judicial reorganization initiated in May 2023, following a ruling by the 3rd Business Court of the Rio District, and forecasts investments of R$10 billion by 2030, including in-housing of outsourced teams and process rationalization; according to a survey by RGF Consultoria, only 17.5% of companies that have resorted to the mechanism since 2013 have successfully completed the process, making Light's case an exception in a sector with an 88% success rate.
