# Brazil: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-27

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-27*

The "Trabuco effect" produced on Friday the sharpest weekly collapse of the dollar since October 2008, compressed medium-term interest rate futures by more than 150 basis points, and completely repositioned the market's bets on the Selic trajectory in 2027 — a move of such magnitude that it turns the appointment of banker Luiz Carlos Trabuco to the Finance Ministry into Brazil's most significant market event in years.

The U.S. currency closed the week at R$5.51, with a cumulative 7% drop over five sessions — a performance that, on a weekly basis, had no precedent since the 2008 global financial crisis, according to Valor Econômico. On Friday, the dollar fell another 4%, amplified by a nearly 1% decline in Brent, which closed at $95.44 a barrel. The Ibovespa, which had surged more than 6% on Thursday on expectations of the nomination's confirmation, ended Friday virtually flat, down 0.13% at 176,573 points — an accommodation consistent with the "buy the rumor, sell the fact" dynamic described by fund managers interviewed by the press. The week nonetheless registered a 6.13% gain for the index, its best performance since the week following the first round of this year's presidential election.

The most eloquent move came on the yield curve. The DI for January 2028 retreated from 14.185% to 13.67%, and the January 2031 contract tumbled from 14.62% to 13.89%, according to Valor. On Tesouro Direto, the Prefixado 2032 fell 65 basis points, closing the session at 14.17%, while the Prefixado 2029 ended at 13.80%. The repricing was enough to rewrite the consensus monetary policy scenario: whereas until last week the market was pricing in a Selic above 15% by the end of 2027, the curves now price in 13.64% — effectively flat or even slightly below the current 13.75%. The implied probability of a 25 basis point hike at December's Copom meeting, which had stood at 16%, plunged to 5.3%.

Bank of America synthesized external consensus in a note released Friday, stating that Trabuco's nomination "shrinks negative scenarios" and "sends an important signal that Lula is willing to respond to market concerns." The EWZ ETF, which tracks the Ibovespa in New York, closed the U.S. session — shortened by the Thanksgiving holiday — up 9.81%, according to Folha de S.Paulo. Bradesco BBI upgraded its recommendation on Brazilian equities to overweight.

But the enthusiasm is not unqualified. Lula himself, during a visit to Universidade Federal Fluminense on Friday, promised "some adjustments" and higher-quality spending, but guaranteed he would not cut social benefits or education expenditures — a framing that reproduces the central tension of his fourth term. Allies describe Trabuco as an attempt to reconcile fiscal discipline with the preservation of the social program, but foreign banks, according to Folha, make the effective improvement of the fiscal accounts conditional on the new minister's real autonomy within the government. Henrique Meirelles, who followed an analogous path when he moved from the private sector to the Central Bank during Lula's first administration, warned that the new minister "will need firmness." The current economic team, led on an interim basis by Durigan, is working to deliver a fiscal package ready before the handover, as a starting point for the Trabuco administration.

The macroeconomic data released Friday reinforce the urgency of the challenge awaiting the incoming minister. The Central Bank reported that household delinquency on non-earmarked credit reached 8.2% in October, the highest level in the historical series that began in 2011. Total credit stock grew just 7.8% in the twelve months through October — the slowest expansion since February 2020 — reflecting the monetary tightening cycle with the Selic at 13.75%. Services sector confidence fell in November to its lowest level in five and a half years, according to FGV, pressured by deteriorating post-election expectations. Retail confidence fell 8.2 points in November to 74.9 points, the lowest level since March 2021.

Running against this current, the government published impressive numbers from the National Treasury: October's primary surplus reached R$52.2 billion, the second-best result for the month in the historical series beginning in 1995. And unemployment stood at 5.1% in the quarter ended in October, the lowest level for the period since 2012, according to IBGE — although FGV Ibre warns that a weaker labor market in 2027 may pressure average monthly income.

Two sector developments deserve attention. B3 announced on Friday an agreement with Bradesco, BTG Pactual, Itaú Unibanco, Safra, Santander and XP to develop a real-referenced stablecoin and a shared digital asset infrastructure — a meaningful step for the digitalization of the Brazilian financial system in line with global trends. On the diplomatic-commercial front, Brazil faces two open battles: Emmanuel Macron described Brazilian beef as "doped" and defended the European Union's barriers, triggering a protest note from the Ministries of Foreign Affairs and Agriculture, while the animal protein sector is pressing for Lula's direct involvement to reopen chicken exports to the EU, suspended since September.

The week ahead will be one of consolidation or of testing for the rally. The market will monitor the formal presentation of the fiscal package to Trabuco's team, any signals on the scope of spending-containment measures, and the progress of a bill in the Senate — denounced by specialists as a rollback of the 2016 State-Owned Enterprises Law — that would reduce from 36 months to just 30 days the cooling-off period for politicians to take positions in state-owned companies and regulatory agencies.

## Corporate Watch

### Petrobras (NYSE: PBR / B3: PETR4)
The state-owned oil company's preferred shares fell 2.43% on Friday, pressured by the dollar's decline — which erodes real-denominated revenues — by the drop in Brent, and by news that the company intends to absorb ExxonMobil's (40%) and QatarEnergy's (30%) stakes in an exploration block in the Campos Basin acquired for R$2.82 billion in 2018, raising its stake to 100%; analysts note that the move pressures cash generation given the scale of investment involved.

### Raízen (B3: RAIZ4)
Founding shareholder Rubens Ometto confirmed to Reuters on Friday that the company is evaluating the sale of its fuel distribution division as part of a broader divestment plan; Raízen, a joint venture between Cosan and Shell with direct exposure to the global energy and ethanol markets, did not disclose the timing or potential value of the transaction.

### B3 (B3: B3SA3)
The operator of the Brazilian stock exchange announced on Friday an agreement with six financial institutions — Bradesco, BTG Pactual, Itaú Unibanco, Safra, Santander and XP — to develop a real-referenced stablecoin and a shared infrastructure for digital assets, positioning Brazil's capital markets in line with similar financial digitalization moves observed in other global marketplaces.
