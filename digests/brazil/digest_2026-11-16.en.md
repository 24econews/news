# Brazil: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-16

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-16*

Brazilian financial markets sent an unmistakable message today to the recently reelected government of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva: Senate approval of the base text of the so-called "Moretti plan" was not enough to convince investors that the public debt trajectory is under control. The dollar jumped more than 1% at Monday's open, surpassing R$5.73 at the intraday high, while the Ibovespa retreated toward the 170,900-point range and futures yields climbed across the entire curve — with the DI contract for January 2031 reaching 15.00% and the longest-dated paper, maturing in 2037, hitting 15.135%. Fifteen consecutive trading sessions of foreign capital outflows, with a negative balance of R$5.4 billion for the month, complete the picture of a market that does not believe the announced fiscal adjustment is sufficient.

The contradiction is stark and, for markets, unsettling: the government unveils an adjustment package, the Senate approves the base text, Vice President Geraldo Alckmin declares that the proposal "goes in the right direction" and could help bring down interest rates — and yet risk premiums keep rising. The explanation lies in both the magnitude and the composition of the plan. PLP 299/2026 projects an impact of up to R$60 billion in 2027, targeting a primary surplus of 0.5% of GDP, but economists consulted by Folha de S.Paulo assess that the proposal falls short of what is needed and leaves untouched the main sources of budget rigidity — the constitutional floors for health and education and the indexation of the minimum wage to real growth. The economic team already acknowledges the package's limitations and, according to Folha, is studying a second phase that would involve a constitutional amendment to tackle spending currently outside the fiscal framework's limits.

There is also a political problem that markets are pricing in. Planning Minister Bruno Moretti was forced to publicly criticize a faction within his own PT for obstructing the measures, claiming that a "sectarian left" is delaying the fight against privileges. The text returns this week to the Chamber of Deputies for a new round of votes, and the risk that the toughest measures will be watered down — such as the review of tax benefits and the possibility of freezing mandatory expenditures — remains high. To make matters worse, a survey by Valor Econômico reveals that electoral promises made on the eve of the runoff have already created an additional fiscal impact of R$40 billion for 2027 that was not included in the original budget proposal, further narrowing the space for adjustment.

Against this backdrop, the Focus bulletin released this morning by the central bank offers little comfort. The median projection for IPCA inflation in 2027 rose for the fifth consecutive week, reaching 5.00%, while expectations for the Selic rate at the end of 2027 climbed from 12.50% to 12.75%. The GDP growth forecast for 2026 was trimmed from 1.75% to 1.72%, and for 2027 it fell more sharply, from 1.15% to 1.02%. The data were accompanied by the September IBC-Br, released by the BCB, which fell 0.25% month-over-month — a slightly better result than the median expectation of a 0.3% decline, but one that confirms the slowdown in activity in the third quarter, with a 0.9% contraction over the period. Industry led the drop, with a 1.2% retreat in September from August.

The combination of rising interest rates and decelerating activity is beginning to produce concrete effects on the banking system. Banco do Brasil is negotiating with the BCB and the Ministry of Finance for more flexible provisioning rules for losses on rural credit, in the face of what the institution describes as the worst crisis in its history in agribusiness. Caixa Econômica Federal, in turn, has already scaled back lending to the sector and expanded provisions after rural delinquency reached 25.44% in September. The problem is systemic: 31,000 rural producers are in default at Banco do Brasil alone, according to its CEO. BCB President Gabriel Galípolo, who has defended the high-interest-rate policy as necessary to reconverge inflation to target, acknowledged last week a "pedagogical challenge" in explaining his stance, in a veiled critique of analysts who question the conduct of monetary policy on social media.

Brazil's financial system still bears the scars of a year marked by the collapse of Banco Master. One year after the extrajudicial liquidation decreed by the BCB, the case has left a trail of roughly R$76 billion in losses, of which R$58 billion were absorbed by the FGC deposit insurance fund — financed by the system's own banks. The wave of liquidations triggered by the episode led the BCB to shut down 21 institutions in 2026, the largest number since 2002, with 11 of them directly linked to Daniel Vorcaro's group. The costs of this episode, passed on to the banking system, constitute yet another source of pressure on the cost of credit in a country where real interest rates already run around 8%, a level that economists from the IMF and the University of Cambridge classify as a genuine global outlier — unexplainable by conventional macroeconomic fundamentals alone.

As markets digest the fiscal package, two sector stories merit attention. On the regulatory front, Cade is preparing to open administrative proceedings against Apple for blocking third-party access to the iPhone's NFC technology — an initiative directly linked to the expansion of contactless Pix, whose regulation turns six years old and is expected to move US$203.4 billion in Brazilian e-commerce in 2027, according to projections by Ebanx. In the power sector, the country's first battery auction, scheduled for December, is at risk: an unresolved dispute over who will pay for the contracts, which could total R$4.5 billion per year for 5 GW of contracted capacity, threatens to delay the tender. At the same time, Enel São Paulo is appealing to Aneel to avoid the forfeiture of its concession, requesting a new operational performance assessment before any recommendation is made to the federal government.

In the coming days, attention will focus on the completion of the voting on amendments to PLP 299 in the Senate, scheduled for Tuesday, followed by the text's dispatch to the Chamber. The return of President Lula from Asia, with the agenda of defining the fourth-term ministerial team and naming a new nominee to the Supreme Court after the rejection of Jorge Messias by senators, adds political uncertainty at a moment when markets need clear fiscal signals. International investors will also be watching the speech by Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh, whose tone will influence global appetite for emerging-market risk assets — and, by extension, the pressure on the real.

### Banco ABC Brasil (B3: ABCB4)
