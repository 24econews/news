# Brazil: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-25

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-25*

Brazil's financial markets woke up on Wednesday (25) with a question that still lacks a definitive answer: is the Moretti plan the end of the fiscal story, or merely the prologue to a much larger adjustment? The tension between the immediate relief that the package's approval brought to assets and the growing pressure for deeper structural measures defines the country's economic moment with unusual clarity.

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva signed into law on Tuesday (24), with no vetoes and one day after approval in the Chamber of Deputies by 353 votes to 128, the so-called Moretti plan — the fiscal adjustment package projected to deliver an estimated impact of between R$46 billion and R$56 billion in 2027, combining higher revenues and spending restraint. The dollar fell 0.32% in Tuesday's session, closing at R$5.908, and future interest rates eased, signaling that the market took the political cue positively. On Wednesday, the central bank conducted two credit-line auctions of up to US$2 billion, in an operation announced the day before, reinforcing its willingness to intervene in the currency volatility that had pushed the dollar to touch R$6 in previous weeks.

But the market is not satisfied. Analysts at SulAmérica assessed that the approved fiscal adjustment is insufficient to produce a meaningful reduction in interest rates, and economists polled by various outlets warn of the risk of stagflation — elevated inflation combined with a stalled economy — should the government fail to move beyond Moretti. Moody's, according to Valor Econômico, continues to press on Brazil's sovereign rating given the lack of structural progress. Senate President Davi Alcolumbre himself opened the possibility of voting on a second adjustment proposal still this year, provided there is backing from party leaders, while the Planalto is drafting a constitutional amendment to contain the growth of mandatory spending — including, in a more ambitious version, the minimum wage itself. The text is not yet ready, but Lula signaled willingness to debate it, with the expectation that it will be sent to Congress only after the choice of a new Finance Minister, whose name is expected to be disclosed by December 2.

The credibility of this fiscal push is tested by numbers that contradict the discipline narrative: the government cut its budget freeze from R$16.1 billion to R$12.6 billion by excluding from the fiscal target R$10.5 billion in diesel subsidy expenses — a maneuver that the technical staff of the Federal Audit Court (TCU) has questioned, flagging concerns about revenue estimates presented by the economic team in the latest adjustment report. The Receita Federal, in turn, maintains the forecast of collecting R$10.5 billion from dividend taxation, despite admitting shortfalls in collection so far. In October, federal tax revenue hit R$293.97 billion, a historical record for the month and a 7.3% increase over the same period last year — a result driven, according to Valor, by oil and income tax on capital. The robust revenue performance coexists, however, with delinquency that hit a record in the third quarter of 2026: R$371.1 billion in debts more than 90 days overdue, up 26.7% from the same period in 2025, according to a FecomercioSP survey. The FGV Consumer Confidence Index fell 1.2 points in November, to 83.5 points, the lowest level since August 2022.

The Ashmore report that circulated in recent days — sketching a scenario of the dollar at R$8.20 and 10-year yields at 19.5% within 18 months in the absence of fiscal adjustment — captures the mood among international managers. Gustavo Medeiros, head of macro research at the British asset manager, which oversees US$55 billion in assets, told Brazil Journal that Ashmore remains cautious on Brazil at the current exchange rate level and did not add to its country exposure during the recent selloff. The firm, on the other hand, does not rule out a swift reversal of pessimism should the government signal credible reforms — echoing the view of major global banks, with JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs identifying the real as among the emerging market currencies with the greatest upside potential in 2027, though conditioned on greater domestic stability.

On the corporate front, Petrobras, whose ADRs trade on the NYSE, unveiled a business plan that raises investment by 12%, to US$122 billion over the next five years, with US$7 billion earmarked for expanding fuel transportation infrastructure. The market received the plan cautiously: analysts see less room for dividends over the next two years and view the plan as overly dependent on oil prices. The state-controlled company's CEO, Magda Chambriard, ruled out the purchase of a fuel distributor "at this moment," pushing back against calls from President Lula. In parallel, Petrobras announced the acquisition of 42% of Neomille, a corn ethanol producer controlled by CerradinhoBio, for R$2.8 billion, signaling expansion in renewables — and sources heard by Reuters indicate the state-run firm will pursue further acquisitions in corn ethanol plants to supply its sustainable aviation fuel production.

The tax reform, which takes effect in January 2027 with the CBS replacing PIS and Cofins, adds a layer of operational uncertainty for exporters: it is still unclear whether credits from the new contribution can be used to offset debits of other federal taxes, which could pressure cash flow in the sector. Studies delivered to parliamentary caucuses suggest that the CBS rate could come in close to 10%, below the 11.3% set by the TCU — a debate the Senate will have to resolve in the coming months. On the labor front, the CNI filed a motion with the Supreme Court (STF) against the government's regulation ending the 6x1 work schedule, which takes effect on December 16, opening a legal battle whose outcome could redefine costs across the industry.

What to watch in the coming sessions: the name of the new Finance Minister, whose selection Lula promised to announce by December 2, will be the main short-term catalyst for the currency and future interest rates. The progress of the constitutional amendment to contain mandatory spending growth and its political viability before the December 15 recess will determine whether the current asset relief has legs or whether the market will once again test the R$6-per-dollar level.

## Corporate Watch

### Braskem (NYSE: BAK / B3: BRKM5)
Braskem shares tumbled 14.08%, to R$2.50, on Tuesday (24), following the announcement of a preliminary restructuring agreement on roughly US$11 billion in debt, under which creditors may convert US$3.6 billion into equity, accept a 30% haircut or receive bonds maturing in 2060 — narrowly avoiding a judicial recovery filing for which documentation had been prepared for the same day.

### Petrobras (NYSE: PBR / B3: PETR4)
The state-controlled company raised its 2027–2031 investment plan by 12%, to US$122 billion, and announced extraordinary dividends, but the market reacted cautiously given flat projected output and reliance on oil prices; in parallel, the acquisition of 42% of Neomille for R$2.8 billion marks the company's return to the ethanol sector after divestments made under the Temer and Bolsonaro administrations.

### Kaszek Ventures
The venture capital firm founded by the creators of Mercado Livre closed its seventh early-stage fund at US$650 million — the largest in Latin American history in that category — beating its initial target of US$600 million thanks to demand from endowments, foundations and family offices in the United States, Europe and Asia, in a sign that foreign capital continues to bet on the Latin American tech ecosystem despite Brazil's macroeconomic turbulence.
