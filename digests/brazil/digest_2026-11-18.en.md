# Brazil: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-18

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-18*

The Brazilian real closed yesterday as the world's worst-performing currency since Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's re-election, having shed nearly 16% of its value against the dollar since the runoff vote — and Wednesday's news flow helps explain why the pressure is unlikely to ease in the near term. The exchange rate is the barometer of a confidence crisis that lacks a single epicenter but has several: a fiscal adjustment stumbling through Congress, a structural cost of doing business estimated at R$3.5 trillion a year, new labor legislation whose effects companies are still trying to gauge, and an external agenda that combines US tariffs, European trade barriers, and restrictions on AI chips.

The dollar closed Tuesday at R$5.781, a new yearly high, after touching R$5.811 during the session before retreating slightly. The Ibovespa rose just 0.31%, ending at 169,817 points — a move that reflected less optimism than technical relief tied to the expectation, ultimately dashed, that the Senate would wrap up voting yesterday on the floor amendments to the so-called "Moretti plan." According to Valor Econômico, future interest rates fell sharply through the afternoon, driven by rumors about potential names for the next administration's economic team — a sign that the market is pricing in not only the current fiscal picture but already placing bets on the eventual successor to Finance Minister Dario Durigan.

Durigan himself compounded the uncertainty by publicly distancing himself from the adjustment plan negotiations, even as Vice President Geraldo Alckmin came to his defense. Planning Minister Bruno Moretti tried to broker an agreement that would preserve the text from opposition amendments, without success. Senate President Davi Alcolumbre postponed the vote to Wednesday after the opposition rejected a deal that involved removing the article deferring the transfer of R$12 billion from the federal government to states and municipalities under the Kandir Law. Lower House Speaker Hugo Motta avoided setting a date to receive the text — a signal that the legislative calendar for the adjustment remains open-ended.

The stakes are considerable. The Moretti plan has a potential impact of between R$46 billion and R$56 billion on the 2027 budget, at a moment when the private sector has already revised upward its deficit estimates for this year and next, as shown in the Prisma Fiscal survey released by the Finance Ministry. The National Treasury projects that gross debt will only stop growing in 2030, reaching 86.3% of GDP by 2036. Milton Maluhy, CEO of Itaú Unibanco, was even more direct: without fiscal measures, debt could exceed 100% of GDP within four years. Diogo Guillen, the bank's chief economist, projects growth of just 0.5% for 2027, inflation at 6.1%, and the Selic stuck at 13.75% — a combination that would amount, in Itaú's own words, to mild stagflation.

Analyst Sérgio Vale, of MB Associados, warned Valor that "going from R$5 to R$6 is a relevant shock to inflation" and that any reversal of the currency to prior levels would only occur in the face of "a strong and clear signal on the fiscal question." The Central Bank responded by announcing two line auctions totaling up to US$1 billion for Wednesday — a targeted intervention that signals concern about the pace of depreciation, but which markets know cannot alter the structural trend.

Against this same fragile economic backdrop, the Movimento Brasil Competitivo released a study estimating the Brazil Cost at R$3.5 trillion per year, equivalent to 27.5% of 2025 GDP — a figure that encompasses regulatory, tax, logistical, and infrastructure inefficiencies. The number arrives at a particularly opportune moment: the tax reform takes effect in January, but is already generating controversy before implementation. The CBS rate has been raised by court decisions that broadened the scope of tax exemptions, as Durigan himself acknowledged. Fraport Brasil, concessionaire of the Fortaleza, Porto Alegre, and Jericoacoara airports, announced it intends to seek financial rebalancing from Anac due to the reform's impacts. Brazil Journal, meanwhile, argues that the Moretti plan, far from being a spending-cut package, is fundamentally a tax hike — with the linear cut to tax benefits jumping from 10% to 20%, raising the effective burden on companies under the presumed-profit regime for the second consecutive year.

The business environment doesn't benefit from the external agenda either. Washington maintains the 25% tariff on Brazilian exports, and the Brazilian Congress approved two rounds of compensatory credit yesterday — R$13.5 billion by the Senate and R$6.5 billion by the Lower House — for affected sectors, from steel to footwear, from sugar to fertilizers. At the same time, the Lula government is assessing the impact of US restrictions on AI chip imports, which include Brazil among the restricted countries, with the analysis being handled by the Industry and Management and Innovation ministries. On the European front, the ban on Brazilian poultry and honey was upheld after a postponed vote in Brussels, and the anti-deforestation rule taking effect in January is expected to raise the cost of Brazilian soy exported to the bloc by around 12%.

In the corporate sector, General Motors announced a voluntary layoff plan for its São José dos Campos plant, offering seven nominal salaries, 24 months of health coverage, and an Onix — a sign of capacity adjustment amid competitive pressure from Chinese brands which, according to sector analysis, intend to enter the Brazilian truck market via diesel models before moving on to electrification. The battle for the delivery market gained a new chapter with Keeta expanding its network following a TJ-SP ruling that struck down 99Food's exclusivity clauses. Aneel formed a majority of four votes in favor of terminating Enel São Paulo's contract, although a request for review by the director-general delayed the final decision — a process that, if completed, would be forwarded to the Ministry of Mines and Energy. In the power market, auctions held last week revealed discounts of just 0.4% to 2.4% versus ceiling prices, compared with 26.5% in the equivalent auction a year earlier — a compression that signals scarcity of available supply for 2027 and structural pressure on tariffs.

What to watch over the coming hours and days: the conclusion of the Senate vote on Moretti plan amendments on Wednesday is the single most critical event for the currency and the yield curve. The signing of the presidential decree regulating the end of the 6x1 work schedule is scheduled for Thursday and will determine the degree of impact on payrolls at labor-intensive companies. Aneel's decision on Enel SP and the forwarding of the process to the Ministry of Mines and Energy will shape the future of a distribution asset serving 24 million consumers. And on the geopolitical front, Brazil's challenge to the European antitrust review of the sale of Anglo American's nickel unit to Hong Kong-listed MMG will test the limits of national regulatory sovereignty in a transaction with direct implications for global ferronickel supply — a market that, according to consulting firm MarketsandMarkets, is expected to grow from US$57.6 billion to US$80.7 billion by 2031.
