# Brazil: The Economic Pulse — 2026-09-01

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-09-01*

Brazil's second-quarter 2026 GDP arrived with a seemingly reassuring headline — growth of 0.5%, marginally above the median forecast — but the internal details of the report reveal an economy decelerating more sharply than consensus expected, with direct consequences for the fiscal, monetary and electoral debate that now dominates Brasília and the markets.

Agribusiness momentum saved the aggregate print. Soybeans, coffee and livestock — particularly poultry and cattle — pulled the agricultural sector to 2.8% quarter-on-quarter growth and 6.8% year-on-year, according to IBGE. But that impulse masks a worrying portrait on the demand side: household consumption contracted 0.4%, services advanced just 0.2% and industry was essentially flat. What sustained growth on the expenditure side was public investment and government consumption — not the private sector. IBGE's National Accounts coordinator, Ricardo Montes de Moraes, acknowledged that the macro environment of high interest rates, elevated household debt and delinquency may "in theory" have weighed on consumption. For economists such as Rodolfo Margato at XP, the disaggregated data were "weak," evidencing "an economy losing traction despite a still-heated labor market." Brazil, which grew 1.1% in Q1 and ranked tenth in the global GDP ranking among 50 countries, tumbled to 27th place in the second quarter, according to Valor Econômico.

Financial markets processed the data in peculiar fashion. The Ibovespa closed Monday up nearly 1%, at 177,418 points, and traded back above 180,000 in Tuesday's session, cutting against Wall Street, where S&P 500 futures were lower and ten-year Treasury yields climbed to 4.79% — the highest level since January 2025. The reason for the decoupling is political: election polls showing a narrowing lead for President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva over Senator Flávio Bolsonaro have eased the risk premia embedded in Brazilian assets, on the logic that a more contested electoral scenario raises the probability of a fiscal pivot after October. At the same time, weak household consumption has opened room for bets on a longer Selic-cutting cycle. Rates on DI contracts maturing in January 2028 fell to 13.775% in the morning session, and the real stood out as one of the best-performing global currencies, with the spot dollar sliding to R$5.1630 — despite the greenback's broader global rally, driven by Middle East tensions, where fresh U.S. strikes on Iran and the threat to traffic through the Strait of Hormuz pushed Brent above US$92 per barrel.

That oil rally, combined with geopolitics, creates a first-order domestic paradox. Shares of Petrobras, Prio and PetroReconcavo rallied together on the previous day, propping up the Ibovespa. But the federal government, which saw an injunction suspend the crude export tax just a few days ago, quickly reversed the ruling: federal appellate judge Roberto Carvalho Veloso struck down the injunction, reinstating the 12% rate on crude oil exports. The decision cites the risk of "harm to the economic order" and to state planning on exchange rate policy, in addition to the multiplier effect that keeping the injunction in place could have across the country. The tax, introduced earlier this year as part of a package to offset the diesel subsidy announced in March, had already been extended by Camex last week.

The external landscape offers Brazil little relief. The European Union confirmed the suspension, starting Thursday, of imports of Brazilian beef and poultry, citing non-compliance with sanitary standards. At the same time, the Chinese quota for imports without the 55% surcharge is nearly exhausted, more than 90% consumed before year-end, as Fernando Queiroz, CEO of Minerva Foods, disclosed. The window that opens is American: Trump temporarily suspended tariffs on the 300,000-tonne lean beef quota — exclusively benefiting Brazil and Paraguay, in a move that, according to the Wall Street Journal, was directly influenced by Joesley Batista. With Europeans closing one door and the Chinese about to do the same, the U.S. market once again becomes strategic for Brazilian meatpackers — although the broader context of trade negotiations with Washington remains unstable. Lula government officials told the Americans that the tariff hike is "unfair and discriminatory," but Industry Minister Márcio Elias Rosa acknowledged that Monday's meeting produced no concrete progress, only a "new scenario" for negotiation after Trump signaled willingness to seek a deal.

Inside the 2027 Budget, presented on Monday, the fiscal picture reveals tensions the market is already testing in prices. The proposal projects a primary surplus of R$18.6 billion, equivalent to just 0.13% of GDP — a promise Finance Minister Dario Durigan reinforced by arguing that Brazil will post growing and recurring surpluses starting next year. But the surrounding numbers contradict the rhetorical optimism: R$97.9 billion in subsidized credit, R$44.8 billion in parliamentary earmarks, R$6 billion for the postal service Correios, R$651 million for Eletronuclear and a R$30 billion line approved by the lower house to finance vehicle purchases by ride-hailing drivers. Gross debt reached 82.5% of GDP in July, the highest level since April 2021, according to Banco Central data. Economists quoted by Folha de S.Paulo are blunt: the next president will need to correct course starting in 2027. Lula, for his part, promised business leaders on Monday night that he will do "everything within his reach" to bring rates down, but reaffirmed he will not compromise on social commitments — an equation the market continues to disbelieve can be closed without structural adjustment.

In the corporate arena, Chinese penetration into the Brazilian economy is taking on new contours. Cofco International, the trading arm of the Beijing government, signed a deal to acquire two Bunge sugar mills in Brazil, expanding Chinese state capital's presence in the sugar-ethanol sector. Battery manufacturers such as CATL and Jinko ESS are lining up local partnerships to participate in the unprecedented energy storage systems auction scheduled for December. BYD overtook General Motors in cumulative sales through August, forcing the U.S. carmaker to resort to last-minute bonuses in an attempt to reclaim third place in the domestic auto market. And iFood filed a formal complaint at Cade against Keeta, accusing the Chinese rival of pricing artificially low to strangle competition — a front that could define the future of food delivery in the country.

The coming weeks will be decisive on multiple fronts simultaneously. Congress votes today on the provisional measure that eliminates the "taxa das blusinhas" — with direct impact on domestic retail. Companies under the Simples Nacional regime have 30 days to choose their tax framework in light of the reform. BNDES expanded from R$5 billion to R$9 billion its own resources for Brasil Soberano 3, a program supporting companies affected by the U.S. tariff hike, with applications open for up to 15 days. The U.S. payroll report on Friday and September's Copom meeting will set the pace of the Selic. And with the election campaign already stamping volatility onto assets, the market will monitor not just economic data but the degree of fiscal credibility each candidate projects — a filter that, for now, appears to favor the hypothesis of a course change over the continuation of the current model of fiscal expansion combined with structurally high interest rates.
