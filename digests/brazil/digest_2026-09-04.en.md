On the eve of the September 7 holiday, Congress handed the Lula administration a political victory with ambiguous economic contours: approval of the provisional measure that scraps the so-called "blusinhas tax," the 20% import duty on international purchases of up to US$50 made on platforms such as Shopee and Shein. The decision, taken less than a month before the October elections, is being cheered by consumers and by the Asian e-commerce giants, but has triggered a furious backlash from domestic industry and retail, which brand it as electioneering and an economic backslide capable of destroying jobs. The Finance Ministry will have three months following presidential sanction to assess sectoral impacts — a timeline that, on the electoral calendar, coincides with the post-election period, effectively neutralizing any immediate practical relief for the sectors affected.

The contradiction is glaring: even as the government tears down barriers to low-value e-commerce imports, on the external front it is grappling with the first day of the European suspension on Brazilian meats — beef, poultry, and eggs — a restriction that threatens one of the most profitable pillars of the country's trade balance. Brasília responded by dialing up its reciprocity rhetoric, signaling it could invoke the Reciprocity Law should negotiations with Brussels fail to advance. Analysts warn, however, that the most vulnerable segment is not beef, whose producers can pivot to markets such as China and the United States, but poultry, where international competition is set to intensify and Brazilian exporters have less room to maneuver. The EU crisis lands at a delicate moment for agribusiness, which has yet to fully absorb the global tariff uncertainties generated by Donald Trump's second term and by the U.S.-Israel conflict with Iran — an event that, according to Valor Econômico's analysis, has again pressured inflation in developed economies and interrupted the global rate-cutting cycle.

Finance Minister Dario Durigan admitted in an interview with SBT News that the pace of debt growth is worrying but insisted the process is reversible, and ruled out any discussion of a new pension reform before the elections. The political message is clear, but markets received it with skepticism: the dollar closed at R$5.103, virtually flat, while the Ibovespa ended at 185,188 points, snapping an eleven-session winning streak after touching an intraday high of 188,891 points — a level not seen since early May.

The sticking point in the talks is governance: neither Rubens Ometto nor André Esteves, according to sources, is willing to concede political power disproportionate to the stake acquired.
