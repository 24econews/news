# Brazil: The Economic Pulse — 2026-10-30

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-10-30*

The Ibovespa closed down for a fourth consecutive session on Thursday, accumulating losses of 16.6% since Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's re-election — but the aggregate numbers dramatically understate the sectoral devastation. Casas Bahia plunged 20% in the session, Cosan lost 12%, Cyrela retreated 11%, and Cogna fell 10%. Since the re-election, Vamos has lost 60% of its market value, Magazine Luiza 59%, and Cosan 55%. The dollar closed at R$5.387, up 0.76% on the day, while BTG analysts raised their 2027 IPCA forecast from 4.8% to 5.9%, raising the possibility that the Central Bank could prematurely halt any eventual monetary easing cycle. The market is not merely reacting to post-election political uncertainty — it is structurally repricing Brazil risk.

The epicenter of this reassessment is fiscal. The Independent Fiscal Institution (IFI), the technical body linked to the Senate, projected this week that the commitments assumed by Lula during the campaign could push the primary deficit to 1% of GDP in 2027, versus the official surplus target of 0.13%. The IFI estimates that stabilizing the debt-to-GDP ratio would require an adjustment of roughly 3 percentage points of output — a figure that stands in direct conflict with the president's reluctance to propose more robust consolidation measures, out of concern he would be accused of breaking campaign promises. Within the government, the impasse is real: the economic team is debating the size of the adjustment while the chief executive hesitates. The opposition is trying to get ahead of the issue, with the Entrepreneurship Parliamentary Front drafting its own constitutional amendment on public accounts controls, in an attempt to force the debate in Congress before the government defines its agenda.

The data released by the Central Bank and the National Treasury on Thursday illustrate why the market is so nervous. General government gross debt remained at 82.9% of GDP in September, the highest level since 2021, equivalent to R$11.1 trillion. Federal public debt rose 0.97% on the month to R$9.4 trillion, pressured by interest expenses. Federal state-owned enterprises recorded a primary deficit of R$2 billion in September — the worst result for the month since the historical series began in 2001. The central government's primary surplus of R$4.8 billion in the same month, while better than the R$14.4 billion deficit recorded in September 2025, does not alter the worrying trajectory: on a trailing-12-month basis, the consolidated public sector posted a shortfall of R$70.1 billion, or 0.52% of GDP. The IFI also warned about the growing opacity of the accounts, since programs such as Minha Casa Minha Vida financed via the Social Fund, loans to ride-share drivers, and credit to exporters affected by the US tariff hike do not enter the fiscal target calculation, distorting the read on the actual adjustment.

Onto this already deteriorated fiscal landscape fell, with the force of a bomb, the 11.3% CBS rate — the Contribution on Goods and Services — approved by the TCU on Wednesday. The government's own estimate, kept under wraps during the election period, surpassed all market and tax-consultancy projections, which had been working with a range around 9.21%. Combined with the estimated IBS rate of between 19% and 20%, Brazil's total VAT could exceed 30%, potentially the highest in the world — above the 25% charged by Finland, Denmark, and Sweden. Abrasca warned of the risk of an increased effective burden on companies, and the possibility of changes to IOF taxation worsens the picture. With less than 60 days until the start of 2027, thousands of companies need to redo their tax planning. The retail sector is the most immediately hit, and the deterioration in commerce confidence — the Icec index fell 2.7% in October, to its lowest level since May 2021 — suggests the impact is already being felt even before implementation. The rate may still be revised by the Senate by December, but specialists are skeptical about a significant reduction, and tax lawyer Carolina Romanini, of Schneider Pugliese, notes that the period during which the higher rate is in effect will not generate refund rights.

Soaring future rates are already contaminating the mortgage market. In the last week of October, Bradesco, Itaú, and Santander announced hikes in housing finance rates, reversing the small reductions seen between late 2025 and early this year. Construction lost momentum in the third quarter, according to Cbic, even though infrastructure works sustained sector employment. Household debt surpassed 50% of 12-month accumulated income for the first time, while individual default rates on free-resources credit hit 8.1% in September — an all-time high and the fourth consecutive monthly increase. Total credit balance in the financial system grew just 0.4% in September, and 12-month accumulated expansion of 8.6% is the slowest since February 2024. The Central Bank decreed the liquidation of MW, a payment institution based in Joinville, for serious violations of payment-system rules — a signal that regulatory supervision remains active in an environment of growing stress.

On the corporate front, there are contradictory signals. Raízen closed the largest tax settlement in Brazilian history, extinguishing R$22.4 billion in debts with an 85% discount, enabling the advance of its extrajudicial recovery plan. BYD, which inaugurated its Camaçari plant 16 months ago, confirms 17 suppliers installed at the complex and accumulated investments of R$11 billion, with plans to export to Mercosur — a narrative of productive integration that contrasts with domestic macroeconomic turbulence. Petrobras, whose ADRs trade on the NYSE, announced its third oil discovery at the Morpho well, in the Foz do Amazonas basin, and set records for diesel production and refined-product exports in the third quarter, in an effort to avoid imports made costlier by the war in Iran. BP made another hydrocarbon discovery in the Santos Basin. Exports benefiting from the Mercosur-European Union agreement grew 21% between May and September, according to the Mdic — a positive data point that, however, risks being overshadowed by domestic uncertainty. Foreign investment in telecommunications and IT totaled US$5.6 billion in the first nine months of the year, a record for the period, while live-cattle prices approach R$400 per arroba in São Paulo, pressuring consumer inflation.

Next week's agenda will focus attention on three fronts: Aneel's definition of the energy tariff flag, the release of the September Pnad Contínua, and any signal from the government on the size and composition of the fiscal adjustment it intends to present to the market. The Senate has until December 15 to define the final CBS rate. And until the government communicates clearly what it is willing to cut or reform, buyside analysts are unanimous: financial conditions, asset prices, and future rates are likely to continue deteriorating.

## Corporate Watch

### Raízen (B3: RAIZ4)
Raízen closed a tax settlement with the federal government to extinguish R$22.4 billion in debts — the largest transaction of its kind since the mechanism was regulated in Brazil — with a final discount of 85% and a cash payment of R$2.5 billion; the agreement was a condition precedent for creditor approval of the company's extrajudicial recovery plan, which calls for converting 45% of debts into equity.

Vale reported net income of R$6.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 56% from the same period a year earlier, a result reflecting pressure on iron ore prices and one that will be closely watched by international investors given the company's weight in emerging-market indices.

Itaú announced the acquisition of Brazilian AI startup NeoSpace for approximately R$3 billion, aiming to develop proprietary foundational models and position Latin America's largest bank as a financial AI platform — CIO Ricardo Guerra described the strategy to Brazil Journal as building a "financial ChatGPT."
