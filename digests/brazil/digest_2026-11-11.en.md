# Brazil: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-11

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-11*

The dollar touched R$5.61 at Tuesday's peak before easing back to close at R$5.586 — the highest print since July 2025 — and that move neatly captures the dilemma Brazil faces as the year turns: inflation under pressure from multiple simultaneous shocks, a central bank unwilling to hike rates, and a newly re-elected government still without a credible fiscal plan to anchor expectations. The combination of the Copom minutes, released in the morning, and hotter-than-expected U.S. consumer inflation data was the immediate market trigger. But the FX move is symptomatic of something deeper.

The Copom minutes brought no surprise in their conclusion — the Selic remains unchanged — but they did surprise in their diagnosis. The committee enumerated not one, not two, but four supply shocks pressuring inflation simultaneously: the war in Iran, which sent oil prices soaring; El Niño, which is affecting vegetable production and whose effects are expected to linger through the first quarter of 2027, according to Valor Econômico; the end of the 6x1 work schedule, whose implementation Justice Flávio Dino of the Supreme Court is weighing postponing by Friday; and the depreciation of the real. Bradesco revised its 2027 inflation projection from 4.1% to 5.3%, acknowledging the difficulty of calibrating the impact of the new labor rules and the tax reform on prices. Valor Econômico noted that, despite the worsening inflation picture, the minutes rule out a Selic hike, since the central bank recognizes that economic activity is slowing more sharply than expected — the labor market, previously "overheated," now shows "signs of cooling at the margin."

It is in this context that fiscal adjustment takes on maximum urgency. According to Valor Econômico and Folha de S.Paulo, the government's congressional base is working on a fiscal effort of R$70 billion to R$80 billion for 2027, with the administration aiming for the center of the primary result target of 0.5% of GDP. Measures on the table include authorization to cancel up to an additional R$12.8 billion in the 2027 Budget, plus a 20% expansion in the base of expenditures subject to contingency freezes — which would include mandatory spending historically considered untouchable. Senate President Davi Alcolumbre signaled that the package should be presented next week. For UBS, however, the problem is one of magnitude: with public debt at 80% of GDP, a nominal deficit close to 10% of GDP, and the Selic at 14%, any insufficient adjustment "sucks all the oxygen out of the room," in the words of the Swiss bank's CIO for emerging markets in the Americas. Gabriel Galípolo, president of the central bank, struck a similar note when he stated that the fact that Brazil issues nearly all its debt in reais does not eliminate the risks for government financiers nor guarantee lower rates — a clear message that the local currency is no shield against fiscal mistrust.

On the corporate front, Petrobras, whose ADRs trade on the NYSE, stole the show by reporting net income of R$39.75 billion in the third quarter, up 21.5% year-on-year, with adjusted EBITDA of R$85.8 billion — growth of 34.3%. The combination of international prices elevated by the Middle East conflict and record operational performance led the state-controlled company to revise its 2026 production guidance upward, from 3.1 million barrels of oil equivalent per day to a range of 3.3 to 3.4 million. Capex for the year was also revised, from US$19.4 billion to a range of US$24 to US$25.5 billion — a move the company attributed largely to FX effects. The strong result, however, comes alongside an institutional clash of growing gravity: an ANP director, Pietro Mendes, delivered a lengthy public statement at the agency's meeting denouncing that Petrobras has been systematically trying to interfere in the gas sector's regulatory agenda, pressing for delays both in the tariff review of gas pipelines and in the gas release mechanism. The ANP suspended the tariff review process after intervention by the Ministry of Mines and Energy — complying with requests from the state-controlled company and Nova Transportadora do Sudeste. The episode highlights the limits of governance in the Brazilian energy sector.

Embraer, listed on the NYSE and B3, was the day's star performer, with shares climbing 9.3% after reporting net revenue of US$2.3 billion in the third quarter, growth of 17% in dollar terms. The company is intensifying commercial negotiations in India and the United States, which reinforces its geopolitical relevance at a moment when defense and regional aviation supply chains are the subject of strategic contention. JBS, listed on B3 with a global footprint, saw its profit plunge 72% in the third quarter, to US$164 million, pressured across multiple divisions, even as the U.S. beef segment showed signs of recovery. In parallel, the company announced the acquisition of Flora for US$542 million, signaling a clear diversification into higher value-added consumer goods — hygiene and personal care — in search of more stable margins.

In retail, the picture is one of contraction. The Cielo Broad Retail Index fell 4% in October year-on-year — the worst October since 2023. Credit card delinquency reached 10% in the third quarter, 0.9 percentage point above the same period in 2025, according to Abecs. It is in this environment that Magazine Luiza, despite reporting a 34.6% drop in adjusted quarterly profit, is telling a structural recovery story: with Casas Bahia in judicial reorganization and closing 500 stores, Magalu posted an acceleration in brick-and-mortar growth from 8% year-on-year to 16% in October, and reversed a 12% decline in 1P e-commerce into 14% growth in the same month.

Tax reform remains at the center of political and corporate disputes. Seventy-four industry groups signed a letter requesting an extension of testing before the new rules take effect in January 2027, but both Minister Dario Durigan and allied lawmakers ruled out any postponement. The reform's co-author, Professor Eurico Marcos Diniz de Santi of FGV, rejected the argument that the system is not ready. CSN is selling its cement division to China's Huaxin for approximately R$9 billion in cash — a deal approved at roughly 7x EBITDA — while Bradesco BBI reports that Lula's re-election has reignited Chinese appetite for M&A in Brazil. The partnership between PPSA and Argentine state company Gas y Petróleo del Neuquén to evaluate the supply of gas from Vaca Muerta opens a new geopolitical dimension in the regional energy matrix.

What to watch over the coming hours and days: the government's fiscal package, which the Senate expects to see formalized next week, will determine whether the R$70 to R$80 billion signal gains substance or evaporates in political negotiations. Banco do Brasil's results on Wednesday, with particular attention to agribusiness portfolio quality and cost of risk, will provide an additional read on the health of corporate credit. And the restructuring negotiations at Braskem, whose creditors were seeking to reach an agreement by Wednesday, will test market appetite for high-risk corporate credit in an environment of elevated rates and currency pressure.
