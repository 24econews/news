The Ibovespa opened Monday up 1.5%, reclaiming the 178,000-point level, while the dollar slipped 0.54% to R$5.168 — and the market's tone crystallized the tension defining this moment in Brazil: a country whose day-to-day fundamentals are reasonably in order, but whose fiscal trajectory, in the words of BTG Pactual chairman André Esteves, is "out of place" and prevents interest rates from following a civilized path. Speaking at the opening of BTG Macro Day, Esteves argued that the Selic, currently at 14%, could sit at 7% if public debt were under control — and framed the electoral moment as a "golden opportunity" for Brazil to leave an institutional and fiscal legacy. The irony of the day is that, as the banker spoke, the Central Bank was releasing data that made his argument all the more urgent: gross debt reached 82.5% of GDP in July, the highest level since April 2021, up from 81.9% in June. In nominal terms, the stock hit R$10.947 trillion.

The public sector's nominal deficit came in at R$97.6 billion for July alone — a figure that, though better than the R$175.6 billion of July 2025, still carries an interest bill of R$98.9 billion for the month. Over the twelve months through July, the nominal deficit totals R$1.24 trillion, or 9.34% of GDP. Compounding the structural picture, federal state-owned enterprises posted a combined deficit of R$8.28 billion between January and July, the worst result for the period since the historical series began in 2002 — a jump of nearly 50% from the same span in 2025. The government had already announced a R$6 billion capital injection into Correios in the 2027 Budget, and now confirms that Eletronuclear will receive an additional R$651.8 million. These numbers directly contradict Planning Minister Bruno Moretti's signal that the government expects a primary surplus of R$18 billion to R$20 billion in 2027.

## Corporate Watch

### **Casas Bahia (B3: BHIA3)**
The 2nd Bankruptcy Court of São Paulo granted the effects of the "stay period" in the retailer's judicial reorganization proceeding, suspending for 180 days — counted from August 19 — all creditor actions and enforcement measures, after the company demonstrated judicial blocks of roughly R$9 million within days of filing for reorganization. Shares surged more than 40% at the open, though the judge has not yet formally granted the reorganization request, conditioning the final decision on an accounting audit.

### **Natura &Co (B3: NATU3)**
The company announced the departure of CEO João Paulo Ferreira, to be replaced by Alessandro Carlucci — who previously ran the company from 2005 to 2014 and more recently served as chairman of the board — effective October 1; Fábio Barbosa returns as chairman. Shares rose about 7.7% in the morning, with the market welcoming the return of an executive with a proven track record at the company at a moment of restructuring accelerated by Advent's entry into the capital base.

### **Cyrela (B3: CYRE3)**
Asset manager TRX walked away from its planned acquisition of a real estate portfolio from Cyrela and its subsidiaries — including office floors in the Edifício Oscar Freire Corporate and stakes in four logistics warehouses — valued at R$2.14 billion plus a potential additional R$103 million, according to a non-binding memorandum of understanding signed on August 5; the deal also envisioned the structuring of two real estate investment funds with TRXF11 as anchor investor.
