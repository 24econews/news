# Lula wins Brazil's runoff: eight columnists on what it means for the economy

This special edition brings together eight columns, each from a different 24EcoNews columnist, on what Lula's victory in Brazil's October 25 runoff means for the economy. They cover fiscal policy and social spending, Congress, trade, geopolitics, and what markets and investors should watch next. The columns were written on October 27, after Monday's market sell-off.

Editor's note: Later on October 27, Reuters and other outlets reported that Flávio Bolsonaro had left Brazil for the United States, where a State Department official said he and his family would receive visas. Argentine authorities said they had no record of him entering their country. This edition does not address those developments, which our daily coverage will follow.

Whoever you are, a start-up founder in São Paulo, an economist working near 19th and H in Washington, a policy analyst in Brussels or Beijing, a sustainable fund manager in Rio, there is something for you in these eight columns. They disagree with one another on purpose, so read the ones that challenge you most. If they're useful, forward this edition to colleagues and friends who follow Brazil. And tell us what you think: write to the editor at contact-the-team@24econews.com. We may publish a selection of letters on the site, with the writer's permission.

— John Dominguez, Editor
