# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-10-16

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-10-16*

The Banco Central de la República Argentina purchased USD 182 million on Thursday, pushing its gross reserves above USD 50 billion for the first time in weeks, while in Congress the opposition failed to muster the quorum needed to repeal DNU 70. That these two stories coincided on the same day was no narrative accident: they captured with precision the paradoxical condition in which Argentina finds itself in October 2026 — a country accumulating dollars at an accelerating pace and consolidating its domestic political position, yet still paying a cost of financing that keeps it locked out of voluntary debt markets.

Reserve accumulation is, for now, the strongest story the economic team can tell. Since the start of the fourth stage of the monetary program in January, the BCRA led by Santiago Bausili has added more than USD 15.483 billion, a figure that comfortably exceeds the USD 10 billion annual target agreed with the IMF. In October alone, purchases amounted to USD 915 million, more than September and August combined. The wholesale dollar closed stable at $1,513, roughly 28% below the ceiling of the currency band set at $1,935, and economist Gustavo Ber noted that "the peso debt swap between the BCRA and the Treasury gives the BCRA an additional USD 718 million to intervene in the foreign exchange market." The exchange rate, which has risen only 9.6% in a year against year-on-year inflation of 33.4%, has become a deliberate disinflationary anchor.

Yet that very currency calm coexists with country risk that closed Thursday at 622 basis points, having climbed 33 units over the week. The number deserves careful reading: according to Infobae's own analysis, much of the move was exogenous. US Treasury yields fell sharply to 5.07% from the 5.40% recorded on October 7, driven by capital outflows from AI-linked assets following doubts about a possible bubble in that sector — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company dropped 2% despite record results. The arithmetic of the spread is unforgiving: with a higher risk-free benchmark, the Argentine excess compresses mechanically even if local bonds hold steady. Still, at 32 units below the year's high of 655 points recorded on October 2, the JP Morgan-compiled indicator rules out, for now, any possibility of Argentina returning to international debt markets — a goal the economic team itself had set for 2026. Against that backdrop, the Treasury wisely opted not to offer the Bonar 2029 in dollars at its latest auction, raising instead $1.75 trillion in pesos with a rollover of 136.14%, providing a cushion to meet maturities of up to $95 trillion before year-end.

The political picture briefly accompanied the markets. The opposition failed to secure the votes needed to convene and repeal DNU 70/23, the cornerstone of the government's deregulatory architecture. The opposition bloc fell eight deputies short of quorum, in large part thanks to pressure from governors aligned with the ruling party, several of whom had accompanied Milei days earlier at Argentina Week in Paris, where he gathered European investment commitments geared primarily toward the RIGI. Marcos Galperin, founder of Mercado Libre, whose shares trade on the Nasdaq, summed it up in terms that resonated on social media: "The day they tried to shake the government with violent marches and the opposition tried to knock down a DNU, the BCRA ended up buying USD 182 million." The simultaneity was its own argument.

From Bangkok, where he attended the annual meetings of the IMF and the World Bank, Minister Luis Caputo played several boards simultaneously. Addressing investors convened by JP Morgan, he assured that even in a scenario of no refinancing of the USD 5 billion in Bonares maturing in 2027, the financial program would show a surplus of USD 2 billion, a figure that would climb to USD 7 billion if refinancing is secured. He also revealed that the swap with China constitutes an active contingency line. The IMF, for its part, trimmed its 2026 growth forecast for Argentina from 3.5% to 2.1%, though Nigel Chalk, head of the Western Hemisphere Department, anticipated a recovery for 2027 driven by domestic demand. Kristalina Georgieva, on a panel shared with Caputo, went so far as to suggest that Argentina would prepay its debt to the Fund, as Greece did. The minister's reply — "you're going to be surprised" — was cryptic but deliberately optimistic.

Behind these short-term headlines, structural data tell a tougher story. According to INDEC, in the second quarter of 2026 there were 22,566,000 jobs, just 101,000 more than a year earlier; but that figure hides the destruction of 156,000 registered salaried jobs, offset by 28,000 informal workers and 229,000 additional self-employed. Since November 2023, formal private employment has accumulated a decline of 265,765 positions. Labor Secretary Julio Cordero argued before the Budget Committee that many workers prefer autonomy to dependent employment, but data from IERAL at Fundación Mediterránea add a more worrying dimension: over the past decade, hours worked rose 11% and capital services grew 14%, but output advanced only 3%, implying a 6% decline in labor productivity over the period. The economy is working more and producing proportionally less.

The trade opening imposed by the import policy is reconfiguring the productive fabric with ambiguous consequences. A Midas Consulting survey of 276 corporate decisions in sectors exposed to foreign competition found that 59.1% correspond to adaptation or reconversion, against 35.5% of downsizing or exit. Nearly 32,300 companies have stopped operating or registering formal workers since Milei took office, a 6.3% contraction of the productive network. Still, INDEC data show that installed capacity utilization stood at 58.5% in August, with five sectors — among them textiles (44.6%) and metalworking (35.9%) — operating below 50%. The 37.9% year-on-year drop in agricultural machinery manufacturing is the most eloquent symptom of the sectors losing ground under the new scheme.

Among the longer-term developments that consolidated this week, YPF CEO Horacio Marín — whose ADR trades on the New York Stock Exchange — confirmed at the CICyP that on November 26 the final investment decision will be signed for Argentina LNG, the USD 51 billion megaproject with Eni and XRG that could generate exports of USD 55 billion in 2031. Profertil, meanwhile, selected Techint Ingeniería & Construcción and Italy's Saipem for the technical services of its second urea plant in Bahía Blanca, an investment estimated at USD 2 billion that could be submitted under the RIGI. And Transportadora de Gas del Sur (TGS), controlled by Pampa Energía, is close to closing a USD 1.4 billion loan with JP Morgan, Santander, Citi, Itaú, Deutsche Bank, BBVA and ICBC for its project to export gas liquids from Vaca Muerta.

Globally, US September inflation at 3.7% year-on-year — driven by energy price increases stemming from the conflict with Iran, with Brent crude topping USD 106 — raised to 40% the probability that the Federal Reserve will hike rates on October 28, directly conditioning external financing costs for all emerging markets. A Bank of America survey revealed, however, that 41% of Latin American fund managers expect an improvement in Argentine assets over the next six months, up from 27% in September. A dissonance between medium-term investor sentiment and short-term rates that defines the central dilemma of the coming months.

What remains to be settled is whether the BCRA can maintain its pace of reserve purchases as the election year approaches, whether the Treasury will manage to lock in the sources of financing that Caputo mentioned without detailing to Bangkok investors, and whether inflation — which marked 1.9% in September and accumulates 33.4% over twelve months — can break below the 1% monthly threshold that the government itself needs to reach the October 2027 presidential elections with visible disinflation. The answer to those three questions will determine whether Thursday's paradox — reserves rising, country risk rising too — resolves in favor of the optimism shown in fund surveys or confirms the doubts that bond prices continue to reflect.
