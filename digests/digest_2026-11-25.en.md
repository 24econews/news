# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-25

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-25*

The official confirmation that Argentina entered technical recession in the third quarter of 2026 arrived on Tuesday with a built-in paradox: the very data point certifying the problem is already, in part, old news.

Indec released the September Monthly Economic Activity Estimator (EMAE), which posted a seasonally adjusted monthly rise of 1.1% — not enough to offset the 2.5% drop in July and the meager 0.4% rebound in August. With that result, the third quarter of 2026 closed with a contraction of roughly 1.8% versus the second, which had itself retreated 0.6% from the first. Two consecutive quarters of decline: textbook technical recession, though formal confirmation from the GDP data will not arrive until December 16.

Javier Milei's government chose attack as its best defense. Minister Luis Caputo highlighted the monthly improvement in September and pointed out that the EMAE trend-cycle sits 6.1% above the December 2023 level. Finance Secretary Federico Furiase was more explicit in denying that there is a recession in the strict sense, arguing that the drop was caused by "transitory" factors in July — the soccer World Cup, rains and the impact of the gas tariff adjustment — and that the economy has already shown a recovery over the following two months. For the market, however, the diagnosis is less benign: the BCRA's Market Expectations Survey projected 3.2% growth for the year back in January 2026; today that estimate has fallen to 0.7%. Cumulative growth over the first nine months of 2026 has shrunk to 1% year-on-year, a far cry from the 5% the government itself had budgeted.

What makes today's data particularly relevant is not just its content, but the moment in which it lands. Country risk climbed 57 points on Tuesday to 1,094 basis points — a new high since October 2025 — with dollar-denominated Global bonds averaging losses of more than 2% on the day. Pressure on the exchange rate is simultaneous: the wholesale dollar reached $1,593, the retail rate at Banco Nación hit $1,615 and the blue dollar touched $1,700, all nominal record highs. The contado con liquidación is trading near $1,800, leaving a 12.5% gap with the official rate. The pile-up of these moves over just a few days — with the peso carry trade being unwound and additional dollar demand tied to the maturity of a nearly USD 1.9 billion dollar-linked bond, D30N6 — paints a picture of financial pressure that the economic team can no longer dismiss as purely external.

Economist Luis Secco put it precisely: the economy has been stagnant since late in the first quarter of 2025, oscillating around a flat line. What is distinctive about the last two oscillations is that they have been to the downside. And the Financial Times, in an article published days earlier, had captured the international reading: the yield spread markets demand from Argentina has gone from just over 400 points in July to more than 1,000, driven by the drop in presidential approval — from 40% in January to around 30% in recent months — and doubts about the country's ability to meet upcoming debt payments.

The government faces, in parallel, a concrete and immediate financial challenge. With USD 4.4 billion in maturities due on January 9, the Economy Ministry confirmed the closing of loans with CAF and Fonplata totaling USD 800 million, to be disbursed in the first week of 2027. But that amount covers only a fraction of the total. On Thursday, moreover, the Treasury must roll over ARS 25.4 trillion in peso-denominated debt — of which an estimated 82% is in private hands — in a context where country risk has already surged past 1,100 points. The chosen strategy, according to market sources, was to offer short-dated instruments maturing before the 2027 elections and to include FX hedging options — an implicit signal that the government itself acknowledges the pressure on the exchange rate.

Against that backdrop of macro fragility, the contrast with the export sector is striking. Agribusiness exports accumulated USD 47.802 billion between January and October 2026, 14.2% more than in the same period of 2025. Beef exports have already surpassed in value the full-year total for 2025, with USD 4.3166 billion accumulated through October, an all-time record driven by elevated international prices. Corn sales grew 41% year-on-year in the first ten months of the year, overtaking Brazil in that segment. And YPF filed on Tuesday its application to join the RIGI regime for the Narambuena project in northern Vaca Muerta: USD 11.688 billion over 21 years, with an estimated production plateau of 80,000 barrels per day between 2035 and 2045. With this filing, total investment committed by the company under that regime is approaching USD 100 billion.

The divergence across sectors is, ultimately, the truest snapshot of Argentina's economy right now: hydrocarbons and agribusiness operating at historic highs while the metallurgical industry posted in October its worst installed-capacity utilization reading since 2016, at just 41.8%. Mass consumption showed in October its first positive year-on-year variation in ten months — a tepid 0.9% — but has still accumulated a 2.3% decline in the first ten months of the year.

What the market will be watching in the coming hours is the outcome of Thursday's debt auction, which will be the first real test for Caputo's team in front of private investors amid maximum tension. Further ahead, on December 16, the official third-quarter GDP confirmation arrives. And in the background, with the electoral year drawing ever closer, the question that will frame the economic debate over the coming months is whether the fourth quarter of 2026 will deliver enough of a recovery to flip the recessionary narrative before the presidential campaigns start shaping expectations.

## Corporate Watch

### YPF (NYSE: YPF)
The majority state-owned oil company filed on Tuesday its application to join the RIGI regime for the Narambuena project, a shale oil development in northern Vaca Muerta held 50/50 with Chevron, involving USD 11.688 billion in investment over 21 years and projected exports of USD 33 billion between 2027 and 2059. With this filing, total investment commitments made directly by YPF under the RIGI regime are approaching USD 100 billion.

### Kaszek Ventures
The venture capital firm founded by two former Mercado Libre executives closed its Kaszek Ventures VII fund at USD 650 million, exceeding its initial USD 600 million target on stronger-than-expected investor demand, making it the largest early-stage venture capital fund dedicated to Latin America, with cumulative commitments since 2011 nearing USD 4 billion.

### SanCor (in bankruptcy)
Three dairy companies — Milkaut (owned by France's Savencia group), Punta del Agua and Failar — filed individual proposals before the Rafaela court to produce under tolling arrangements at four of the six plants SanCor has kept operational since declaring bankruptcy in April 2026, in a scheme that would generate revenue for the creditor pool while the asset liquidation process moves forward.
