# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-09-18

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-09-18*

The figure that defines the state of Argentina's economy today isn't macroeconomic in the abstract sense: it's 1.73 million people without work in a country where GDP has just contracted for the second consecutive quarter in terms of domestic demand, where private consumption fell 2.4% quarter-on-quarter, and where labor informality climbed to 45%, its highest level since Javier Milei's government began the comparable series. The Indec data for the second quarter of 2026, released Thursday, confirms what partial evidence had been signaling: the real economy is deteriorating in ways that exports and export-oriented sectors cannot offset.

GDP declined 0.6% between April and June on a seasonally adjusted basis versus the prior quarter, after growing 0.7% in the first quarter. The quarterly drop was smaller than some private consultancies had projected, but the internal pattern is telling: every component of domestic demand retreated. Public consumption fell 2.3%, gross fixed capital formation dropped 0.8%—marking five consecutive quarters of year-on-year contraction—and imports sank 3.5%, signaling weakness in the internal market rather than external restraint. The lone exception was the export sector, which advanced 2.5% for the quarter and 13.7% year-on-year, propelled by Vaca Muerta, mining, and a harvest that will exceed USD 100 billion for the year for the first time. The economy, as Minister Luis Caputo himself described it, is K-shaped, and today's data certify that geometry with statistical brutality.

The unemployment rate rose to 7.9%, 0.3 points above the same period in 2025 and 0.1 point higher than the first quarter of 2026. While the quarterly differences aren't statistically significant, according to Indec itself, the direction of movement is: unemployment is rising, informality is expanding, and Universidad Torcuato Di Tella estimates the probability that the economy enters a technical recession at 82%. If the third quarter confirms another sequential contraction, Argentina will have met the technical definition of recession. Available July data offers no grounds for optimism: industrial production fell 5% year-on-year and installed capacity operated at 58.2%, with six sectors below 50%.

Faced with this picture, the government has deployed in recent days a battery of policy signals aimed at steering expectations toward the second half of the year and into 2027. The most notable was Senate approval of the Fiscal Innocence II law, which seeks to mobilize part of the USD 170 billion that, according to Banco Central estimates, Argentines hold outside the financial system. The measure broadens the universe of taxpayers eligible for the Simplified Income Tax Regime and strengthens legal guarantees for those who choose to declare these holdings, addressing objections that the accounting profession had raised since the original version took effect in December 2025. Tax specialists remain cautious: they warn that the pace at which those resources will flow into the formal circuit will be gradual and that promulgation and regulation will need to be accelerated to avoid leaving exposed the September 22 deadline for filing sworn declarations.

In the same session, the Senate gave preliminary approval to the new biofuels law, which raises the mandatory biodiesel blend from the current 7.5% to 10% and bioethanol to 15%. The Consejo Agroindustrial projects the new framework will attract USD 1 billion in investment. But the approval didn't come without friction: the deal between the ruling bloc and northern governors was the currency used to convene the session, illustrating the Milei government's growing dependence on ad hoc negotiations with allies in a year when the 2027 Budget arrived at Congress with projections the private market considers optimistic. The bill itself estimates growth of 4% for next year, versus the 2.9% projected in the BCRA's Market Expectations Survey, and end-to-end inflation of 18% when private analysts anticipate 22% to 25%.

The 2027 Budget also introduced elements that generated discomfort within the ruling coalition itself. The repeal of the automatic adjustment mechanism for the Universal Child Allowance took key allies by surprise, including PRO Senate bloc leader Patricia Bullrich, who publicly acknowledged she had not been informed before the bill was sent to Congress. The revelation that "Jurisdiction 91"—a budget line with no ministry or employees attached—will absorb ARS 14.8 trillion in next year's budget, a 163% increase over projected 2026 execution against estimated inflation of 18%, also raised questions about the true scope of the fiscal discipline the government proclaims.

On the external front, the environment grew more complicated this week with the U.S. Federal Reserve's decision to raise its benchmark rate by 25 basis points to a range of 3.75%-4%, the first hike since 2023. The move, unanimous among FOMC members under Kevin Warsh's leadership, was read as a signal that an additional hike could come before year-end. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.04%, its highest level since 2007, before easing slightly to 4.95%. For Argentina, the impact is twofold: it raises the potential cost of any return to international debt markets and puts upward pressure on sovereign risk. Argentina's EMBI+ closed above 510 basis points, the highest level of the month, despite a rebound in Argentine equities on Wall Street on the order of 1.1% for the Merval and up to 1.7% on the global indices, on a session when investors read the Fed's decision as a gesture of institutional credibility. The 2027 Budget explicitly acknowledges that the option of issuing debt under foreign law exists but is not committed to, given that "the persistence of the conflict between the United States and Iran, tensions in energy markets, and rising U.S. Treasury yields have increased global financial volatility."

The wholesale exchange rate traded at ARS 1,510, up just 1.50 pesos so far in September and 3.8% cumulative in 2026 versus retail inflation of 22%. The Banco Central purchased only USD 9 million on the day, confirming that the reserve accumulation strategy is contingent on genuine market supply rather than active intervention. Gross reserves stand at USD 49.816 billion, well below the 26.9%-of-GDP coverage ratio exhibited by Peru, whose central bank has spent two decades building that cushion with institutional continuity.

Two vectors will command attention in the coming weeks. The first is the arrival Monday the 21st of the IMF mission for the third review of the agreement, in which the government will have to explain the first-half fiscal target miss in a context where Caputo and Milei will be in New York for the UN meetings. The second is whether the dollar inflows from Vaca Muerta—TGN just announced a USD 1.5 billion pipeline under the RIGI to connect the Neuquén basin with the north of the country—and a wheat harvest expected to be a record will be enough to sustain exchange rate stability in a fourth quarter where electoral uncertainty is already beginning to weigh on investment and consumption decisions. Consumer confidence rose 2.4% in September, breaking a two-month decline, but remains 13% below the January 2025 peak. It's a rebound that halts a trend, not one that reverses it.

## Corporate Watch

### Transportadora de Gas del Norte (TGN)
TGN, controlled by Tecpetrol of Grupo Techint and CGC of Corporación América, announced the construction of the Manuel Belgrano Gas Pipeline, 753 kilometers between Neuquén and Córdoba, with a USD 1.5 billion investment to be filed under the RIGI; with complementary works on its existing network, total outlay would reach USD 2.2 billion. The project, scheduled to come online in April 2029, aims to replace USD 700 million in annual LNG imports and generate export capacity to neighboring countries of roughly USD 450 million per year.

### YPF (NYSE: YPF)
YPF, whose shares trade on the New York Stock Exchange and slipped 0.3% to USD 54.48 in the reference session, filed with the RIGI the LLL Oil project, an integrated Vaca Muerta development program contemplating USD 25 billion in investment over 15 years, the drilling of 1,152 wells, and a target production of 240,000 barrels of oil per day by 2032, primarily for export.

### Burford Capital (LSE: BUR / NYSE: BUR)
