# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-18

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-18*

Argentina's country risk topped 850 basis points this week while local assets showed relative resilience in the face of global turbulence, but what's truly telling isn't that number itself but the contradiction that defines it: the same country posting the largest financial surplus of the year and announcing the biggest automotive investment in its history cannot access international credit markets, faces a projected shortfall of USD 17 billion for 2027, and watches as its star economist acknowledges that inflation isn't falling as fast as expected. Argentina today operates as two overlapping countries, and the gap between them widens as next October's presidential elections approach.

The Ministry of Economy confirmed that the National Public Sector posted a financial surplus of $1.578 trillion in October, the largest of the year, with revenues growing 31.2% year-on-year and primary spending contained at 23.6%. Luis Caputo hailed the figure and reaffirmed that the cumulative primary result for the first ten months of the year hovers around 1.4% of GDP. Fiscal discipline is real. But that same week, a report from IERAL of the Fundación Mediterránea warned that in 2027 the government will have to meet maturities of USD 28 billion plus USD 5.6 billion net to the IMF, with an estimated shortfall of USD 17 billion that has no visible financing. The Central Bank bought on average USD 1.9 billion per month between January and July, a pace that fell to USD 880 million between August and October. With country risk hitting an intraday high of 863 points during the week, the window to issue debt in international markets — which was briefly open when the indicator grazed 400 points in July — has slammed shut. Morgan Stanley warned that pre-election portfolio dollarization could pressure the exchange rate and interrupt disinflation even if the economic program remains intact. JP Morgan projects that Argentines will reach the polls with monthly inflation around 2%, virtually unchanged from current levels.

That inflationary stagnation is one of the sharpest points of political tension. President Javier Milei acknowledged at the BCRA's Monetary and Banking Conference that inflation is not falling at the pace the government expected, attributing the deviation to external shocks — the doubling of oil prices due to the conflict between the United States and Iran, elevated international rates — and to the so-called "Kirchnerist risk" that, according to Caputo, keeps corporate credit demand paralyzed. BCRA Vice President Vladimir Werning sharply rejected proposals for pre-election fiscal stimulus, calling them "very dangerous." Against this official framing, Domingo Cavallo warned at UBA that the program "runs the risk of falling apart before the elections" unless monetary and exchange-rate reforms are introduced, particularly the full lifting of the capital controls. Carlos Melconian was more vivid: "they kicked the anthill, and now they're hanging from the crossbar." Economist Martín Tobal, of the Bank of Mexico, offered the most uncomfortable data point for the administration: of the 44 governments that implemented stabilization plans in his historical sample, only 17 won reelection.

The wholesale dollar closed the week at $1,552, with a cumulative rise of just 6.7% in 2026 against inflation of 26.3% over the same period. The multilateral real exchange rate hit lows since the start of the band scheme, fueling the divergence between the official dollar and financial dollars. The contado con liquidación reached $1,692 with a 9.1% gap versus the official rate, while the blue dollar hit $1,600. The depreciation of the Brazilian real — up 16% since Lula's reelection and hitting 5.78 reais per dollar, its highest since April 2025 — adds pressure on Argentine competitiveness given that Brazil is the country's main trading partner. The VIX, the global volatility index known as the "fear index," rose 11% to 25.45 points on the week, approaching the 30 threshold that historically triggers alarms in emerging markets. The Dow Jones lost 1.6% on Tuesday's session and the Nasdaq fell 0.7%, though Argentine assets showed relative resilience: the MSCI Merval rose 2% in pesos while YPF ADRs rebounded 2.2% to USD 36.41 and Pampa Energía advanced 2.6%.

The real economy operates at two speeds that the UIA formalized in a diagnosis presented to Treasury Secretary Carlos Guberman. Industry has posted 22 consecutive months of declining employment, the textile sector lost more than 29,000 jobs in 2026, and industrial activity fell 12% versus 2022, with sectors competing with imports retreating 19%. Export sectors, by contrast, show an opposite reality: agro-industrial exports set a record with more than 101 million tons over ten months, surpassing the previous record by 10 million, while oil displaced soy as the top export product with a 58% year-on-year rise. In this dual context, Neuquén is the only province that has increased its number of employer firms since November 2023, pulled along by Vaca Muerta, while 23 jurisdictions post declines and the total number of private-sector employer firms has fallen 6.3%, equivalent to 32,163 firms since that month.

The Toyota investment — USD 1.341 billion under the RIGI to manufacture the hybrid Tacoma in Zárate starting in 2030, with capacity for 50,000 units a year and 70% destined for export — is the industrial story of the year and the largest investment by an automaker in the country's history. The project joins the award of the 24-inch pipes for Argentina LNG to Tenaris of Grupo Techint, reversing three consecutive failed tenders in the sector; the announcement that Pampa Energía will set up Pampa AI to develop data centers powered by Vaca Muerta gas; and the dredging works at the port of Bahía Blanca awarded to Jan De Nul to reach 50 feet of draft with 15 years of maintenance. In parallel, the Government amended the RIGI via Decree 1366/2026 to allow foreign-capital companies based in the country to compete for the 20% local-supplier quota, a decision that worries domestic industry. Peter Thiel, co-founder of Palantir and PayPal, expanded his position in Vista Energy by 58.6% to 1.88 million shares valued at USD 122.8 million, matching his bet on Vaca Muerta with his Amazon holding.

What lingered in the air this week is that the gap between the Argentina of infrastructure megaprojects and the Argentina of delinquent micro-SMEs — a 17.6% default rate, with food services at 29.2% — remains a structural fault line that no RIGI can fix. The government rolled over 60% of the dollar-linked bill maturing at month-end, reducing the commitment from USD 4.742 billion to USD 1.882 billion, a technically competent maneuver that nonetheless underscores the persistence of the 2027 liquidity problem. The key items for the coming weeks are the approval of the 2027 Budget in the Chamber of Deputies, the second RIGI Round on December 2 at the Hipódromo de San Isidro, the debate over suspending the PASO primaries — which the market considers the only political catalyst capable of compressing country risk — and the third-quarter activity data that Indec will publish shortly and that could confirm or rule out a technical recession.

### Toyota Motor Corporation / Toyota Argentina (TYO: 7203 / NYSE: TM)

### Vista Energy (NYSE: VIST)

### Tenaris (NYSE: TS / Borsa Italiana: TEN)
