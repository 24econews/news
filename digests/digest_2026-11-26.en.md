# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-26

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-26*

The peso debt auction that the Argentine Treasury faces today — $25.4 trillion in maturities concentrated primarily in two fixed-rate instruments — arrives at the worst possible moment and turns this session into a real-time stress test of Luis Caputo's financial program.

The backdrop explains everything. In just four months, country risk has climbed from the 402-basis-point low recorded on July 10 to surpass 1,100 basis points at its intraday peak this week, before easing slightly to 1,067 points at Wednesday's close. Behind that 165% surge — unprecedented since past electoral crises — at least three vectors converge: the tightening of global financial conditions, with the Federal Reserve preparing another rate hike in December and the U.S. PCE climbing to 3.8% year-on-year in October according to the Commerce Department, its highest level in three years; the depreciation of the Brazilian real which, following Lula da Silva's victory in October's runoff, pushed the dollar close to six reais and spread currency pressure across the region; and, the dominant factor according to Wall Street analysts consulted by La Nación, the electoral premium that the market is pricing in a full year ahead of the 2027 presidential vote.

That premium has a precise and revealing quantitative expression. The AO27 bond, which matures in 2027, yields just 4.7% annually, while the AO28, maturing on October 31, 2028 — that is, already under the next administration — yields 21.6%. The nearly 17-percentage-point gap represents, according to market calculations published by Infobae, 43% of the current country risk level. Uncertainty about who will govern from December 2027 is not a future risk the market can defer: it is already operating as the main distorter of Argentine asset prices today.

Caputo tried to stem the bleeding in two simultaneous media appearances on Wednesday. He publicly detailed how the government plans to meet the USD 4.378 billion maturity in January: USD 1.2 billion from the IMF and multilaterals — subject to the Fund's board approving the third review of the agreement, scheduled for the first week of December — USD 1 billion in guaranteed loans from CAF and Fonplata to be disbursed before year-end, USD 300 million from the privatization of AySA, and USD 1.8 billion that the Treasury will purchase from the Central Bank using the primary surplus. Deputy Minister José Luis Daza went further: he confirmed at El Cronista's Leaders Summit that the government has already submitted the request to activate USD 5 billion from the currency swap with China as a contingency if the debt market does not reopen. The combined signals partially calmed Wednesday's session — country risk retreated 25 points from the intraday high of 1,139 — but traders note the messages are insufficient to resolve the uncertainty extending toward the July 2027 maturity, which totals USD 5.7 billion.

Today's auction is, in that context, the first concrete thermometer. The Finance Secretariat adopted the defensive strategy the market expected: eight instruments, nearly all maturing before the elections, including currency hedging options via dollar-linked securities, with the intent of attracting investors seeking protection from peso depreciation. The wholesale dollar has already advanced 4.6% in November, well above the estimated monthly inflation rate, and the contado con liquidación is trading near $1,770, opening a gap of more than 10% with the official exchange rate.

The real economy offers little political relief for the government. September data from the Monthly Economic Activity Estimator, released this week, confirmed that GDP contracted 1.8% in the third quarter of 2026 relative to the second — which had already fallen 0.6% — configuring the technical recession that analyst consensus had taken for granted but whose formalization will pressure the official narrative. Accumulated growth in the first nine months of 2026 fell to 1%, down from the 2.2% recorded at the close of the first half. JP Morgan, in its Argentina 2027 Economic Outlook report published today and signed by Diego W. Pereira and Lucila Barbeito, projects growth of 1.8% for 2027 and inflation of 23%, with the caveat that a tight election could increase pressure on the peso, bonds and reserves. The bank projects continuity of Milei's policies as its base case but warns that a return to fiscally lax policies in the event of political alternation would raise questions about the surplus, the exchange rate regime and the IMF agreement.

Industry presents the same bleak picture. According to Orlando J. Ferreres & Asociados, factory output fell 3% year-on-year in October and has accumulated a 2.6% contraction in the first ten months of 2026, the fourth consecutive month in the red compared to 2025. FIEL recorded an even steeper year-on-year decline of 4.3%. The metallurgical sector operated in October at 41.8% of installed capacity, the worst reading for that month since 2016, according to Adimra. Supermarket sales at constant prices fell 3% year-on-year in September, stringing together nine consecutive months of declines, as private consumption remains the Achilles' heel of a domestic demand that cannot find traction. Against that backdrop, Ford decided to extend the summer shutdown at its General Pacheco plant to eight weeks — double the usual period — citing weaker regional demand for the Ranger pickup and the need to reduce inventory. Stellantis, which this week inaugurated its turbodiesel engine plant in Córdoba — part of a USD 385 million investment — made clear that the continuity of its Argentine operations depends on competitiveness: its regional president, Herlander Zola, publicly called for "clear rules" and "balanced competition," weeks after warning that local labor costs are double those of Brazil.

Partially offsetting the bleak picture is export performance. Agro-industrial exports reached USD 47.802 billion in the first ten months of 2026, a 14.2% year-on-year increase. The 2025/2026 harvest set a historic record of 171 million tons, 21% more than the previous cycle. Beef exports have already surpassed in value those of all of 2025, reaching USD 4.317 billion between January and October, on track to close the year around USD 5 billion according to the ABC Consortium. YPF, for its part, presented this week to the RIGI the Narambuena project for the northern part of Vaca Muerta, with planned investment of USD 11.688 billion shared 50/50 with Chevron, the second of three projects that will bring the oil company's direct commitments under the regime to nearly USD 100 billion cumulatively.

What the market will be watching in the coming hours is the outcome of the peso auction: the rollover rate will determine whether the Treasury manages to refinance maturities without releasing additional liquidity to the system. Beyond that, next week brings the IMF's third review — crucial to unlocking the disbursements that form part of the January payment scheme — the 2027 Budget debate in the Chamber of Deputies, and Indec's release on December 9 of official October industrial production data. Third-quarter GDP data, which will confirm or qualify the technical recession, will be known on December 16. None of those milestones will resolve the variable the market considers decisive: the 2027 electoral outcome.

## Corporate Watch

### YPF (NYSE: YPF / BCBA: YPFD)
The state-controlled oil company presented to the RIGI the Narambuena project, a shale oil development in the northern part of Vaca Muerta shared 50/50 with Chevron that contemplates USD 11.688 billion in investment over 21 years, with an estimated production plateau of 80,000 barrels per day between 2035 and 2045 and projected exports of approximately USD 33 billion between 2027 and 2059; with this filing, YPF's direct commitments under the RIGI are approaching USD 100 billion.

### Tether / Lemon
Tether, the world's largest stablecoin issuer, announced a USD 14.5 million investment in Lemon, the Argentine crypto wallet with 4.5 million users in the region, including USD 5 million in direct investment and USD 9.5 million earmarked for an incentive program to expand the use of USDT and XAUT in Latin America; the deal adds to the more than USD 66 million Lemon had previously raised in rounds led by Kingsway, ParaFi and F-Prime (Fidelity).

### Los Grobo Agropecuaria
The agribusiness firm, in reorganization proceedings since February 2025, requested judicial authorization to sell its stake in the Cánepa Hermanos mill and a grain storage facility in Chivilcoy to Molinos Fénix for USD 5.5 million, with proceeds earmarked to partially cancel a syndicated loan from seven banks — Galicia, Santander Argentina, Supervielle, Hipotecario, Macro, Provincia de Buenos Aires and Industrial — totaling USD 25 million, according to sources familiar with the transaction cited by El Cronista.
