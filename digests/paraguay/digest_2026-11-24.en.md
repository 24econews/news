# Paraguay: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-24

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-24*

The collapse of Ueno Bank has ceased to be a contained financial crisis and has become the most significant institutional disruption Paraguay has faced in at least two decades, with the real scale of the damage — USD 888 million in committed public funds, as confirmed by the Ministry of Economy and Finance — being revised upward with each passing day.

What began as the intervention of a recently established banking entity has evolved into an earthquake simultaneously shaking the Banco Central del Paraguay, Itaipú Binacional, the Banco Nacional de Fomento, the Administración Nacional de Electricidad, and President Santiago Peña's own economic cabinet. The BCP warned that the fiscal deficit stemming from the crisis could reach USD 1.5 billion, a figure that a former Deputy Minister of Economy has already labeled the worst fiscal management since 2003, casting doubt on the budget convergence target pledged for 2028.

The architecture of the scandal continues to reveal new layers. According to ABC Color, Itaipú awarded a multi-million-dollar security contract to a company linked to the Vázquez group, Ueno's owner, despite an internal document from the binational entity requiring its banking providers to demonstrate at least five years of market experience — a requirement that Ueno, being recently established, failed to meet. The contradiction between Itaipú's internal rules and the actual practice of its officials forced the removal of Paraguayan Director General Zacarías Irún and led to a complete overhaul of the leadership on the Paraguayan side of the hydroelectric plant. Hugo Zárate took over as the new Director General with a declared mandate to prioritize the tariff agreement with Brazil for 2027, though his own prior onboarding with Ueno is already under journalistic scrutiny, as reported by Última Hora.

The cascade of institutional effects does not stop there. The Banco Nacional de Fomento cancelled the tender to acquire a new core banking system after the process was contaminated by the chosen provider's connections to the Ueno universe. The Public Prosecutor's Office secured the Vázquez group's IT backup, while the Ministry of Economy formally filed criminal complaints against its executives. Nicanor Duarte Frutos joined the Itaipú Board alongside three ministers, in a reconfiguration of the binational entity's governance that, according to ABC Color, left the Foreign Ministry without direct representation.

The impact on the real economy is already quantifiable. Micro-enterprises and MSMEs report revenue declines of up to 35%, according to Última Hora, in part because many held restricted deposits at the intervened entity. The Agencia Financiera de Desarrollo opened an emergency credit line for the segment and the BCP approved exceptional liquidity measures, but trade associations such as Capasu and the Unión de Gremios de la Producción warn that what is hardest to recover is not the money but the trust, lost in a matter of days. The UGP was categorical: "the most precious thing was lost."

Markets have also reacted. The guaraní, which J.P. Morgan recently singled out for its appreciation against the dollar — a trend the UGP itself had flagged as damaging to export competitiveness — now faces bidirectional pressures: on one hand, uncertainty over the health of the financial system; on the other, the BCP's international reserves, which according to ABC Color rose by USD 600 million amid the turbulence, providing a credibility cushion the central bank urgently needs. The Dirección Nacional de Ingresos Tributarios reported a 10% drop in customs revenue, complicating fiscal headroom at the worst possible moment.

The macroeconomic backdrop is, paradoxically, among the strongest in the region. Economic activity grew 5.5% year-to-date through September and 3.9% in that specific month, driven by services and industry, and BCP projections for 2026 remain around 4.5%. The IMF, which began its periodic review of the Paraguayan economy this week in meetings with authorities, will find a robust macro picture contrasting with severe institutional vulnerability. The underground economy, which according to figures cited by ABC Color reached 47.1% of GDP with flows of USD 23.595 billion, offers the structural context that made Ueno's existence and expansion possible.

The new Minister of Economy, Óscar Lovera — who replaced Carlos Fernández Valdovinos and softened the "war economy" rhetoric toward "intelligent austerity" — confirmed that the Executive submitted to Congress an addendum to the 2027 General National Budget that cuts USD 276 million in spending. The Senate is expected to begin the budget debate next week, with a vote possible on Thursday or Friday, in a context in which business associations are demanding that the State adjust its own spending before seeking to raise the tax burden, and some senators are already pushing a bill to roll back the "super-powers" that the Ministry of Economy law granted the MEF over budget reallocations.

What should be watched closely in the coming sessions is, first, whether the Senate approves the 2027 budget with the cutback addendum or returns it modified, which would determine the government's real fiscal maneuvering room in a high-pressure year. Second, the progress of the IMF review and whether its public conclusions question the soundness of the banking regulatory framework. Third, the State's capacity to effectively manage the companies tied to Ueno — a challenge the Centro de Importadores del Paraguay described as difficult to execute — and whether the USD 888 million in committed public funds can be recovered within reasonable timeframes. The macroeconomic stability Paraguay built over years is real, but the Ueno crisis has shown that it can coexist with institutional fragilities capable of threatening it.
