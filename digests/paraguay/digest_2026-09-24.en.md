# Paraguay: Economic Pulse — 2026-09-24

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-09-24*

Paraguay's financial system has shifted from being a narrative asset of the government to becoming its principal source of political vulnerability, and that reversal of roles defines the week with unusual clarity.

The meeting convened by President Santiago Peña with the Asociación de Bancos del Paraguay (Asoban) and the Banco Central del Paraguay (BCP) exposed tensions the ruling party would have preferred to keep out of public debate. As ABC Color reported, former Finance Minister Dionisio Borda warned about concentration risk in the financial system and called for preserving the BCP's institutional autonomy, while economist Manuel Ferreira flagged the danger of the country losing confidence in its central bank. Business associations placed the institution's credibility at the center of the debate — a sign that the "noise" surrounding the financial system, in the words of a former minister cited by ABC Color, has spilled beyond academic circles and reached organized business. Bank delinquency stands at 2.3%, slightly below the regional average according to figures cited in the press, meaning the system faces no technical crisis. The problem is one of governance and signaling, not solvency — which, paradoxically, makes the damage harder to contain, because there is no single number to fix it.

This internal debate coincides with a 2027 budget that the Bicameral Commission takes up again this week, with figures generating pushback on several fronts at once. The Ministry of Public Works and Communications (MOPC) will see its allocation rise between 33% and 34%, with physical investment climbing to USD 1 billion according to ABC Color, concentrated on road infrastructure. At the same time, the Instituto de Previsión Social (IPS) acknowledged before lawmakers a monthly deficit of up to USD 15 million and carries an accumulated debt of USD 1.2 billion that legislators questioned directly. The Ministry of Education is cutting resources for physical investment and learning while raising its wage bill — a trade-off that specialists consulted by ABC Color describe as concerning given the already limited budget execution in 2026. The Ministry of Health, meanwhile, will receive more resources despite the same track record of low execution. The Ministry of Economy and Finance projects that the budget will be back on track in 2028, once the peak of debt maturities has been absorbed, according to La Nación Paraguay.

The IMF, in documents cited by Última Hora, listed eleven pending tasks for Paraguay, including improving tax efficiency, broadening the fiscal base and advancing against money laundering. That agenda ties directly into the creation of the Dirección Nacional de Ingresos Tributarios (DNIT), the product of the merger between SET and Customs pushed since the start of the Peña era. Óscar Orué, its director, has promised to lift the tax burden from 10% to 12% of GDP and boost revenue by USD 400 million annually, according to the review published by Hoy. It is an ambitious promise in a country where, according to the same IMF, the tax burden remains among the lowest in the region.

Fuel prices add another layer of complexity. Diesel has accumulated an increase of up to 43.8% according to ABC Color, and the fifth consecutive hike at private pumps began this week. The private sector complains that Petropar distorts the market by keeping subsidized prices that the market suspects will be held artificially until after October's municipal elections, according to sources cited by ABC Color. The state oil company can sustain that stance financially for some time, but the upward trend is already penetrating the logistics chain and threatens to spill into production costs across multiple sectors. For an economy whose BCP reported 5.6% expansion through the end of July according to La Nación Paraguay, and where optimism about growth near 5% annually persists among economic agents, the inflation risk via energy is the external vector to watch most closely.

On the energy front, Paraguay and Brazil resumed conversations on electrical interconnection following a diplomatic visit, according to ABC Color, in a process that could expand the export capacity of Paraguay's hydro surpluses. At the same time, ANDE is preparing to demand a USD 40 million guarantee from British firm Atome if it fails to meet the September 30 deadline tied to a green hydrogen project, while a USD 117 million complaint before the Comptroller's Office remains unresolved, according to the same outlet. A consultant cited by ABC Color warned that the state entity's position would be weakened in any eventual international arbitration, pointing to institutional negligence in managing the contract.

The guaraní closed the week showing appreciation against the dollar, a trend that benefits importers and makes exports more expensive, according to ABC Color's foreign exchange reports. Remittances, which according to the same outlet reach USD 732 million annually, act as a structural support for domestic demand and the real estate sector. In parallel, President Peña announced new credit lines for the productive sector and the merger of the Ministry of Industry and Commerce (MIC) with the Secretaría Nacional de Turismo (Senatur), an institutional consolidation that the MIC minister presented as part of "deep reforms" for industry, according to ABC Color.

What to watch this week: the Bicameral Commission's progress on the 2027 PGN and whether lawmakers manage to modify the IPS or MOPC figures; the BCP's stance in the face of criticism over its institutional autonomy; a possible price adjustment from Petropar; and the September 30 deadline in the Atome contract, which could trigger the first major international arbitration against a Paraguayan state company in recent years.
