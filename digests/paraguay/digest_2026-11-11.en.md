# Paraguay: Economic Pulse — 2026-11-11

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-11*

The guaraní posted the steepest depreciation of any currency in the world against the dollar this week, according to Bloomberg — a distinction nobody in Asunción is celebrating. The slide, triggered by the collapse of Ueno Bank and the subsequent resignation of President Santiago Peña, which the government later sought to play down, has turned what had been a macroeconomic success story into a governance crisis whose ramifications stretch from the bond markets in New York to the stalled construction sites of Asunción's would-be "Puerto Madero."

The immediate trigger is well known, but its dimensions keep expanding. Ueno Bank, taken over by the Banco Central del Paraguay after disclosing a non-performing loan ratio that jumped from 0.10% to 14.49% before the collapse, has dragged a growing number of state institutions down with it. The binational Itaipú entity had around USD 163.8 million plus guaraní-denominated equivalents on deposit at the bank; Yacyretá will also move to recover funds; the Ministry of Economy and Finance filed a criminal complaint over PYG 373.838 billion from the Caja Fiscal — roughly USD 60 million — tied up in the institution. The National Service for Plant Quality and Health (Senave) reported the loss of PYG 40 billion, and state-owned Petropar also appears in the court filing. The accumulation of public funds at a bank that was already showing severe signs of portfolio deterioration constitutes, in itself, a systemic supervisory failure that goes beyond individual fraud.

Moody's was blunt in its assessment: the Ueno affair "highlights governance weaknesses," though the agency expects an orderly transition. Standard & Poor's, for its part, indicated that the political transition does not, for the moment, alter the country's rating. The Ministry of Economy insists that investment grade is not at risk, but analysts warn that Paraguayan debt could become more expensive and that pressure on the fiscal accounts will intensify. Public debt now stands at USD 22.569 billion, up 13.2% from 2025. Ueno's bonds issued in New York also raise the prospect of international lawsuits that could complicate the Paraguayan state's access to capital markets.

Pressure on the exchange rate adds another dimension to the problem. Economists consulted by ABC Color note that the massive return of deposits — the BCP reported that 93% of depositors eligible for guarantees have already received their advance, and that guarantee payouts have reached 1.2 million affected clients — could generate dollar demand, as part of the funds returned in guaraníes will seek refuge in hard currency. The BCP has already returned nearly PYG 1 trillion, and the operation will consume roughly 50% of the resources of the Deposit Guarantee Fund. The new central bank president, Jorge Schreiner — whose appointment was approved by the Senate alongside that of Fernando Rivarola as director — said the immediate priority is to "restore credibility" and to intervene in the FX market when necessary. International reserves, which rose by USD 600 million amid the uncertainty, give the BCP some room to act.

The crisis has triggered an accelerated reorganization of the state's economic apparatus. Carlos Fernández Valdovinos, the economy minister who had managed the investment-grade narrative and the robust growth story, was removed along with Claudia Centurión from the Itaipú Council. Óscar Lovera takes over the Ministry of Economy carrying a complex immediate agenda: revenue under pressure, outstanding debts to suppliers and pharmaceutical companies — the MEF announced a disbursement of PYG 184.194 billion to suppliers and USD 80 million in payments to pharmaceutical firms — and the need to rework the 2027 General Budget, whose consideration in the Chamber of Deputies was suspended precisely because of the crisis. The BCP, meanwhile, is keeping its monetary policy unchanged, citing cumulative growth of 5.7% through August and prices under control.

The web around Grupo Vázquez — owner of Ueno Bank and of companies including Itti, Monchis and media outlets — has set off a cascade of additional institutional consequences. The Comptroller General's Office recommended terminating Nubicom's contract for the country's 5G network, citing an "unauthorized sale of shares," and forwarded the report to the Public Prosecutor's Office. Conatel opened an administrative proceeding against the same company. The National Directorate of Public Procurement ordered the Banco Nacional de Fomento to reassess the bids in the tender for its core banking system, in which Itti had participated. The National Navigation and Ports Administration contract for the Asunción port project, which Ueno Bank had taken under its control, has seen construction come to a halt. The Public Prosecutor's Office opened three criminal cases — including complaints against Peña himself and former BCP president Carlos Carvallo, who has already turned himself in — and reinforced its investigative team. A prosecutorial delegation entered the central bank as part of the inquiries.

The organized private sector is demanding institutional responses that go beyond emergency management. The Unión Industrial Paraguaya, Feprinco, the Centro de Importadores, Cappro, the Asociación Rural del Paraguay and other trade groups are unanimous in calling for investigations without privileges, protection of legal certainty and effective sanctions. Fecoprod, which groups agribusiness cooperatives, estimates that the direct impact on its members will be limited, which suggests that the risk of contagion to the more robust real sector of the Paraguayan economy — agriculture, which the BCP identifies as the pillar of current growth — remains contained for now.

What markets and investors will need to watch in the coming days is how quickly the new economic team can stabilize the exchange rate, how receptive Congress proves to the revised budget the MEF will have to submit under a different logic than before, and whether Moody's or S&P move to adjust their outlook once the damage to the Deposit Guarantee Fund and to the finances of state institutions is quantified with precision. Cumulative growth of 5.7% through August and favorable projections from the IMF and the UN for Paraguay in 2026 offer a solid macroeconomic floor, but institutional credibility — the hardest variable to rebuild — is now the country's scarcest asset.

## Corporate Watch

### Ueno Bank
The fintech institution taken over by the BCP saw its non-performing loan ratio rise from 0.10% to 14.49% ahead of the collapse, with at least 27 people indicted and funds from state institutions — including Itaipú, Yacyretá, Petropar and the Caja Fiscal — trapped at the bank; the MEF filed a criminal complaint over some USD 60 million in public funds committed to the entity.

### Nubicom / Grupo Vázquez
The Comptroller General recommended terminating Nubicom's contract for the rollout of the national 5G network on the grounds of an "unauthorized sale of shares" linked to Grupo Vázquez, and referred the file to the Public Prosecutor's Office; at the same time, Conatel opened administrative proceedings against the company, putting at risk the continuity of the only 5G spectrum contract awarded in Paraguay.

### Atome
The renewable energy company rejected allegations of contractual breach with the Administración Nacional de Electricidad (ANDE) and clarified that its international arbitration notice is directed against the Paraguayan state, in a context in which the Sitrande union links the return of Félix Sosa to the ANDE presidency with his previous refusal to sign the contract with the company.
