# Paraguay: The Economic Pulse — 2026-10-26

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-10-26*

The guaraní has emerged this week as Latin America's best-performing currency, and that seemingly good news is generating one of the sharpest tensions in Paraguay's current economic landscape: the same interest-rate differential that is strengthening the local currency is squeezing exporter margins, sowing doubts about the sustainability of the 2027 budget, and forcing the government's new economic team to urgently calibrate its priorities.

The currency move is no accident. According to ABC Color, the guaraní leads regional currencies driven by sustained export flows and by the interest-rate differential attracting short-term capital. But that same mechanism, analysts cited by the outlet warn, "increases pressure on investment" by raising the real cost of credit for the productive sector. Ten business chambers have already requested an urgent meeting with the Ministry of Economy and Finance to flag the impact on production, while the Intergremial de Empresarios announced a protest in front of the ministry's headquarters. The grievance is concrete: a cheaper dollar reduces, in guaraní terms, the revenues of soy, meat and agro-industrial exporters — sectors that account for much of the dynamism the Central Bank reported for 2025, when the economy grew 6.6%, a figure the BCP itself accompanied with the unorthodox label of a "war economy."

The currency backdrop aggravates a fiscal picture that was already under strain. The MEF reported that the annualized deficit through September reached 3% of GDP, equivalent to nearly USD 2 billion, a level that economists gathered in public debate acknowledged will be practically impossible to steer back to the legal limit in less than a decade. Caja Fiscal funds have fallen to levels not seen since 2009, interest payments on public debt grew 12.9%, and central government payroll spending advanced 9.9% — all while the MEF declared it lacked the resources to raise salaries for healthcare workers. Wages, pensions and debt service already account for nearly 70% of public financing, according to ABC Color, leaving genuinely narrow fiscal room for maneuver. In that context, the discussion of the 2027 General Budget turns politically explosive: legislators have openly questioned how the MEF will finance public spending if the dollar remains depressed, given that a significant share of tax revenue originates in dollar-denominated transactions.

The Dirección Nacional de Ingresos Tributarios — which brought together the former SET and Customs under single leadership following the merger enacted in the final days of the Abdo Benítez government but conceived by Santiago Peña's team — projects collecting 14.3% more in 2027, though it explicitly warns of the risk the guaraní's appreciation poses to those projections. The same DNIT is proposing to apply VAT to hybrid and electric vehicles and insists on regulating corporate retained earnings, initiatives that have reignited tensions with the private sector. The Intergremial is not the only dissenting voice: the Asociación de Bancos del Paraguay called a recent BCP report "incomplete," urging equal treatment under the law, in a dispute that forms the backdrop to the crisis between the regulator and the fintech Ueno — a situation whose outcome concerns the head of the Unión Industrial Paraguaya given the potential impact on employment and confidence in the financial system. One economist cited by ABC Color was more direct: the BCP must deliver visible results with swift corrective measures if it wants to preserve public confidence.

While the domestic front accumulates tensions, two structural pieces of news point in the opposite direction. The Ministry of Industry and Commerce's confirmation that US companies Araico and Crusoe will use power from Itaipú to install an artificial intelligence hub in Paraguay is the most significant signal of foreign investment in recent weeks. ANDE has already created a special tariff for AI centers, set between USD 48.91 and USD 56.39 per MWh, and estimates monthly revenues of up to USD 10 million once the operation reaches full capacity — some USD 120 million in additional annual income for the state utility, according to official projections. Nonetheless, ANDE engineers have requested that future similar contracts go through public tender, and large industrial consumers have demanded clear rules through 2037, a sign that the regulatory framework for this new energy-intensive industry is still being built on the fly. In parallel, the Bioceanic Route Bridge entered its final stretch with USD 103 million in financing from Itaipú, a project that could reshape Paraguay's logistical positioning at the heart of South America.

On the multilateral front, the IMF highlighted the country's macroeconomic strength but delivered a list of eleven pending tasks that includes deficit reduction, combating money laundering, and improving financial supervision. The Minister of Economy and the BCP President took part in meetings with the World Bank and the IMF, at a time when the MEF itself projects returning to the legal deficit limit in 2028 — a promise markets will watch with the same attention as the exchange rate's behavior.

What to watch in the coming days: whether the government yields to pressure from export chambers with some form of currency compensation mechanism or credit relief measure, how the 2027 PGN debate advances in the Bicameral Committee amid the constraints imposed by a weak dollar, and whether the BCP-Ueno crisis escalates to a point that forces broader regulatory intervention with ripple effects across the banking system.
