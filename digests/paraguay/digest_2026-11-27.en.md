# Paraguay: Economic Pulse — 2026-11-27

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-27*

This morning's arrest of former Economy and Finance Minister Carlos Fernández Valdovinos transforms the Ueno Bank collapse into something qualitatively different: no longer merely a financial crisis, but an institutional crisis with criminal consequences reaching the highest levels of the Paraguayan state. According to La Nación, a judge ordered his arrest and Fernández Valdovinos turned himself in at a police station — a development that upends the official narrative framing the Ueno affair as primarily a private banking governance problem. BCP President Liz Cramer was even more blunt: "there is much more to unravel," she declared, taking direct aim at the former official for interference in the very institution he once led.

The scale of the crisis becomes clearer with each disclosure. Itaipú, the binational hydroelectric entity that generates royalties vital to Paraguay's budget, had funds frozen at Ueno Bank, and according to Última Hora, works tied to the bank remain stalled with no action plan yet defined. The binational denied having requested a royalty advance ahead of the bank's collapse and clarified that its solvency is not compromised, but Director Balmelli pointed to responsibilities within both boards of the institution. Meanwhile, legislators are demanding reports from public entities regarding funds placed at Ueno, and the Attorney General's Office, the BCP, and Senabico are coordinating actions on seized assets, among them 27 luxury items frozen by court order.

For retail depositors, the week brings a measure of relief. The BCP confirmed that roughly a thousand savers will be transferred to other banks in the system and will receive coverage of up to G. 5 billion per client, while the recovery of Ueno Cash mutual funds will begin on December 1 through Investor Casa de Bolsa, with 99% of holders getting their money back within a two-week window. The BCP itself, however, is now pushing an extrajudicial liquidation law to address the broader universe of creditors, including bondholders already demanding clarification on the scope of the bill. Consulting firm Dende warned that the crisis has dealt a "heavy blow" to the credibility of both the BCP and the Ministry of Economy and Finance — an assessment that resonates in markets watching Paraguayan financial supervision closely. Argentina, meanwhile, offered cooperation on recovering offshore assets, signaling that the cross-border dimension of the Ueno case is far from exhausted.

Running parallel to this crisis, the fiscal debate has taken on added urgency today. The Senate approved the 2027 General Budget at G. 165.3 trillion and sent it to the Chamber of Deputies, but did so with modifications that set off alarms: it restored extra compensation for public officials while cutting items from the Hambre Cero program by some USD 130 million, in a maneuver Última Hora characterized as "self-raises" approved without real debate. The Foro Multisectorial labeled the exercise "fiscal irresponsibility" and announced an independent watchdog effort. Industrialists grouped in the UIP demanded cuts equivalent to 1.5% of GDP, while economists consulted by ABC Color warned that the projected 2027 deficit "is not sustainable in the medium term." At the same time, the Executive asked Congress for new borrowing of USD 482 million to close out fiscal 2026 and requested modifications to the 2026 budget to allocate an additional USD 72 million to the Health sector — signs that pressure on public finances is not waiting for the new year. Consulting firm Dende projects GDP will grow below 4% in 2027, in a scenario combining fiscal strain, reputational damage to the financial system, and climate effects: the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock warned today of unrecoverable crops due to the El Niño phenomenon, a direct threat to a country whose export base rests on soy, sesame, and cattle.

On the energy front, the government of Santiago Peña, through Alliana's leadership, decreed a holiday for December 7 and implemented a reduction in gas prices: Petropar cut G. 600 per kilogram, a measure that provides some relief to consumers amid depressed consumption. The Ministry of Industry and Commerce also opened an opportunity to supply vessels with B100 biodiesel, targeting Paraguay's river navigation as a new niche for local agroindustrial output. On regional integration, Paraguayan business leaders explored productive integration opportunities with Argentine President Javier Milei, while the foreign ministers of Paraguay and Brazil announced progress in Itaipú negotiations, with Minister Riquelme pledging "patriotic positions" in the Annex C renegotiation.

What will determine the next chapter of this story is the judicial advance against Fernández Valdovinos and the speed with which the BCP manages to get the extrajudicial liquidation law approved: if Congress delays its consideration, the gap between depositors protected by the Guarantee Fund and the broader universe of creditors — bondholders, SMEs, public entities — will continue to widen. In parallel, the Chamber of Deputies will receive a 2027 budget that the private sector considers inflated and that local capital markets anticipate with concern: sources in the securities sector already foresee reduced activity on the stock market during 2027, in part as a direct consequence of the confidence damage generated by the Ueno case.
