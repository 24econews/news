> TITLE: Argentina's Sovereign Risk Rebounds on Wall Street Gains, Not Local Reforms

# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-10

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-10*

The relief that reached Argentina's country risk on Monday —a 67-basis-point drop to 872, the first significant decline in several weeks— was not the product of any shift in local economic policy nor an autonomous vote of confidence in Javier Milei's program. It was, in essence, an imported bounce: Wall Street rallied strongly, the Nasdaq advanced 2.7%, the S&P 500 rose 2.3%, and oil tumbled another 6%, with Brent falling to USD 92 per barrel after statements from President Donald Trump that dampened expectations of further strikes against Iran. That combination —renewed appetite for risk assets and easing global inflationary pressures— pulled Argentine Global bonds higher, closing with an average gain of 1.8%. Argentine stocks in New York followed timidly, with Grupo Galicia rising 3.3% and Mercado Libre 1.5%, though YPF retreated 1.6% to USD 38.03 on the direct impact of the oil price drop on its revenues.

What makes this bounce especially revealing is not its magnitude, but the context in which it occurs. Argentina's country risk more than doubled in four months: from the 402 basis points it touched in early July to the 939 it reached last Friday, its highest level since the October 2025 midterm elections. According to Bloomberg data compiled by JP Morgan, Argentina is now the second-highest sovereign risk country in Latin America, behind only Venezuela. Its spread is four times the regional average of 208 points and more than seven times that of Paraguay —which is navigating its own political crisis around the collapse of Ueno Bank. The deterioration has no recent precedent under the Milei administration and raises questions that go beyond external noise.

At the Economy Ministry, Luis Caputo and Central Bank Vice President Vladimir Werning insist the jump reflects external factors: the fallout from the US midterm elections —where the Democratic victory curbed expectations around the artificial intelligence expansion and hit the tech sector—, global volatility, and geopolitical uncertainty tied to the Middle East. It is not a baseless reading: the previous week, the Nasdaq had lost 3.7% and the S&P 500 shed 2.6%. But the problem is that Argentina's move was notably more pronounced than that of its emerging-market peers, suggesting there are endogenous factors the markets are pricing in with growing concern. Consultancy Invecq was blunt: the economic program Caputo unveiled months ago was based on assumptions that today look more demanding, among them the possibility of issuing corporate dollar debt for USD 6 billion in 2026 and another USD 5 billion in 2027. With country risk at these levels, that scheme faces severe difficulties.

The architecture of official containment is visible. The government intervened in the Single Free Exchange Market, in futures, and in financial dollars to prevent sovereign distrust from spilling over into the exchange rate. The official dollar remains at $1,570, the blue at $1,555, and the contado con liquidación around $1,679, with a contained gap compared to prior episodes of stress. The Central Bank has also applied an implicit crawling peg of roughly 1% monthly, below inflation, in a strategy EcoAnalytics described as a semi-fixed exchange rate scheme that is only sustainable as long as the capital controls remain in force. In parallel, the market detected government intervention in peso bonds of close to $2 trillion, aimed at setting a rate ceiling for long-dated instruments. Consultancy 1816 noted that the objective appears to have been to cap the yield on dual bonds at no more than TAMAR plus 10 percentage points in annual nominal terms.

While financial markets oscillate, the real economy continues to send mixed signals. Construction posted its largest monthly gain of the year in September, with a seasonally adjusted jump of 7.8% and year-on-year growth of 5.1%. Industry, by contrast, barely advanced 0.7% month-on-month in the same period and has accumulated a 2.6% decline in the first nine months of the year versus 2025. SME retail sales fell 1.6% year-on-year in October, with consumption concentrated in low-ticket essentials. CyberMonday posted a 6% drop in purchase orders versus 2025. Poverty reached 34.4% of the population in the second quarter, according to UCA estimates based on Indec data, with 16.5 million people below the line. Peso credit to the private sector has grown just 21% so far this year, against cumulative inflation of 23.6%. The Central Bank itself acknowledges in its latest Monetary Policy Report that deposit rates are negative in real terms.

On the energy front, which is shaping up as the main source of foreign exchange for the coming years, concrete milestones are piling up. On Monday the consortium formed by YPF, Italy's Eni and XRG —the investment arm of Emirati Adnoc— opened the largest pipeline tender in Argentine energy history, valued at roughly USD 2 billion, for the trunk pipeline of the Argentina LNG project. In parallel, San Matías Pipeline began welding work on the duct that will connect Vaca Muerta with the Gulf of San Matías to export liquefied natural gas, in a project that also includes Pan American Energy, YPF, Pampa Energía, Harbour Energy and Golar LNG. According to the plans submitted to the government under the RIGI framework, 27 approved projects add up to investment commitments of USD 76.173 billion, of which USD 51.314 billion should be executed before 2035. The oil sector has already recorded additional revenues of USD 5.165 billion in the first eight months of 2026 versus the same period in 2025, driven by the rise in crude prices —which brushed USD 115 earlier this month— and the growth of production at Vaca Muerta, which reached 700,000 barrels per day in September, 28% more than a year earlier. This week's Brent decline, if sustained, will trim those revenues over the coming weeks.

What lies ahead concentrates several variables in simultaneous tension. On Thursday Indec will release the October CPI, with private estimates around 2% monthly, implying a mild reacceleration from September's 1.93%. Markets will reopen on Wednesday, after the holidays for the visit of Pope Leo XIV, and the behavior of country risk in the first sessions will determine whether Monday's bounce was the start of a sustained recovery or merely technical relief in an oversold market. The final investment decision on Argentina LNG, scheduled for November 30, will be another focal point. And on the more distant horizon, the 2027 election year already operates as a structural source of uncertainty: based on bond market prices, the implicit odds of Milei's reelection stand at just 45%, though analysts caution that such a reading may be overstating the political deterioration.

## Corporate Watch

### YPF (NYSE: YPF)
The majority state-owned oil company on Monday opened the tender for roughly USD 2 billion in pipelines for the Argentina LNG trunk gas pipeline, the country's largest LNG export project, in partnership with Eni and XRG, Adnoc's investment arm; the megaproject's final investment decision is scheduled for November 30. YPF's ADR slipped 1.6% in Monday's session to USD 38.03, hit by the 6% drop in the oil price.

### Mercado Libre (NASDAQ: MELI)
ADRs of the company led by Marcos Galperin rebounded 1.5% on Monday after shedding 12.4% the previous Thursday, when quarterly earnings coincided with Wall Street's worst session of the year; the stock has accumulated a sharp correction from its recent highs amid the global rotation out of the tech sector.

### Adecoagro (NYSE: AGRO)
The group closed last week on the purchase of the nearly 900,000 Patagonian hectares that the Benetton Group had held in Argentina for more than 30 years, in the largest land transfer transaction recorded in the country in recent years; the deal reflects the trend of "Argentinization" of local assets as foreign multinationals exit.
