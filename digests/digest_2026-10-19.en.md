# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-10-19

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-10-19*

Argentina's country risk brushed 650 basis points at week's end, holding near its 2026 highs at a moment when Javier Milei's government is piling up favorable domestic signals that international markets refuse to reward. That paradox—good local data, battered assets—is the central tension defining this week's economic landscape.

The central bank closed its best stretch of FX purchases since July, accumulating more than USD 983 million so far in October and overshooting by more than 50% the USD 10 billion annual target agreed with the International Monetary Fund. Gross reserves climbed back above USD 50 billion. At the same time, September inflation came in at 1.9% month-over-month, below the 2% consensus, while core inflation eased to 1.68%, its lowest reading in three months. The Treasury rolled over all of its October maturities in the month's first auction with a 136% rollover ratio and extended the average duration of peso-denominated debt to 535 days. On the political front, the government kept the opposition from reaching a quorum in the lower house to debate Decree 70/23, an outcome market participants read as a sign of institutional stability. Marcos Galperin, founder of Mercado Libre, captured the official reading of the moment with a line that circulated widely: "Something powerful is changing in Argentina."

And yet, sovereign dollar bonds averaged a weekly decline of 1.6% for Globales and 0.9% for Bonares. Country risk climbed 59 points on the week to 648 basis points, near its highest level since November 2025. The S&P Merval slid 0.4% in pesos on Friday and is down 11.5% in dollars year to date, with the notable exception of YPF, whose New York ADR is up 52% in 2026 and closed the week around USD 52. The explanation for this divergence has two mutually reinforcing components: the external backdrop and electoral uncertainty.

On the global front, U.S. Treasury yields hit two-decade highs, pushing the 10-year above 5%, driven by stickier-than-expected U.S. inflation—headline CPI accelerated to 0.6% month-over-month in September—and by fiscal uncertainty tied to the midterm elections. The Nasdaq fell more than 5% on the week, dragged down by mounting doubts about how the AI build-out will be financed in a high-rate environment. For Argentina, which has been locked out of international credit markets since 2018, the rise in the risk-free rate acts as a silent tax: every additional point on Treasuries widens the spread investors demand to hold emerging-market debt. President Milei acknowledged as much publicly: "The world has higher rates, and that produces lower growth."

Domestically, the IMF trimmed its 2026 growth forecast for Argentina by nearly two percentage points, though it signaled confidence in an acceleration into 2027. The available data justifies the Fund's caution: GDP has contracted 2.5% since February 2025, the economy shrank 0.6% in the second quarter versus the first, and registered private employment fell for the fourteenth consecutive month. According to Indec, net job creation is being driven by self-employment and informality—257,000 new unregistered jobs over the past year—while formal employment continues to lose ground. SME retail sales for Mother's Day fell 1.4% year-over-year in real terms, marking the fifth consecutive decline since 2022, even as 89% of the merchants surveyed by CAME offered discounts and promotions. Oxford Economics projects inflation will average 2.1% monthly over the next six months, above the 1.7% local consensus, pointing to the resumption of subsidy cuts and sustained high oil prices as resistance factors.

Oil, in fact, cuts both ways for Argentina. Brent closed the week near USD 110, up almost 3% weekly, lifted by Iranian attacks in the Strait of Hormuz that cut daily flows to 11.6 million barrels against a benchmark of 17 million. Goldman Sachs and economists at Brookings anticipate an eventual downward correction in crude, but that outlook did little to ease the immediate pressure: wholesale inflation accelerated to 3.2% in September, driven by a 15% jump in crude oil and gas, which is up 77.1% year to date. At the same time, the trailing twelve-month energy surplus reached USD 11.071 billion and now accounts for 45.3% of the country's entire goods trade surplus, an unprecedented share in Indec's historical series. In less than three years, Argentina has gone from an energy balance of USD 41 million to one topping eleven billion dollars.

It is against this backdrop that the Argentina LNG megaproject—led by YPF alongside Italy's Eni and the UAE's XRG, with total investment estimated at USD 51 billion—takes on a strategic dimension that reaches well beyond the energy sector. Tuesday will bring the results of a USD 2 billion tender for the pipes supplying the 500-kilometer gas duct that will feed the two FLNG units off the Golfo San Matías. It is the largest pipe procurement in Argentina's energy history. Meanwhile, Profertil, the country's only urea producer, selected Techint Ingeniería and Italy's Saipem to develop the technical services for its second plant in Bahía Blanca, a USD 2 billion project that would double installed capacity and could be submitted under the RIGI incentive regime.

Minister Luis Caputo, in Bangkok for the annual meetings of the IMF and the World Bank, told the World Bank's vice president for Latin America that Argentina has "a surplus of dollars" and that the flow of RIGI investment—with USD 50 billion in approved projects and another USD 160 billion in the pipeline—will be sufficient to lift the remaining FX controls. On Wall Street, however, investment funds are doing their own math and aren't quite convinced. According to Banco Galicia, investors want to see actual reserve accumulation flows rather than aggregate estimates. Some 41% of Latin American fund managers surveyed by Bank of America expect an improvement in Argentine assets over the next six months—up from 27% in September—but that improving sentiment coexists with a country risk spread that refuses to tighten.

What markets will be watching in the coming weeks is whether the BCRA can sustain October's pace of purchases, whether that month's inflation confirms the downward trend in core, and whether the Argentina LNG pipe tender generates concrete signals of progress on the largest investment project in Argentina's history. The third review of the IMF program, the trajectory of oil prices, and the first moves on the 2027 electoral calendar will round out the picture investors need to decide whether the paradox of good data and battered assets finally resolves.

## Corporate Watch

### YPF (NYSE: YPF)
YPF, whose ADR trades on the New York Stock Exchange, rose 2.5% on the week and closed around USD 52, bringing its year-to-date gain to 52% in 2026, the best performance among Argentine risk assets. Tuesday will bring the results of the USD 2 billion tender for the gas pipeline tubing for the Argentina LNG project—the largest pipe procurement in the country's energy history—in which YPF leads the consortium alongside Eni and XRG, with total investment estimated at USD 51 billion.

### Telecom Argentina (BYMA: TECO2)
Telecom Argentina is advancing the transfer of roughly six million mobile customers from the Movistar brand to Tuenti, the new operator spun out of the USD 450 million sale to Metrotel—a divestment required by the National Competition Authority as a condition for approving the acquisition of Telefónica de Argentina; individual customer notifications began Monday, October 19 and will run through the end of November. The stock is up 11.8% in dollars in 2026.

### Profertil
Profertil, Argentina's sole urea producer with approximately 60% of the domestic market, selected Techint Ingeniería & Construcción and Italy's Saipem to provide technical services for its second plant in Bahía Blanca, a USD 2 billion project that would triple daily capacity to 4,450 tons and could be submitted under the RIGI regime; the final investment decision is still pending, but it represents the first concrete step forward on a plan that has been on the drawing board for more than a decade.
