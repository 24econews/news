# Argentina: Economic Pulse — 2026-11-13

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-13*

October's inflation print drove a wedge through the Milei government's official narrative: Indec confirmed on Thursday a 2.2% monthly increase, four tenths above the median expectation in the Market Expectations Survey and the highest reading since April. The acceleration was not catastrophic in absolute terms — the index remains far from the peaks of the Kirchner era — but it arrives at a moment when the economic team needed precisely the opposite: a datapoint that would validate the narrative of convergence toward price stability ahead of the electoral cycle that already dominates investor horizons.

Minister Luis Caputo wasted no time going on the offensive. Through his official channels, he argued that the jump is largely explained by changes to the Cold Zones regime, which — by sharply curtailing the reach of gas subsidies — left 2.4 million users without the tariff discount they had been receiving, pushing the Housing, water, electricity and gas category to a 4.2% monthly rise. Sebastián Menescaldi, of Eco Go, estimated that this factor alone added 0.3 percentage points to the index. Fuels also played a role — with tensions in the Middle East still producing lagged effects on local prices — along with seasonal items, where butternut squash, onion, potato, lemon and sweet potato recorded monthly increases above 20%. The element the government chose to defend its position was core inflation, which fell to 1.5% monthly, the lowest level in more than seven years with the sole exception of July 2025. Caputo highlighted that figure on social media; the market processed it with more nuance.

The tension is real: while the underlying price component is showing signs of deceleration, regulated and seasonal items continue to push upward, and the total basic basket climbed 3.5% in October, far outpacing the headline index. For a typical family, the poverty threshold now requires monthly income of $1,690,000. The purchasing power of the average pension has accumulated a loss of close to 12.7% since November 2023, and private formal employment strung together its fifteenth consecutive month of decline. The labor reform the government presented as an engine of formalization generated 82,000 contracts under the new regime between May and August, but over that same period the net balance was 37,800 fewer formal jobs. These are the numbers that explain the political noise in the background.

Markets responded with a significant disconnect: sovereign dollar bonds rebounded by an average 1.3%, the JP Morgan country risk index gave back 27 units to close at 829 basis points — after touching 867 during the morning — but the Merval index extended its losing streak to five consecutive sessions, with a 1.1% decline in pesos. External noise did not help: the resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, after a national security scandal involving an alleged Chinese spy, shook Wall Street with a 4.4% drop in the chipmaker's shares. The ten-year Treasury marked a yield of 5.10%, a backdrop that raises the cost of emerging market financing globally and complicates Argentina's financial program heading into January, when the Treasury must face maturities of nearly USD 4.4 billion. The sources of those dollars — IMF disbursement, privatization proceeds, multilaterals — are not yet fully defined. Morgan Stanley, in a report cited by Bloomberg, judged that the sell-off in Argentine debt had gone too far: strategist Simon Waever argued that bonds are pricing in a probability of between 41% and 52% for his downside scenario, which he characterized as an "overreaction." The Central Bank, for its part, purchased USD 103 million during the session, the largest amount in November, accumulating USD 16.147 billion for the year.

On the domestic front, industry remains the most visible scar of the model. Textile production fell 20.8% year-on-year in the first eight months of the year, with just 40.6% of installed capacity in use and the loss of more than 28,000 jobs since December 2023. At the ProTextil 2026 convention, Fundación Pro Tejer president Luciano Galfione was categorical: "The viability of the textile sector is a political decision." The apparel brand Luxo exemplified the dynamic: it closed its plant in La Rioja, moved 70% of its production to China and reinvented itself as an importer. Stanley Black & Decker confirmed the closure of its abrasive discs factory in San Luis, with 61 layoffs. The Pauny tractor factory, in Las Varillas, deepened its labor conflict by failing to appear at a hearing called by the Córdoba Labor Ministry and proposing voluntary retirements for 150 of its 450 employees. The displacement of regional automobile production by Chinese electric vehicles — which in Brazil hit a historic import record of USD 9.42 billion year-to-date, 76.7% more than in 2025 — is simultaneously eroding bilateral trade: Argentine car exports to Brazil fell 7.8% in the first ten months of the year.

Against that adverse industrial backdrop, the government accelerated its concessions and privatizations agenda as a long-term structural signal. Decree N° 1358/2026 authorized the tender of 27 stretches of national highways under the road concession scheme. The concessions of five hydroelectric plants were extended until April 2027 while new tenders move forward. The signing of the contract for the sale of 90% of AySA, scheduled for Friday, was postponed until December 17 at the request of the buyers to review the documentation. The Paso de los Libres border crossing, the country's second-largest customs point, received the declaration of public interest for its private concession. And the government announced a second RIGI business round for December 2, focused on oil, gas and energy, aimed at connecting local suppliers with the major projects that already total USD 76 billion in approved commitments, though the distribution is markedly uneven: Neuquén and Río Negro concentrate 56.6% of the total. The IMF, meanwhile, downplayed the recent volatility: communications director Julie Kozack urged against overstating "short-term fluctuations" and confirmed that the board is moving toward approval of the third program review.

What the market will be watching in the coming weeks is whether the November print confirms the official reading — that October was essentially transitory noise — or whether inflation shows greater stickiness than expected. The fate of the multilateral real exchange rate, which the Central Bank already places at Milei-era lows and which economist Lucas Llach described as "the lowest level since 2001" in unified market terms, adds another variable of competitive pressure. January's debt maturities, the IMF's third review in December and the inexorably shrinking electoral calendar define the frame within which the economic team will have to demonstrate that the exchange rate anchor can keep containing expectations without further deteriorating the competitiveness of the productive sector.

Mexican industrial group Grupo Simec announced the acquisition of 100% of Argentine steelmaker Acerbrag from Brazil's Votorantim, in a transaction valued by regional media at around USD 350 million; the deal, subject to regulatory approvals, marks Votorantim's exit from the steel sector and adds a new international player to Argentina's long steel landscape.
