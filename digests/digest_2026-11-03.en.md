# Argentina: The Economic Pulse — 2026-11-03

*AI-generated analysis | 2026-11-03*

Argentina's country risk crossed 800 basis points on Monday for the first time in more than a year, hitting levels unseen since the October 2025 midterm elections — and it did so on a day that concentrated a confluence of external shocks and domestic question marks rarely stacked with such intensity into a single session. What stands out is not the magnitude of the indicator itself, but rather the speed and simultaneity of the forces that pushed it to that threshold, and the way the move exposes the structural fragilities of an economic program that until a few weeks ago was boasting of its strengths.

According to analysts and specialized outlets, three factors explain the jump. The first is the US election day itself, with the entire House of Representatives and one-third of the Senate up for grabs. The outcome of these midterms matters to Buenos Aires not only as a thermometer of the political alignment between Milei and Trump, but because it directly conditions the US executive's room to sustain financial backing for Argentina heading into a 2027 loaded with maturities. Estimates from Grupo Atenas suggest a Republican victory could bring country risk down to the 600-650 zone, while a divided or Democrat-controlled Congress would push it into the 750-1,000 range. The second factor is the so-called "Lula effect": the Brazilian president's reelection triggered a 14% drop in the Bovespa and a nearly 9% depreciation of the real, with immediate reverberations across Argentine assets. The third is the slide in crude prices — Brent fell close to 8% — which dragged down YPF, whose New York ADR shed 6.7%, pressuring the MSCI Merval index, which retreated 3.2% in pesos and extended its losing streak to six consecutive sessions.

Banks were the hardest-hit sector. ADRs of Grupo Financiero Galicia are down 40% year-to-date in 2026; Banco Supervielle, 46%; BBVA Argentina, 37%; and Banco Macro, 33%. In every case, share prices sit at year-to-date lows and have lost more than half their value from the January 2025 peak, when international funds piled massively into Argentina before rotating toward Brazil. Today that question is reversed: with the Bovespa's collapse following Lula's victory, some analysts are speculating whether capital flows could reverse course, though for now there are no signs of it.

FX pressure accompanied the deterioration. The wholesale dollar advanced 0.7% to $1,545.50, a fresh nominal high, while the contado con liquidación approached $1,653 and the blue dollar held unchanged at $1,565. The Central Bank recorded no purchases on the day, breaking a dynamic that had been encouraging through October: the BCRA accumulated USD 1.395 billion in purchases that month, lifting gross reserves to USD 48.562 billion. However, the current pace of accumulation falls short of the USD 19.5 billion target Minister Caputo projected in September; to meet it, the monetary authority would need to buy roughly USD 90 million per day through year-end, a level surpassed in only three months this year.

The underlying macro backdrop to this market deterioration is complex. The economy is facing what consultancies such as Eco Go describe as a technical recession: GDP is estimated to have contracted in the third quarter, marking two consecutive quarterly declines. The IMF cut its 2026 growth projection to just 2.1%, well below the 3.5% forecast in April. Industry has posted nine consecutive months of decline and is producing 11% less than in 2022 and 2023, a reality that the Unión Industrial Argentina brought today to its 32nd Industrial Conference, where Minister Caputo and Cabinet Chief Santilli represented the Executive at an event President Milei himself has not attended since labeling industrialists "rent-seekers" in 2024. October tax collection offered some relative relief: revenues totaled $21.6 trillion, up 33.7% nominally year-on-year and 0.7% in real terms, stringing together the second consecutive month of real growth — something not seen since 2022.

Agriculture, by contrast, is enjoying its best run in decades. Agricultural exporters liquidated USD 2.88 billion in October, the highest reading for that month in 25 years, driven by corn flows, soybean crushing and wheat shipments. Natural gas production hit an all-time record, with Vaca Muerta now accounting for 67% of total output. And YPF submitted the Bajo del Toro project to the Large Investment Incentive Regime (RIGI), with an estimated investment of USD 8.729 billion and projected exports of USD 25 billion between 2027 and 2055. This bifurcation — a hydrocarbons and agribusiness export sector in expansion versus a weakened manufacturing industry and domestic consumption — is precisely the tension that former Minister Domingo Cavallo crystallized in a public warning to the government: unless reserve accumulation accelerates, unless the capital controls are lifted and unless the deterioration in activity is addressed, an electoral stumble in 2027 cannot be ruled out.

The government, for its part, is pushing an agenda of consumer stimulus with a pre-electoral bent. On Thursday, a Banco Nación loan plan will be announced at a 25% fixed rate for the purchase of domestically produced vehicles, with terms of up to 72 months — a rate that would run below projected inflation and that aims to reverse the 12.8% year-on-year drop in car registrations. Deputy Minister José Luis Daza is also weighing extending the Sustainability Guarantee Fund's terms for mortgage credit beyond five years. On the tax front, the UIF published Resolution 144/2026, completing the regulation of the Fiscal Innocence Law II and setting at $100 million per year the threshold above which banks may apply simplified due diligence to those depositing "mattress" dollars into the formal system.

What the market will be watching in the coming hours is the outcome of the US midterms, which could reposition Argentine assets in one direction or another quickly. Beyond that, the immediate calendar includes an USD 800 million maturity payment to the IMF — whose Board still has to approve a pending USD 900 million disbursement scheduled for December — and the release of October inflation data, which private consultancies place between 1.8% and 2%, with the government unable to pierce that floor. The possibility that Mercado Pago obtains a formal banking license before year-end, following the BCRA's technical approval, and the sale of Supermercados DIA to one of its three remaining candidates — Grupo de Narváez, Intercorp or PedidosYa — are two other events with the potential to move the local microeconomy in the near term.

## Corporate Watch

### YPF (NYSE: YPF / BCBA: YPFD)
The majority state-owned oil company submitted the Bajo del Toro project to the RIGI, with an estimated investment of USD 8.729 billion and projected exports of USD 25 billion between 2027 and 2055; its New York ADR fell 6.7% on the day, hit by the 8% collapse in Brent prices. The project is the first of three RIGI filings YPF plans to submit for northern Vaca Muerta, a package contemplating gross investments of USD 44 billion over 26 years.

### Mercado Libre (NASDAQ: MELI)
The Central Bank granted technical approval to Mercado Pago's application to become a bank — under the name Mercado Banco — moving toward final approval by the BCRA Board before year-end; the stock rose 3.2% on the day, decoupling from the broad-based punishment of Argentine assets. The transformation from digital wallet to banking entity will require Marcos Galperín's company to comply with the reserve requirements, capital standards and regulatory obligations that currently govern traditional banking.

### Adecoagro (NYSE: AGRO)
The partners and directors of the agribusiness firm, led by CEO Mariano Bosch, personally acquired the more than 900,000 hectares the Benetton Group held in Argentina through Compañía de Tierras Sud Argentino and Ganadera Cóndor, in one of the largest land transfers in the country's recent history. The deal size was not disclosed; Adecoagro, which trades on NYSE, was not involved as the acquiring entity.
